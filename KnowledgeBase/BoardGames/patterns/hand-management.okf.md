## Summary

Across the corpus, citable hand-management commentary remains rare — most games carrying the
tag have reception docs that discuss deck construction (already covered by
`patterns/deck-building.okf.md`) or general onboarding/setup friction rather than the
moment-to-moment experience of holding and cycling a hand. Where evidence exists, it splits
three ways: a large personal card pool that resists filtering and produces "output randomness"
(Ark Nova), a simultaneous-selection turn structure that removes the wait for other players'
hand decisions (Heat), and a grimoire/hand-reserve design that lets players bank specific cards
— Silver, Inscription, a multiplier — as a legible tactical reserve while still supporting
immediate use of newly-acquired cards (Dark Pact). The positive cases share a common thread of
legibility: players can see and plan around exactly what a hand holds, whether that's a small
curated reserve or a purchase usable the instant it arrives; the negative case is the inverse —
an oversized, unfiltered pool where a needed card may simply never surface. Evidence is thin
overall — three games — and this pattern should still be treated as a working sketch rather than
a settled synthesis.

## Evidence by game

- ark-nova (src-008): A solo reviewer says the large 212-card personal pool produces "output randomness" that undermines planning ahead, and wished for "either less cards, or more ways to churn through the deck," confidence: high
- ark-nova (src-009): A second reviewer reports the same complaint pattern — the 212-card zoo deck can mean "waiting for cards to come that never do," though the reviewer frames adapting to whatever is in hand as the intended antidote, confidence: high
- dark-pact (src-007, src-010): Grimoire access lets players hold "Silver, Inscription, and the extra multiplier as a tactical reserve" in hand, confidence: medium
- dark-pact (src-007, src-009): "gain-to-hand" purchases support "immediate use of acquired cards," confidence: high
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-006): Co-op Board Games lists "Simultaneous card selection eliminates downtime" as a pro, describing all players choosing and revealing a hand card at once rather than waiting through sequential turns, confidence: medium

## Where it works

- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-006): Simultaneous hand-card selection is praised specifically because it removes the downtime of watching other players deliberate over their hand, one of the sharpest positive claims about hand-management mechanics found in this corpus.
- dark-pact (src-007, src-009): "Gain-to-hand" purchases let players use a newly-bought card the same turn, rewarding immediate tactical adaptation rather than banking it for later.
- dark-pact (src-007, src-010): Holding Silver, Inscription, and multiplier cards in hand as a named "tactical reserve" gives players a legible buffer to plan around instead of being forced to spend as soon as a card is drawn.

## Where it fails

- ark-nova (src-008): A large, un-thinned personal card pool feeding the hand can make plans fall apart when needed cards simply don't come up — the reviewer's explicit fix request was more hand/deck cycling tools, not a smaller pool.
- ark-nova (src-009): The same "waiting for cards that never arrive" friction is corroborated by a second reviewer, though that source treats forced adaptation to your current hand as a feature rather than a flaw.

## Coverage gaps

Canonical hand-management-heavy designs in this list — Mage Knight, Gloomhaven, Spirit Island,
Marvel Champions, Arkham Horror, and Slay the Spire — all carry the mechanic tag, but the
reception docs actually read for this pass discuss onboarding, setup/teardown, deck construction,
or campaign scale rather than hand size, hand limits, or in-hand planning specifically. Dark Pact
adds a third data point (grimoire reserve, gain-to-hand timing), but it is still a design-level
claim about card acquisition rather than a hand-limit or forced-discard complaint. The corpus
would benefit from a review pass (or a game) that targets hand-limit/hand-cycling commentary
directly — e.g. "too many cards to track in hand," "forced discards," or "hand planning under a
limit" — since the current three-game evidence base cannot support a confident cross-game read.
