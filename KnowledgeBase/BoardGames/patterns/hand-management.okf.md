## Summary

Fifteen of the sixteen games carrying `hand-management` in this corpus have reception docs, but
almost none of the sourced claims are actually about hand-of-cards friction as distinct from
deck-building/construction (already covered by `patterns/deck-building.okf.md`) or general
onboarding/setup complaints. Only two games produced citable evidence squarely about the
moment-to-moment experience of holding and cycling a hand: a large personal card pool that
resists filtering, versus a simultaneous-selection turn structure that removes the wait for
other players' hand decisions. Evidence is thin overall — this pattern currently rests on two
games and should be treated as a starting sketch, not a settled synthesis.

## Evidence by game

- ark-nova (src-008): A solo reviewer says the large 212-card personal pool produces "output randomness" that undermines planning ahead, and wished for "either less cards, or more ways to churn through the deck," confidence: high
- ark-nova (src-009): A second reviewer reports the same complaint pattern — the 212-card zoo deck can mean "waiting for cards to come that never do," though the reviewer frames adapting to whatever is in hand as the intended antidote, confidence: high
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-006): Co-op Board Games lists "Simultaneous card selection eliminates downtime" as a pro, describing all players choosing and revealing a hand card at once rather than waiting through sequential turns, confidence: medium

## Where it works

- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-006): Simultaneous hand-card selection is praised specifically because it removes the downtime of watching other players deliberate over their hand, one of the sharpest positive claims about hand-management mechanics found in this corpus.

## Where it fails

- ark-nova (src-008): A large, un-thinned personal card pool feeding the hand can make plans fall apart when needed cards simply don't come up — the reviewer's explicit fix request was more hand/deck cycling tools, not a smaller pool.
- ark-nova (src-009): The same "waiting for cards that never arrive" friction is corroborated by a second reviewer, though that source treats forced adaptation to your current hand as a feature rather than a flaw.

## Coverage gaps

Canonical hand-management-heavy designs in this list — Mage Knight, Gloomhaven, Spirit Island,
Marvel Champions, Arkham Horror, and Slay the Spire — all carry the mechanic tag, but the
reception docs actually read for this pass discuss onboarding, setup/teardown, deck construction,
or campaign scale rather than hand size, hand limits, or in-hand planning specifically. The corpus
would benefit from a review pass (or a games) that targets hand-limit/hand-cycling commentary
directly — e.g. "too many cards to track in hand," "forced discards," or "hand planning under a
limit" — since the current two-game evidence base cannot support a confident cross-game read.
