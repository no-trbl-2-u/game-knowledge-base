## Summary

Most games tagged `cooperative-game` in this corpus carry the tag as background metadata; their reception docs discuss deckbuilding, campaigns, or onboarding without ever reviewing the co-op dynamic itself. Where reviewers do engage with cooperation directly, the pattern is consistent: shared handoffs and free-order actions (Heroes of Terrinoth, Slay the Spire) are what make players feel like a team rather than parallel solitaires, while open information and rigid turn structure are what let one player start dictating everyone else's moves (Heroes of Terrinoth's quarterbacking risk, Mage Knight's "multiplayer solitaire" complaint). Discussion itself becomes a cost at higher player counts (Spirit Island). Two newly-added hidden-agenda titles, Nemesis and Shadows over Camelot, finally supply the hidden-information co-op evidence this pattern's coverage gap previously called for: both are praised specifically because cooperation is *imperfect* — hidden goals, uncertain traitor presence — and both reviewers warn that removing that imperfection would remove the design's best fun, not fix it. Evidence is thin overall because many tagged records still lack co-op-specific commentary.

## Evidence by game

- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): Reviewer states open information in the co-op permits one player to take over decisions — an alpha-player/quarterbacking risk flagged as a potential issue, confidence: medium
- mage-knight (src-011): Fan critique argues the co-op mode is "not cooperative enough," describing it as "multiplayer solitaire" where players solve parallel puzzles rather than truly interdepend, confidence: medium
- nemesis (src-006, src-008, src-009): reviews repeatedly identify hidden goals, not a conventional revealed-traitor structure, as the tension source, supporting a call to calibrate semi-coop expectations up front rather than treat the design as fully cooperative, confidence: high
- shadows-over-camelot (src-006, src-007): both reviewers independently name imperfect communication and uncertain Traitor presence as the base game's strongest fun, warning that making cooperation fully transparent would remove it, confidence: high
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-007): Reviewer calls cooperative play "a revelation" and the central reason the physical adaptation justifies itself alongside the video game original, confidence: high
- spirit-island (src-008): Reviewer notes higher player counts slow the game down "due to extensive discussion and coordination," making communication overhead a direct cost of cooperating, confidence: high

## Where it works

- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): Cooperation is "materially supported by Aid tokens, enemy handoffs, and complementary hero abilities," giving players concrete, repeated reasons to coordinate rather than merely play in parallel.
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-007): "Cooperative play is a revelation" — reviewer treats the multiplayer co-op mode as the strongest justification for the board-game adaptation existing at all.
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008, src-003): The rulebook's free-order rule — players may play cards, use potions, or activate abilities in any order — is praised as the mechanism that turns cooperative play into genuine tactical collaboration rather than rigid turn-taking.
- shadows-over-camelot (src-006, src-007): imperfect communication and hidden-Traitor uncertainty are named as the single strongest source of fun, an explicit case of engineered incomplete-information cooperation being praised over full transparency.
- nemesis (src-006, src-008, src-009): hidden, often self-preserving agendas are credited across three independent reviews as the actual source of tension, distinct from and stronger than a simple revealed-traitor mechanic.

## Where it fails

- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): Fully open information invites a single player to dictate the group's choices (alpha-player takeover).
- mage-knight (src-011): The co-op mode is experienced as parallel puzzle-solving rather than true interdependence — "multiplayer solitaire."
- spirit-island (src-008): Extensive discussion and coordination at higher player counts becomes a pacing tax on the group.
- nemesis (src-006, src-008, src-009): the same hidden-agenda uncertainty that produces tension also means cooperation is often self-preserving rather than guaranteed, so expectations need calibrating before play or groups may feel misled about how "cooperative" the game actually is.

## Coverage gaps

The reception docs with genuine co-op-dynamics commentary are heroes-of-terrinoth, mage-knight, nemesis, shadows-over-camelot, slay-the-spire-the-board-game, and spirit-island. Other tagged records presently discuss deckbuilding, campaign structure, or onboarding rather than cooperation itself. The earlier gap here — restricted-communication or hidden-information co-op design — is now substantially filled by Nemesis and Shadows over Camelot; what remains is a genuinely restricted-*communication* design (e.g. a Hanabi-style limited-communication game, as opposed to hidden-agenda/traitor designs) to test whether that different flavor of imperfect information produces different reviewer language than the hidden-goal cases cited here.
