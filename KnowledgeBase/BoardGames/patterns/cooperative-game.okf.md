## Summary

Where reviewers engage with cooperation directly, the pattern is consistent: shared handoffs, free-order actions, and an accessible on-ramp are what make players feel like a team rather than parallel solitaires (Heroes of Terrinoth's aid tokens, Slay the Spire's free-order rule, Horrified's "hyper-thematic replacement for Pandemic"), and The Crew's trick-taking cooperation earns the same kind of praise ("tense, satisfying," "a tense collaborative experience"). The failure mode is the mirror image: open information and rigid turn structure let one player dictate everyone else's moves (Heroes of Terrinoth's quarterbacking risk, Mage Knight's "multiplayer solitaire" complaint), and cooperation can be thin enough to barely register — Onirim's two-player mode is "close to two-player solitaire." Discussion overhead is itself a cost at higher player counts (Spirit Island). A further cluster of tagged games treats "cooperative" as one mode among several rather than the defining feature: Dead of Winter's baseline cooperative difficulty is cited only as one factor among many in a semi-cooperative/betrayal hybrid, and Elder Sign's cooperative mode works but isn't what reviewers choose to write about, next to solo play. Evidence remains thin overall relative to how many records carry the tag.

## Where it works

- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): Cooperation is "materially supported by Aid tokens, enemy handoffs, and complementary hero abilities," giving players concrete, repeated reasons to coordinate rather than merely play in parallel.
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-007): "Cooperative play is a revelation" — reviewer treats the multiplayer co-op mode as the strongest justification for the board-game adaptation existing at all.
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008, src-003): The rulebook's free-order rule — players may play cards, use potions, or activate abilities in any order — is praised as the mechanism that turns cooperative play into genuine tactical collaboration rather than rigid turn-taking.
- the-crew (src-003, src-004): Trick-taking recast as a cooperative task-completion loop produces a "tense, satisfying" and "tense collaborative experience" that inverts the usual competitive expectations of the genre.
- horrified (src-003): Pitched as an accessible, thematic alternative to Pandemic-style cooperative monster-hunting.

## Where it fails

- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): Fully open information invites a single player to dictate the group's choices (alpha-player takeover).
- mage-knight (src-011): The co-op mode is experienced as parallel puzzle-solving rather than true interdependence — "multiplayer solitaire."
- spirit-island (src-008): Extensive discussion and coordination at higher player counts becomes a pacing tax on the group.
- onirim-second-edition (src-002): The two-player cooperative mode is functionally "close to two-player solitaire," leaving little genuine interaction between players.

## Coverage gaps

The Crew now supplies the restricted-communication cooperative example this doc previously lacked. Remaining gaps: Dead of Winter and Elder Sign contribute only incidental cooperative-mode commentary layered on other features (a semi-cooperative/betrayal hybrid, a solo-vs-group framing) rather than analysis of the cooperative mechanism itself, and Horrified's evidence is a single comparative descriptor rather than a detailed account of what makes its co-op loop work. A game with reviewer commentary specifically on cooperative fail states — e.g. a total-loss condition reviewers say feels unfair to pin on one player — would sharpen "Where it fails" beyond quarterbacking and thin-interaction complaints.
