## Summary

Evidence was narrow through the last pass (two games, both describing a
"completeness pressure" problem — the base product is fine alone, but the
surrounding product line becomes an implicit requirement to reach the
"ideal" experience). This pass adds a large, long-running expansion catalog
(Dominion) for the first time, and it partially confirms the pattern:
reviewers name expansion breadth as a source of maintenance/selection
fatigue even while praising the base box's compact, self-contained identity —
completeness pressure exists here too, just expressed as choice-paralysis
rather than a hard content gate. Dead of Winter: The Long Night adds a
different, sharper shape: a same-line sequel/spin-off box drawing an explicit
"not worth buying if you already own the original" verdict, i.e. bloat as
redundant purchase rather than required purchase.

## Evidence by game

- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-005): Board Game Quest frames the revised
  core set as a response to reprint scarcity that "strained" the product
  line's ability to bring in new players, i.e. product-line sprawl and
  availability became an onboarding tax rather than a content benefit,
  confidence: medium
- dead-of-winter-the-long-night (src-004): the reviewer is "not sure The Long
  Night is a worthwhile purchase if you've already got the original game,"
  naming the spin-off's overlap with its predecessor as the specific
  complaint, confidence: medium
- dominion (src-003, src-007): the review lists expansion fatigue as a minor
  flaw for the base game even while praising its compact identity, and a
  community errata article demonstrates an active, ongoing rules ecosystem
  around the expansion line, confidence: medium
- root (src-006): Gideon's Gaming notes expansion factions create a
  perceived-completeness pressure — "having them all is the ideal
  experience" — and that "the Law of Root booklet itself even heightens the
  feeling of incompleteness," confidence: medium

## Where it works

No praised-design evidence found for this pattern.

## Where it fails

- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-005): reprint scarcity and product-line
  strain hampered new-player entry — "Due to a lack of reprints and
  scarcity, the system was strained by continuing forward while losing the
  ability to bring in new players."
- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-007): base-game content is explicitly
  capped without buying more product — "If you wish to play with more than
  two characters, you will need another copy of the base game."
- dead-of-winter-the-long-night (src-004): a same-line spin-off box is judged
  as offering too little beyond its predecessor to justify buying both,
  bloat as redundancy rather than as a required content gate.
- dominion (src-003): expansion breadth is flagged as a minor but real
  fatigue/selection-burden cost against the base game's own compact appeal.
- root (src-006): the official rules booklet itself is cited as amplifying
  the sense that the base game alone is incomplete, pushing players toward
  the full expansion set to get the "ideal" experience.

## Coverage gaps

Current support for this label comes from reviewer commentary rather than
direct player-count survey data. Dominion is the corpus's first large,
long-running expansion catalog and it produces a milder version of this
pattern (fatigue, not a hard gate) than Arkham Horror LCG or Root — a useful
contrast worth revisiting once a legacy game or app-supported game with
frequent content drops is added, to see whether milder or harder bloat
complaints correlate with how expansions are sold (one-time box vs. ongoing
subscription-like release cadence).
