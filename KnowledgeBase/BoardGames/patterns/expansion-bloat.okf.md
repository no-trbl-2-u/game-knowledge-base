## Summary

Evidence is narrow but has broadened past a single "completeness pressure" shape. The
original cases describe reviewers feeling the base product is a fine design on its
own, but the surrounding product line (more character copies, more factions, more
reprints) becomes an implicit requirement to reach the "ideal" experience. Two newer
games add adjacent shapes of the same problem: a standalone expansion whose value is
questioned for owners of the base box (Dead of Winter: The Long Night), and a base
game whose own designed-in expandability (many Kingdom cards) is itself named a minor
selection-fatigue cost even before any physical expansion is purchased (Dominion).

## Evidence by game

- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-005): Board Game Quest frames the revised
  core set as a response to reprint scarcity that "strained" the product
  line's ability to bring in new players, i.e. product-line sprawl and
  availability became an onboarding tax rather than a content benefit,
  confidence: medium
- dead-of-winter-the-long-night (src-004): the reviewer says they are "not sure
  [it] is a worthwhile purchase if you've already got the original game,"
  questioning a standalone expansion's value proposition for existing owners,
  confidence: medium
- dominion (src-003, src-007): the review lists expansion fatigue as a minor
  flaw while a community errata article demonstrates a broader ongoing rules
  ecosystem, suggesting selection/maintenance fatigue can appear even without
  buying every expansion, confidence: medium
- root (src-006): Gideon's Gaming notes expansion factions create a
  perceived-completeness pressure — "having them all is the ideal
  experience" — and that "the Law of Root booklet itself even heightens the
  feeling of incompleteness," confidence: medium

## Where it works

No praised-design evidence found for this pattern.

## Where it fails

- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-005): reprint scarcity and product-line
  strain hampered new-player entry — "Due to a lack of reprints and
  scarcity, the system was strained by continuing forward while losing the
  ability to bring in new players."
- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-007): base-game content is explicitly
  capped without buying more product — "If you wish to play with more than
  two characters, you will need another copy of the base game."
- root (src-006): the official rules booklet itself is cited as amplifying
  the sense that the base game alone is incomplete, pushing players toward
  the full expansion set to get the "ideal" experience.
- dead-of-winter-the-long-night (src-004): a full-price standalone expansion's
  value proposition is questioned directly by a reviewer who already owns the
  base game.
- dominion (src-003, src-007): expansion/Kingdom-card breadth is named a minor
  fatigue cost even for a base game praised for otherwise-quick, accessible play.

## Coverage gaps

Current support for this label comes from reviewer commentary rather than
direct player-count survey data. dead-of-winter-the-long-night contributes from a
`needs_followup` source doc, which is why this pattern's status has stepped back to
draft pending re-verification. Games with large, ongoing expansion
catalogs — living card games (e.g. other LCGs beyond Arkham), legacy games,
and app-supported games with frequent content drops — would test whether
this "completeness pressure" framing holds outside campaign/asymmetric-board
designs.
