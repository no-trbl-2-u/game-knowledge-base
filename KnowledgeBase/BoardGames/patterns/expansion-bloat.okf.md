## Summary

Evidence is still narrow but no longer limited to one "completeness pressure"
shape. The original cases describe expansion-bloat as the base product being
fine on its own while the surrounding product line (more character copies,
more factions, more reprints) becomes an implicit requirement for the
"ideal" experience. Two new cases add a second shape: a sequel/expansion
product whose own value proposition is unclear against the base game it
extends (Dead of Winter: The Long Night, reviewer "not sure... worthwhile...
if you've already got the original") and a growing rules ecosystem that
accumulates community errata over time even without a campaign/asymmetric
board (Dominion's "expansion fatigue").

## Evidence by game

- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-005): Board Game Quest frames the revised
  core set as a response to reprint scarcity that "strained" the product
  line's ability to bring in new players, i.e. product-line sprawl and
  availability became an onboarding tax rather than a content benefit,
  confidence: medium
- dead-of-winter-the-long-night (src-004): reviewer is "not sure The Long Night is a
  worthwhile purchase if you've already got the original game", confidence: medium
- dominion (src-003, src-007): review lists "expansion fatigue" as a minor flaw;
  community errata article shows an ongoing, growing rules ecosystem, confidence: medium
- root (src-006): Gideon's Gaming notes expansion factions create a
  perceived-completeness pressure — "having them all is the ideal
  experience" — and that "the Law of Root booklet itself even heightens the
  feeling of incompleteness," confidence: medium

## Where it works

No praised-design evidence found for this pattern.

## Where it fails

- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-005): reprint scarcity and product-line
  strain hampered new-player entry — "Due to a lack of reprints and
  scarcity, the system was strained by continuing forward while losing the
  ability to bring in new players."
- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-007): base-game content is explicitly
  capped without buying more product — "If you wish to play with more than
  two characters, you will need another copy of the base game."
- root (src-006): the official rules booklet itself is cited as amplifying
  the sense that the base game alone is incomplete, pushing players toward
  the full expansion set to get the "ideal" experience.
- dead-of-winter-the-long-night (src-004): a sequel product whose incremental value
  over the base game is explicitly in doubt for an existing owner, a different
  complaint from "the base game alone feels incomplete."
- dominion (src-003, src-007): a long-running expansion line accumulates enough
  errata/rules drift that a reviewer names "expansion fatigue" as a flaw even though
  the base game itself is praised as accessible.

## Coverage gaps

Current support for this label comes from reviewer commentary rather than
direct player-count survey data. Dead of Winter: The Long Night's reception doc is
still `needs_followup`, which is why this doc's status drops to `draft` this pass.
Games with large, ongoing expansion catalogs — living card games (e.g. other LCGs
beyond Arkham), legacy games, and app-supported games with frequent content drops —
would further test whether "completeness pressure" or "unclear incremental value" is
the more common shape outside this sample.
