## Summary

Across this sample, "accessibility" friction is rarely about a single missing feature — it's a byproduct of ambition: heavy card text, dense rulebooks, tactical dice/combat systems, large physical footprints, mandatory peripherals (a companion app), and many-linked-systems designs all raise the entry cost for new, casual, or non-expert players. The recurring fix pattern is not simplification but *scaffolding*: icon reminders, guided first plays, official alternate-effect modes, compact reference formats, and systems-mapping aids let designs keep their depth while lowering the barrier to a first successful turn. The Crew is this corpus's first citable case of accessibility in the disability-accommodation sense specifically: its manual already supplies symbols alongside color so colorblind players aren't locked out, a narrower and more concrete kind of "accessibility" than the cognitive-load/table-footprint claims that dominate the rest of this evidence. Evidence is broad but individually thin — most bullets are single-review observations rather than corroborated consensus, so this synthesis should be read as directional, not definitive.

## Evidence by game

- aeons-end (src-001): BGG language-dependence signal flags the game as text-heavy — "Extensive use of text - massive conversion needed to be playable" — meaning non-native-language or reading-limited players face a real barrier, confidence: high
- ark-nova (src-008): A solo-focused review says the game board "is just too big for solo play (or in general, arguably)," a table-footprint/physical-space accessibility complaint, confidence: high
- dune-imperium (src-006): A BGG file-page commenter reports the square rulebook/reference-sheet format is awkward at the table and "You can NOT read it by holding it in one hand," an ergonomic/reference-accessibility complaint, confidence: medium
- forgotten-waters (src-011, src-006, src-009): a mandatory companion-app device is identified as a dealbreaker for tech-averse groups; the 2024 Entry Book substitutes paper for only the first five scenarios, confidence: high
- gaia-project (src-003, src-004): both reviews explain the game through many linked systems rather than one central action, suggesting a concise aid mapping research tracks/upgrades to unlocked resources/actions would help, confidence: medium
- kingdom-death-monster (src-002): The publisher's own product page frames the game as a 21-pound box played "over many nights," which the reception doc treats as a significant physical and time-commitment barrier, confidence: high (note: source doc status is needs_followup)
- seti-search-for-extraterrestrial-intelligence (src-002, src-004): card-use modes (main-action, free-action, mission, income) and recurring-income consequences are hard to compare at a glance; better if player aids addressed this, confidence: medium
- spirit-island (src-007): A cooperative-games review warns of "Heavy 'brain-burner' gameplay that may overwhelm some players," a cognitive-load accessibility barrier distinct from rules complexity alone, confidence: high
- the-crew (src-002): the manual already supplies symbols for color distinction; an accessibility improvement should extend, not replace, that existing color-symbol correspondence, confidence: high
- the-quacks-of-quedlinburg (src-004): A family-oriented review suggests new/younger players benefit from "an adult buddy the first play through," an entry-barrier accessibility observation, confidence: medium
- too-many-bones (src-004): A review distinguishes the game's fit for "seasoned gamers" from its barrier to casual players unfamiliar with tactical dice systems, confidence: medium (note: source doc status is needs_followup)

## Where it works

- ark-nova (src-004): The official FAQ formalizes an accessibility accommodation rather than leaving it to house rules — "If your group dislikes interactive abilities... use the alternate solo-game effects" — turning a social-friction point (forced player-vs-player poison/pilfering effects) into an opt-out mode.
- the-quacks-of-quedlinburg (src-004): Reviewers praise the game's icon-based reminders as lowering rules-recall burden for family/casual play ("icons that help remind players"), alongside its terrific-family-game framing.
- the-crew (src-002): built-in color+symbol redundancy is a genuine disability-accommodation case rather than a cognitive-load or table-footprint mitigation — the first of its kind in this pattern's evidence.

## Where it fails

- aeons-end (src-001): Extensive required card-text reading with no stated iconography/localization mitigation.
- dune-imperium (src-006): Physical reference materials (square rulebook/insert) are reported as hard to hold and read at the table.
- ark-nova (src-008): Full multiplayer board/table footprint is reported as excessive even for a single solo player.
- spirit-island (src-007): Simultaneous multi-spirit planning is called a brain-burner that can overwhelm some players outright, not merely challenge them.
- kingdom-death-monster (src-002): Sheer physical scale (21 lb) and multi-session campaign commitment gate entry before rules are even taught.
- too-many-bones (src-004): Tactical dice-based combat systems are reported as a casual-player deterrent independent of rules-teaching quality.
- forgotten-waters (src-006, src-009, src-011): requiring a companion-app device as the primary interface excludes tech-averse groups outright, a stronger barrier than any rules-complexity complaint in this set.
- gaia-project (src-003, src-004) and seti-search-for-extraterrestrial-intelligence (src-002, src-004): both heavy euros report that the barrier isn't any single rule but the number of linked systems/modes a player has to hold in mind at once, with no in-box aid to compare them at a glance.

## Coverage gaps

legendary-encounters-an-alien-deck-building-game carries the accessibility label in its frontmatter but neither reception doc pins a specific accessibility claim to a cited source — its friction notes (text-heavy cards, IP-specific knowledge) appear only as unsourced summary bullets, so it was excluded here. Three of the cited games (kingdom-death-monster, too-many-bones, forgotten-waters) still carry status: needs_followup on their reception docs, so this pattern's confidence should rise once those are re-verified. The Crew's color-symbol redundancy now gives this pattern its first visual-impairment-accommodation case, partially filling the earlier gap; physical/motor-dexterity accessibility (token/dice handling for players with limited mobility) remains unaddressed by any game in this corpus.
