## Summary

Across this now nine-game sample, "accessibility" friction is rarely about a single missing feature — it's a byproduct of ambition: heavy card text, dense rulebooks, tactical dice/combat systems, large physical footprints, and companion-app/device dependence all raise the entry cost for new, casual, tech-averse, or non-expert players. The recurring fix pattern is not simplification but *scaffolding*: icon reminders, guided first plays, official alternate-effect modes, compact reference formats, and — per The Crew — deliberately preserving redundant sensory cues (color-symbol correspondence) let designs keep their depth while lowering the barrier to a first successful turn. Forgotten Waters supplies a distinct failure mode not seen elsewhere in this sample: mandatory companion-app/device reliance is reported as an outright "dealbreaker" for tech-averse groups, and its 2024 print Entry Book only substitutes for five scenarios, leaving most of the campaign gated behind the device. Evidence is broad (9 of 10 games contributed a traceable claim) but individually thin — most bullets are single-review observations rather than corroborated consensus, so this synthesis should be read as directional, not definitive.

## Evidence by game

- aeons-end (src-001): BGG language-dependence signal flags the game as text-heavy — "Extensive use of text - massive conversion needed to be playable" — meaning non-native-language or reading-limited players face a real barrier, confidence: high
- ark-nova (src-008): A solo-focused review says the game board "is just too big for solo play (or in general, arguably)," a table-footprint/physical-space accessibility complaint, confidence: high
- dune-imperium (src-006): A BGG file-page commenter reports the square rulebook/reference-sheet format is awkward at the table and "You can NOT read it by holding it in one hand," an ergonomic/reference-accessibility complaint, confidence: medium
- kingdom-death-monster (src-002): The publisher's own product page frames the game as a 21-pound box played "over many nights," which the reception doc treats as a significant physical and time-commitment barrier, confidence: high (note: source doc status is needs_followup)
- spirit-island (src-007): A cooperative-games review warns of "Heavy 'brain-burner' gameplay that may overwhelm some players," a cognitive-load accessibility barrier distinct from rules complexity alone, confidence: high

- too-many-bones (src-004): A review distinguishes the game's fit for "seasoned gamers" from its barrier to casual players unfamiliar with tactical dice systems, confidence: medium (note: source doc status is needs_followup)

## Where it works

- ark-nova (src-004): The official FAQ formalizes an accessibility accommodation rather than leaving it to house rules — "If your group dislikes interactive abilities... use the alternate solo-game effects" — turning a social-friction point (forced player-vs-player poison/pilfering effects) into an opt-out mode.
- the-quacks-of-quedlinburg (src-004): Reviewers praise the game's icon-based reminders as lowering rules-recall burden for family/casual play ("icons that help remind players"), alongside its terrific-family-game framing.
- the-crew (src-002): The design's built-in color-symbol redundancy is held up as the accessibility feature to protect — any added accessibility aid should preserve that correspondence rather than override it.

## Where it fails

- aeons-end (src-001): Extensive required card-text reading with no stated iconography/localization mitigation.
- dune-imperium (src-006): Physical reference materials (square rulebook/insert) are reported as hard to hold and read at the table.
- ark-nova (src-008): Full multiplayer board/table footprint is reported as excessive even for a single solo player.
- spirit-island (src-007): Simultaneous multi-spirit planning is called a brain-burner that can overwhelm some players outright, not merely challenge them.
- kingdom-death-monster (src-002): Sheer physical scale (21 lb) and multi-session campaign commitment gate entry before rules are even taught.
- too-many-bones (src-004): Tactical dice-based combat systems are reported as a casual-player deterrent independent of rules-teaching quality.
- forgotten-waters (src-011): "Device reliance" and "component handling" are named as limiting which groups the game fits.
- forgotten-waters (src-006, src-009, src-011): The companion device is a "dealbreaker" for tech-averse groups, and the print Entry Book workaround only covers five of the campaign's scenarios — a partial, not full, accessibility fallback.

## Coverage gaps

legendary-encounters-an-alien-deck-building-game carries the accessibility label in its frontmatter but neither reception doc pins a specific accessibility claim to a cited source — its friction notes (text-heavy cards, IP-specific knowledge) appear only as unsourced summary bullets, so it was excluded here. Three of the nine cited games (kingdom-death-monster, too-many-bones, forgotten-waters) still carry status: needs_followup on their reception docs, so this pattern's confidence should rise once those are re-verified. Forgotten Waters is the first game in this sample to raise device/app dependence as an accessibility axis distinct from text density, table footprint, or cognitive load — the corpus would benefit from a second app-assisted or digitally-companioned game to test whether the "dealbreaker for tech-averse groups" complaint generalizes. None of the sampled games discuss accessibility for players with visual/motor impairments (colorblind-safe iconography beyond The Crew's redundancy point, physical dexterity for token/dice handling) — that remains an open gap.
