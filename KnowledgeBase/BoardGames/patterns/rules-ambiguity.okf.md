## Summary

Rules-ambiguity friction rarely shows up as "the rules are wrong." It shows up as scattered or hard-to-navigate authority: a ruling lives in the rulebook, a card, a walkthrough, and an FAQ, and none of those agree on precedence or are easy to find (Mage Knight's four-source fragmentation, Nemesis Lockdown's dense small-text rulebook with no index). A second failure shape appears where multiple subsystems are layered onto one ruleset — Earthborne Rangers' RPG, prewritten-story, and dynamic-storytelling layers "tend to step on each other's toes," and Terra Mystica's cult track "feels a little disconnected" from the rest of its action economy. The recurring fixes reviewers ask for are not simpler rules but better navigation: a single searchable place to resolve a dispute, an index, an explicit timing-window reference, or — as Betrayal Legacy demonstrates — a straightforward rules-formatting and layout revision that makes a notoriously ambiguous haunt system "much easier to actually implement," even though the underlying system is still called "rough and inconsistent" in places. Evidence is real but thin-to-moderate per game: most complaints come from single secondary reviews rather than aggregated player consensus, so treat this synthesis as directional, not exhaustive.

## Evidence by game

- ark-nova (src-007): Board Game Quest reports that endgame scoring "is not elegant" and forces players to "pull out the rule book" to resolve it, confidence: high
- dune-imperium (src-004): The official FAQ carries repeated timing/definition clarifications (Intrigue cards, optional effects, Reveal turn, troop counts, Alliance, "when you win a Conflict"), indicating a real edge-case load behind the base rulebook, confidence: high
- legendary-encounters-an-alien-deck-building-game (src-003): A reviewer flags that timing between completing an objective and the hive deck spawning "can be an issue," i.e. an unresolved sequencing question at the table, confidence: medium
- mage-knight (src-011): A BGG fan critique complains rules are split across the rulebook, the Walkthrough booklet, individual cards, and the FAQ/site, with no single point of authority, confidence: medium
- root (src-003): Leder's official Rules Library exists specifically to adjudicate disputes such as whether "cannot" is absolute and who resolves ambiguous simultaneous-turn order, confidence: high
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008): A reviewer accidentally shuffled the Summon deck (which must stay ordered/searchable) because the rulebook didn't flag the exception, and separately found post-Act-III progression "very unclear" without prior video-game knowledge, confidence: high
- betrayal-legacy (src-005): "better rules formatting and layout" make "Haunts... much easier to actually implement" versus the base game, though the system is still described as "rough and inconsistent" despite the clearer haunt formatting, confidence: medium
- earthborne-rangers (src-004, src-005): the three subsystems (RPG, prewritten-story, dynamic-storytelling) "tend to step on each other's toes," with better-if asking that the ecosystem "remain legible without requiring players to reconcile every subsystem at once", confidence: medium
- nemesis (src-006, src-007, src-010): rules load and exceptions "can feel fiddly or disjointed", confidence: medium
- nemesis-lockdown (src-008, src-009): "dense small text, no index" forces repeated rulebook reference, and the rulebook is separately called "obtuse," needing "frequent cross-checking," with better-if asking for "an index" and "clearer cross-references", confidence: high
- terra-mystica (src-002, src-003): the "cultist track feels a little disconnected," with better-if asking that "its cult track were integrated more clearly", confidence: medium

## Where it works

- betrayal-legacy (src-005): reviewers credit "better rules formatting and layout" with making "Haunts... much easier to actually implement" than the base game's notoriously inconsistent haunt cards — the corpus's first sighted example of a rules-clarity fix being directly praised, rather than an FAQ/rules-library merely documenting ambiguity that needed resolving. Where an official FAQ or rules library appears elsewhere in this evidence set (dune-imperium src-004, root src-003), it remains cited as documentation of ambiguity that needed resolving, not as praised design.

## Where it fails

- ark-nova (src-007): elegant-looking endgame scoring collapses into a rulebook lookup at the table.
- dune-imperium (src-004): the density of official FAQ timing rulings signals the base rules under-specify several key windows.
- legendary-encounters-an-alien-deck-building-game (src-003): objective-completion vs. hive-spawn timing is ambiguous enough to be called out as "an issue."
- mage-knight (src-011): authoritative rulings are fragmented across four different physical/official sources with no single index.
- root (src-003): even with an official living rules library, base concepts like "cannot" and simultaneous-turn precedence needed explicit adjudication.
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008): genre-breaking instructions (don't shuffle this deck) and post-campaign progression steps go unexplained for players without outside (video-game) context.
- earthborne-rangers (src-004, src-005): the three subsystems (RPG, prewritten-story, and dynamic-storytelling) "tend to step on each other's toes," and better-if asks for the ecosystem to "remain legible without requiring players to reconcile every subsystem at once."
- nemesis-lockdown (src-008, src-009): "dense small text, no index" and an "obtuse" rulebook force "frequent cross-checking"; better-if asks for "an index" and "clearer cross-references."
- terra-mystica (src-002, src-003): the "cultist track feels a little disconnected," with better-if asking that "its cult track were integrated more clearly."
- betrayal-legacy (src-005): despite the clearer haunt formatting, the system is still called "rough and inconsistent" overall.

## Coverage gaps

hoplomachus-remastered and spirit-island carry the rules-ambiguity label in the corpus but did not yield citable evidence here: hoplomachus's better-if doc only infers ambiguity risk from rulebook structure and FAQ existence (no reviewer complaint sighted), and spirit-island's rules-ambiguity bullet in its better-if doc lacks an attached source/evidence pair. Betrayal Legacy now supplies this pattern's first praised-design example, but it's a qualified one — the same reviewer still calls the overall system "rough and inconsistent" — so the corpus still lacks a clean, unqualified "this rulebook solved ambiguity" case. Nemesis's "fiddly or disjointed" claim (src-006, src-007, src-010) is broad rather than a concrete example; a follow-up naming the specific rule or exception in dispute would sharpen it. betrayal-legacy and nemesis-lockdown's underlying docs are still needs_followup, so their claims should be revisited once verified. The corpus would still benefit from a game with heavier, well-documented errata history (e.g. a wargame or living-rules title with an active BGG rules-forum trail) to strengthen this pattern with primary-source ruling disputes rather than reviewer paraphrase.
