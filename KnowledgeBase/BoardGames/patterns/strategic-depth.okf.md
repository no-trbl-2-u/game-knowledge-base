## Summary

Strategic depth splits into three failure modes across the corpus: too much randomness swamping planning (Ark Nova's oversized deck, Heat's catch-up), too little decision space once a system is mastered or under-modded (Spirit Island's "solved" late rounds, Heroes of Terrinoth's scarce clever plays, GI JOE's shallow synergy ceiling), and too little *content* to sustain depth once a system is learned (Bloodborne and Forgotten Waters are both praised for their core loop and separately criticized for running out of fresh scenarios/upgrades quickly). The strongest praised solutions convert apparent randomness into accountable planning (Aeon's End's no-shuffle deck), make mastery itself the depth — asymmetric opposing systems that must be learned, not just your own (Root, Terra Mystica) — or layer alternate win conditions/tactical reserves onto a simpler core loop (Dark Pact's Pacts and multipliers). Evidence is broad (19 games contributed something usable) but individually thin per game — mostly single review quotes.

## Where it works

- aeons-end (src-004): eliminating deck shuffling converts randomness into a planning problem, so losses are diagnosable as choice errors rather than luck.
- root (src-006): asymmetric faction engines create a "high skill ceiling" that reviewers explicitly praise as depth, not just complexity.
- spirit-island (src-005): despite late-game predictability complaints, the same review calls the system a "high strategy game with a ton of variety, player choice, and replay value."
- dark-pact (src-007, src-009, src-010): alternate victory conditions and immediate-use card acquisition are praised as the specific mechanisms that keep the core loop tactically varied.
- dead-of-winter-a-crossroads-game (src-005): the Crossroads card system is singled out as "brilliant" for the weight and extraordinary moments it injects into an otherwise standard cooperative loop.
- earthborne-rangers (src-004): contextual card ecosystems are credited with "a gigantic leap forward" for the card-game niche.
- terra-mystica (src-002, src-003, src-004): 14 factions with deterministic, low-luck victory parameters are praised as high-strategy design.
- the-crew (src-003, src-004): the cooperative trick-taking combination is praised as tense and satisfying, explicitly said to flip familiar genre expectations.
- unmatched-battle-of-legends-volume-one (src-003): a small, fast-taught ruleset is praised for sustaining depth through character asymmetry rather than rules complexity.

## Where it fails

- ark-nova (src-008): a large, high-variance card deck can outrun a player's ability to plan around it.
- gi-joe-deck-building-game (src-005): card synergy exists but doesn't run deep enough for players seeking more from deck construction.
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-005): the unmodded base race can feel "overly simplistic" for experienced players — depth is bolted on via optional modules rather than built into the core loop.
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-006): a narrow verb set and repetitive Rest/administration turns limit space for clever play.
- root (src-006): the same asymmetry that produces depth also produces a mastery burden — understanding every opponent faction, not just your own — that reviewers flag as a barrier.
- spirit-island (src-005): predictable base-game fear/blight cycling lets attentive players "solve" outcomes several rounds ahead, flattening late-game decisions.
- bloodborne-the-card-game (src-005, src-006): a 32-card upgrade deck and five bosses are reported to expose most of the game's content quickly, despite praise for the core risk/reward loop.
- dark-pact (src-008, src-010): critical reviews contrast lengthy card combinations with comparatively modest payoff.
- elder-sign (src-002): no penalty for avoiding the hardest adventures lets cautious play skip the game's most meaningful decisions entirely.
- forgotten-waters (src-011, src-012): much of play reads as "sameish" with only critical moments demanding real tactics, and base-box scenario content is limited enough that further content ships as separate paid DLC.
- terra-mystica (src-002, src-004): the cult-track scoring lane feels disconnected from the rest of the system, and an ahead player's counterplay options are reported as thin — "block them, and that's about it."

## Coverage gaps

Two of the ten originally-tagged games (arkham-horror-the-card-game, hoplomachus-remastered) carry the strategic-depth label but their reception docs had no claim actually about decision space, solved play, or choice quality — only onboarding/setup/rules-clarity evidence — so they were excluded here. This pass adds a genuine competitive-euro counterexample (Terra Mystica, high-strategy but with a thin late-game counterplay lever) alongside several co-op/card-game cases, narrowing but not closing the gap the previous pass flagged.
