## Summary

Strategic depth splits into three failure modes now that the sample has broadened: too much randomness swamping planning (Ark Nova's oversized deck, Heat's catch-up, Elder Sign's dice-gated tasks), too little decision space once a system is mastered, under-modded, or dominated by a no-risk avoidance strategy (Spirit Island's "solved" late rounds, Heroes of Terrinoth's scarce clever plays, GI JOE's shallow synergy ceiling, Elder Sign's ignore-the-hard-adventure exploit, Forgotten Waters' repetitive skill checks), and administrative overhead diluting otherwise-real combinatorial depth (Dark Pact's long combo chains with weak payoff, Nemesis: Lockdown's complexity obscuring meaningful choices). A third thread is thin counterplay: when the only answer to a leading player is blocking a scarce resource (Terra Mystica), reviewers read the strategic promise as narrower than advertised. The strongest praised solutions convert apparent randomness into accountable planning (Aeon's End's no-shuffle deck), make mastery itself the depth — asymmetric opposing systems that must be learned, not just your own (Root, Unmatched) — or preserve a genuinely broad content pool so encounters don't become familiar quickly (the standalone-scope caveat raised for Bloodborne: The Card Game). Evidence is broad but individually thin per game — mostly single review quotes.

## Where it works

- aeons-end (src-004): eliminating deck shuffling converts randomness into a planning problem, so losses are diagnosable as choice errors rather than luck.
- root (src-006): asymmetric faction engines create a "high skill ceiling" that reviewers explicitly praise as depth, not just complexity.
- spirit-island (src-005): despite late-game predictability complaints, the same review calls the system a "high strategy game with a ton of variety, player choice, and replay value."
- unmatched-battle-of-legends-volume-one (src-003): rules-bendy character asymmetry is named the game's strongest feature, and combat's attacker/defender bluffing is credited as the "intelligent part" of otherwise simple fights.

## Where it fails

- ark-nova (src-008): a large, high-variance card deck can outrun a player's ability to plan around it.
- gi-joe-deck-building-game (src-005): card synergy exists but doesn't run deep enough for players seeking more from deck construction.
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-005): the unmodded base race can feel "overly simplistic" for experienced players — depth is bolted on via optional modules rather than built into the core loop.
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-006): a narrow verb set and repetitive Rest/administration turns limit space for clever play.
- root (src-006): the same asymmetry that produces depth also produces a mastery burden — understanding every opponent faction, not just your own — that reviewers flag as a barrier.
- spirit-island (src-005): predictable base-game fear/blight cycling lets attentive players "solve" outcomes several rounds ahead, flattening late-game decisions.
- elder-sign (src-002): a costless avoidance strategy (skip the hard adventures) removes the risk/reward tension the game is built on.
- forgotten-waters (src-012): routine skill-check and resource choices repeat enough to read as "sameish" outside a few tactically demanding moments.
- dark-pact (src-008, src-010): long combinatorial chains can produce more handling than strategic payoff.
- terra-mystica (src-004): the only answer to a leading player is blocking scarce land — a single, thin counterplay lever for an otherwise deep engine.

## Coverage gaps

Two of the originally-tagged games (arkham-horror-the-card-game, hoplomachus-remastered) carry the strategic-depth label but their reception docs had no claim actually about decision space, solved play, or choice quality — only onboarding/setup/rules-clarity evidence — so they were excluded here. dead-of-winter-a-crossroads-game and nemesis-lockdown contribute from `needs_followup` source docs and should be treated as provisional. The sample now includes competitive-euro (Terra Mystica) and direct-conflict (Unmatched) evidence, closing the earlier gap toward co-op/deckbuilder-only coverage; it still lacks a heavy competitive-euro game with an explicit, well-corroborated "dominant strategy" complaint stronger than Elder Sign's avoidance exploit.
