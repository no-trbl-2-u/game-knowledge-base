## Summary

Persistent state is admired when it is legible — a scar, an unlocked location, a
branching story flag, a slightly better deck, a visibly signaled difficulty or
completion state — and resented when it becomes bookkeeping burden, an unkept
promise, or a physical constraint on how many campaigns can even run. The sharpest
complaints are not "add a campaign" but "tell me clearly what persists, what resets,
and what unlocks between sessions": quest-based games that never link sessions leave
players feeling growth was wasted, heavier campaign games draw complaints about the
labor of carrying state forward, and dual-track systems (an investigator's growth
alongside an escalating antagonist, as in Cthulhu: Death May Die) are asked to be
reinforced visibly at the table rather than left to memory. Between-session logistics
matter beyond bookkeeping labor, too: Imperial Assault's between-mission
administration is asked to preserve narrative continuity without demanding a fixed
recurring group, while Arydia's physical component state can constrain how many
campaigns are even ongoing at once — a hardware-bound limitation distinct from the
labor complaints elsewhere. Evidence here is moderate: a handful of games (Heroes of
Terrinoth, Slay the Spire, Cthulhu: Death May Die) supply high-confidence,
quote-backed claims; the rest supply real but thinner medium-confidence signal, and
kingdom-death-monster's and arydia-the-paths-we-dare-tread's contributions rest on
docs still marked `needs_followup`.

## Evidence by game

- gloomhaven (src-003): the campaign layer is described as persistent play with new unlocked locations and branching story decisions carried across sessions, confidence: medium
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005, src-006): both reviews explicitly regret the lack of quest-to-quest continuity — upgrades reset after each quest and character growth "lacks earned power" because there is no campaign layer, confidence: high
- kingdom-death-monster (src-004): the reviewer's mechanism list treats campaign progression as integrated with tactical combat, role-playing, and storytelling rather than a bolt-on system, confidence: medium (source doc status: needs_followup)
- mage-knight (src-012): a BGG ratings comment wants more connective tissue, stating "narration as campaign or events are missing" from the otherwise-praised optimization puzzle, confidence: medium
- oathsworn-into-the-deepwood (src-005): the campaign's world-state "changes and develops from player actions," but this persistence is flagged as a bookkeeping-burden risk rather than pure upside, confidence: medium
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008): a reviewer unfamiliar with the source video game found moving forward after Act III "very unclear," having to lean on outside video-game knowledge to know what persists, resets, or unlocks, confidence: high
- arydia-the-paths-we-dare-tread (src-004): physical component state "can constrain concurrent campaigns" — only one campaign can be ongoing at a time, confidence: medium (source doc status: needs_followup)
- cthulhu-death-may-die (src-002): investigator skills level at sanity thresholds while the Elder One reveals sequential Stages, giving both sides a legible progression track, though reviewers want the two tracks "reinforced at the table," confidence: medium
- star-wars-imperial-assault (src-003, src-004): four Rebel characters (five total participants) may give "the strongest experience," with scaling below that weaker, and between-mission administration "should preserve narrative continuity" without requiring a full recurring group, confidence: medium
- the-crew (src-002, src-004): mission progression "benefits from visibly signaling difficulty and completion," confidence: medium

## Where it works

- gloomhaven (src-003): persistent play, newly unlocked locations, and branching story decisions are presented as the campaign's core draw rather than overhead.
- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-007): scenario completion translating directly into better cards (with scars as a persistent cost) gives campaign state a clear, felt payoff each session.
- kingdom-death-monster (src-004): reviewer praise groups campaign progression together with combat, role-playing, and storytelling as one integrated identity rather than a separate system layered on top (source doc status: needs_followup).
- cthulhu-death-may-die (src-002): investigator skills leveling at sanity thresholds alongside the Elder One's sequential Stage reveals gives both sides of the game a legible, escalating progression track.

## Where it fails

- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005, src-006): no campaign layer means upgrades reset each quest; both reviewers independently name this as the game's clearest missed opportunity despite otherwise strong praise.
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008): post-Act-III progression (what persists, what resets, what unlocks) was "very unclear" without prior video-game familiarity — a ritualized reset procedure is the implied fix.
- oathsworn-into-the-deepwood (src-005): persistent world-state across chapters is flagged as a likely bookkeeping burden even where reviewers admire the design intent.
- mage-knight (src-012): players who want the strong solo/co-op puzzle to feel more like an adventure specifically miss campaign or event narration connecting sessions.
- arydia-the-paths-we-dare-tread (src-004): physical component state "can constrain concurrent campaigns," since only one campaign can be ongoing at a time — a hardware limitation rather than a bookkeeping one.
- star-wars-imperial-assault (src-003, src-004): the strongest experience is scoped to four Rebel characters (five participants), and between-mission administration is asked to preserve narrative continuity even without a stable recurring group.
- cthulhu-death-may-die (src-002): reviewers want the investigator and Elder One progression tracks "reinforced at the table" rather than left implicit.

## Coverage gaps

kingdom-death-monster and arydia-the-paths-we-dare-tread are both games whose
contributions here rest on docs still marked `needs_followup`, so their claims should
be treated as provisional until a librarian/audit pass strengthens them.
gi-joe-deck-building-game carries the `campaign-progression` label in the index but
its own better-if doc only offers a low-confidence inference about resetting between
missions, not a real reviewer claim, so it contributed nothing usable to this
synthesis. Cthulhu: Death May Die and The Crew are the first verified-status
contributions with concrete progression-signaling claims, but neither supplies a
measured session-count or transition-time figure, so the corpus still lacks hard
numbers on session-to-session overhead in this pattern specifically.
