## Summary

Persistent state is admired when it is legible — a scar, an unlocked location, a
branching story flag, a slightly better deck — and resented when it becomes either
bookkeeping burden or an unkept promise. The sharpest complaints are not "add a
campaign" but "tell me clearly what persists, what resets, and what unlocks between
sessions": quest-based games that never link sessions leave players feeling growth
was wasted, while heavier campaign games draw complaints about the labor of carrying
state forward rather than about having state at all. New evidence adds the corpus's
strongest praised-design anchor for this pattern: Betrayal Legacy's narrative/identity
payoff ("the first legacy game I'm actually excited to continue owning") and Descent
(2e)'s "the campaign's the best bit of Descent" both show campaign structure praised
as the headline feature rather than tolerated overhead, while Arydia's single-save-slot
physical-state limitation shows the carrying-state-forward labor complaint at its most
literal. Evidence here is moderate: Heroes of Terrinoth, Slay the Spire, Betrayal
Legacy, and Descent (2e) supply high-confidence, quote-backed claims; the rest supply
real but thinner medium-confidence signal, and kingdom-death-monster's contribution
rests on a doc still marked `needs_followup`.

## Evidence by game

- gloomhaven (src-003): the campaign layer is described as persistent play with new unlocked locations and branching story decisions carried across sessions, confidence: medium
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005, src-006): both reviews explicitly regret the lack of quest-to-quest continuity — upgrades reset after each quest and character growth "lacks earned power" because there is no campaign layer, confidence: high
- kingdom-death-monster (src-004): the reviewer's mechanism list treats campaign progression as integrated with tactical combat, role-playing, and storytelling rather than a bolt-on system, confidence: medium (source doc status: needs_followup)
- mage-knight (src-012): a BGG ratings comment wants more connective tissue, stating "narration as campaign or events are missing" from the otherwise-praised optimization puzzle, confidence: medium
- oathsworn-into-the-deepwood (src-005): the campaign's world-state "changes and develops from player actions," but this persistence is flagged as a bookkeeping-burden risk rather than pure upside, confidence: medium
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008): a reviewer unfamiliar with the source video game found moving forward after Act III "very unclear," having to lean on outside video-game knowledge to know what persists, resets, or unlocks, confidence: high
- arydia-the-paths-we-dare-tread (src-004): review testimony says only one game can be ongoing at a time because so much of the campaign's state lives in physical components, confidence: medium
- betrayal-legacy (src-005, src-006): one reviewer calls the legacy elements "as good as any I've ever seen" and says it's "the first legacy game I'm actually excited to continue owning," while another credits the design with "building identities for the players at the table" through persistent narrative choices, confidence: high
- cthulhu-death-may-die (src-002): investigator skills level at sanity thresholds while the Elder One advances through sequential Stage reveals, giving both sides of the table a visible, mechanically-tied progression track, confidence: high
- descent-journeys-in-the-dark-second-edition (src-005): a review search result states plainly, "The campaign's the best bit of Descent," confidence: medium
- star-wars-imperial-assault (src-001, src-004): BGG calls the campaign a "cinematic tale" and an independent review describes clear scenario-by-scenario progression, confidence: high
- the-crew (src-002, src-004): the manual organizes 50 missions with a difficulty progression, and an independent review confirms missions increase in complexity as the campaign advances, confidence: high

## Where it works

- gloomhaven (src-003): persistent play, newly unlocked locations, and branching story decisions are presented as the campaign's core draw rather than overhead.
- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-007): scenario completion translating directly into better cards (with scars as a persistent cost) gives campaign state a clear, felt payoff each session.
- kingdom-death-monster (src-004): reviewer praise groups campaign progression together with combat, role-playing, and storytelling as one integrated identity rather than a separate system layered on top (source doc status: needs_followup).
- betrayal-legacy (src-005, src-006): this is the pattern's strongest praised-design anchor — one reviewer says the legacy design is "as good as any I've ever seen," the other credits it with building real player identity across sessions.
- descent-journeys-in-the-dark-second-edition (src-005): a review names the campaign outright as "the best bit" of the game, ahead of any other system.
- star-wars-imperial-assault (src-001, src-004): the campaign is praised both by BGG's framing ("cinematic tale") and by independent review as a well-structured scenario-by-scenario arc.
- the-crew (src-002, src-004): the campaign's 50-mission structure is confirmed to step up in complexity as it progresses, giving session-to-session advancement a felt payoff.
- cthulhu-death-may-die (src-002): skill-threshold leveling tied to sanity, paired with sequential Elder One Stage reveals, gives both sides of the table a visible, synchronized progression track.

## Where it fails

- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005, src-006): no campaign layer means upgrades reset each quest; both reviewers independently name this as the game's clearest missed opportunity despite otherwise strong praise.
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008): post-Act-III progression (what persists, what resets, what unlocks) was "very unclear" without prior video-game familiarity — a ritualized reset procedure is the implied fix.
- oathsworn-into-the-deepwood (src-005): persistent world-state across chapters is flagged as a likely bookkeeping burden even where reviewers admire the design intent.
- mage-knight (src-012): players who want the strong solo/co-op puzzle to feel more like an adventure specifically miss campaign or event narration connecting sessions.
- arydia-the-paths-we-dare-tread (src-004): because so much campaign state lives in physical components, only one game can be ongoing at a time — the sharpest "carrying state forward is a literal bookkeeping burden" example in the corpus.
