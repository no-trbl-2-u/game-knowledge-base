## Summary

Component-clarity friction spans four recurring shapes across this sample: physical legibility (icon systems, reference-card layout), physical handling and durability (chip wear, tracker clips that slip loose and can damage cardboard), visual/art presentation (drab or low-contrast card art), and table-scale readability (crowded tiles, room/card information that's hard to inspect across a large shared table). Heavier euros and app-adjacent adaptations (Ark Nova, Mage Knight, Slay the Spire) draw complaints that icon-heavy systems demand repeated rulebook/reference lookups rather than resolving from the table state; legacy games with physical trackers (Betrayal Legacy) add a durability failure mode on top of that; and large-footprint cooperative games (Nemesis) and miniature-heavy scenario games (Cthulhu: Death May Die) show that even a complete, well-organized component list can still crowd out physical space at the table. Lighter, family-weight games (Quacks) draw complaints about wear on handled components rather than legibility. The clean praised-design examples remain narrow: Quacks' in-line status icons and Cthulhu: Death May Die's complete per-box component lists both suggest that small, low-cost documentation and iconography choices are effective fixes for memory load — a much narrower intervention than the "ruthless visual hierarchy" heavy games seem to need.

## Evidence by game

- ark-nova (src-009): Tabletop Gaming's review concludes "component and design irks" keep the game from being the best it could be, using the phrase "veer towards ugly duckling," confidence: medium
- dune-imperium (src-006): a BGG file-page commenter called the square rulebook/reference-sheet format hard to hold and read one-handed and "awkward to use and find space for at the table," confidence: medium
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-006): Co-op Board Games' review lists "Player board iconography requires learning" as a friction point for new players, confidence: medium
- mage-knight (src-012): BGG Ultimate Edition rating comments describe "rules checking is 30% of playing time" and "really fiddly rules," tied to the game's site/enemy-effect iconography needing multiple lookups, confidence: medium
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-007, src-010): Rolling In The Meep's dislikes list cites fiddly tracking and "many cubes/effects," and Miniature Market retail snippets call setup "a little lengthy" and "a bit heavy," confidence: high
- the-quacks-of-quedlinburg (src-005): a reviewer reports "beginning to notice wear on the chips" after just five plays and calls the plastic chips "a bit expensive" to replace, confidence: high
- betrayal-legacy (src-005): "clips ... will inevitably slip around" — tracker components fail to stay put and "can slip or damage cardboard", confidence: medium
- bloodborne-the-card-game (src-006): criticizes the "drab palette and game-render card art," with better-if asking for "higher-contrast presentation", confidence: medium
- cthulhu-death-may-die (src-003): praises that "the Elder One and scenario boxes include the full lists of components," though "You will run out of room for miniatures on some of the tiles," pointing to a need for "a documented stacking or token-substitution convention", confidence: high
- nemesis (src-005, src-008): room/card info is "hard to inspect across a large table," with better-if asking for "a readable personal flow aid" beyond the two shared quick-reference cards, confidence: medium

## Where it works

- the-quacks-of-quedlinburg (src-004): The Board Game Family's review praises the game's "icons that help remind players" of ingredient effects, crediting them alongside variable setup for the game's family-friendly accessibility, confidence: high
- cthulhu-death-may-die (src-003): "I appreciate that the Elder One and scenario boxes include the full lists of components" — a reviewer crediting complete, well-organized component manifests as a clarity win, even though the same review flags crowded miniature space on some tiles (see Where it fails).

## Where it fails

- ark-nova (src-009): "component and design irks" are cited as the reason the game falls short of being the best it could be, confidence: medium
- dune-imperium (src-006): the rulebook/reference-sheet physical format is "bad for using and reading it," awkward to hold and to fit at the table, confidence: medium
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-006): player-board iconography "requires learning," slowing onboarding for new players, confidence: medium
- mage-knight (src-012): fiddly rules push rules-checking to roughly 30% of playing time, implicating unclear iconography/reference design, confidence: medium
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-007): fiddly cube/effect tracking and a "massive box/table footprint" are named dislikes, confidence: high
- the-quacks-of-quedlinburg (src-005): repeated handling wears visible marks into the chips within a handful of plays, confidence: high
- betrayal-legacy (src-005): tracker clips "will inevitably slip around" and "can slip or damage cardboard" — a durability failure mode distinct from the legibility complaints elsewhere in this pattern.
- bloodborne-the-card-game (src-006): the "drab palette and game-render card art" draws a better-if call for "higher-contrast presentation."
- nemesis (src-005, src-008): room and card information is "hard to inspect across a large table," with better-if asking for a personal quick-reference beyond the two shared cards.

## Coverage gaps

Every citable claim here comes from secondary reviews, not from direct component/graphic-design analysis. The "where it works well" side now has a second data point — Cthulhu: Death May Die's complete component manifests, alongside Quacks' status icons — but both are narrow, low-cost documentation fixes rather than evidence that a full graphic-design or iconography overhaul was praised. betrayal-legacy and bloodborne-the-card-game's underlying docs are still needs_followup, so their claims should be revisited once those records are verified. A game whose reviewers specifically credit iconography or a reference-card redesign for resolving prior complaints (e.g., a revised/second edition), and a source addressing Nemesis's or Cthulhu's crowded-table problem directly, would strengthen this pattern considerably.
