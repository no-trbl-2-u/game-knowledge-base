## Summary

Component-clarity friction shows up two ways across this sample: physical legibility (icon systems, reference cards, board/graphic design) and physical handling (chip durability, table footprint, tracking overhead). Heavier euros and app-adjacent adaptations (Ark Nova, Mage Knight, Slay the Spire) draw complaints that icon-heavy systems demand repeated rulebook/reference lookups rather than resolving from the table state. Lighter, family-weight games (Quacks) draw complaints about physical wear on handled components rather than legibility. The one clean praised-design example (Quacks' status icons) suggests small in-line reminder icons are an effective, low-cost fix for memory load — a much narrower intervention than the "ruthless visual hierarchy" heavy games seem to need. Two new praised-design examples narrow this further: Betrayal Legacy's rules-formatting pass and Cthulhu: Death May Die's complete per-box component lists both show that *reference clarity* (what a component does, what's in a box) is a cheaper, more frequently solved sub-problem than physical board/table legibility, which stays unsolved everywhere it's complained about (Nemesis, Ark Nova, Dune Imperium).

## Evidence by game

- ark-nova (src-009): Tabletop Gaming's review concludes "component and design irks" keep the game from being the best it could be, using the phrase "veer towards ugly duckling," confidence: medium
- dune-imperium (src-006): a BGG file-page commenter called the square rulebook/reference-sheet format hard to hold and read one-handed and "awkward to use and find space for at the table," confidence: medium
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-006): Co-op Board Games' review lists "Player board iconography requires learning" as a friction point for new players, confidence: medium
- mage-knight (src-012): BGG Ultimate Edition rating comments describe "rules checking is 30% of playing time" and "really fiddly rules," tied to the game's site/enemy-effect iconography needing multiple lookups, confidence: medium
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-007, src-010): Rolling In The Meep's dislikes list cites fiddly tracking and "many cubes/effects," and Miniature Market retail snippets call setup "a little lengthy" and "a bit heavy," confidence: high
- the-quacks-of-quedlinburg (src-005): a reviewer reports "beginning to notice wear on the chips" after just five plays and calls the plastic chips "a bit expensive" to replace, confidence: high
- betrayal-legacy (src-005): the reviewer says the clips used for the legacy box can slip or damage cardboard, confidence: medium
- bloodborne-the-card-game (src-006): Meeple Mountain criticizes the drab visual palette and game-render card art, confidence: high
- cthulhu-death-may-die (src-003): the reviewer says players "will run out of room for miniatures on some of the tiles," confidence: high
- nemesis (src-005, src-008): reviews praise production quality while noting the large table footprint and the need for a rulebook plus two separate quick-reference cards to navigate a dense information set, confidence: medium

## Where it works

- the-quacks-of-quedlinburg (src-004): The Board Game Family's review praises the game's "icons that help remind players" of ingredient effects, crediting them alongside variable setup for the game's family-friendly accessibility, confidence: high
- betrayal-legacy (src-005): the same reviewer who flags clip durability separately praises "better rules formatting and layout," saying "Haunts are much easier to actually implement" than in the prior edition — a reference-clarity fix independent of the physical-component complaint.
- cthulhu-death-may-die (src-003): the reviewer appreciates that "the Elder One and scenario boxes include the full lists of components," crediting complete per-box inventories as a clarity aid distinct from the miniature-storage complaint about the same game.

## Coverage gaps

Every citable claim here comes from secondary reviews, not from direct component/graphic-design analysis. The "works well" side now has three entries instead of one, but two of them (Betrayal Legacy, Cthulhu: Death May Die) are about reference/documentation clarity rather than board or icon legibility — physical board/table legibility specifically (the complaint behind Ark Nova, Dune Imperium, Mage Knight, Nemesis) still has no praised-design counterexample anywhere in the corpus. A revised/second edition whose reviewers specifically credit an iconography or board-legibility redesign would close that gap.
