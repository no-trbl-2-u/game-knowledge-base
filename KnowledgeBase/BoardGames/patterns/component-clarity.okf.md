## Summary

Component-clarity friction shows up two ways across this sample: physical legibility (icon systems, reference cards, board/graphic design, visual contrast) and physical handling (chip durability, table footprint, tracking overhead). Heavier euros and app-adjacent adaptations (Ark Nova, Mage Knight, Slay the Spire) draw complaints that icon-heavy systems demand repeated rulebook/reference lookups rather than resolving from the table state; two new games this pass add a visual-contrast angle (Bloodborne's "drab visual palette" criticized for slowing quick reads in a timing-sensitive game) and a reference-aid angle (Nemesis's shared quick references reported as needing to carry more information than two cards can support). Lighter, family-weight games (Quacks) draw complaints about physical wear on handled components rather than legibility. The one clean praised-design example (Quacks' status icons) suggests small in-line reminder icons are an effective, low-cost fix for memory load — a much narrower intervention than the "ruthless visual hierarchy" heavy games seem to need.

## Evidence by game

- ark-nova (src-009): Tabletop Gaming's review concludes "component and design irks" keep the game from being the best it could be, using the phrase "veer towards ugly duckling," confidence: medium
- bloodborne-the-card-game (src-006): Meeple Mountain specifically criticizes the drab visual palette, supporting higher-contrast presentation for quick reading in the game's simultaneous, timing-sensitive play, confidence: medium
- dune-imperium (src-006): a BGG file-page commenter called the square rulebook/reference-sheet format hard to hold and read one-handed and "awkward to use and find space for at the table," confidence: medium
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-006): Co-op Board Games' review lists "Player board iconography requires learning" as a friction point for new players, confidence: medium
- mage-knight (src-012): BGG Ultimate Edition rating comments describe "rules checking is 30% of playing time" and "really fiddly rules," tied to the game's site/enemy-effect iconography needing multiple lookups, confidence: medium
- nemesis (src-005, src-008): the review reports the existing pair of shared quick references must support a large, information-dense table, supporting a readable personal flow aid for the Event phase, bag development, and final engine/coordinate/contamination/objective sequence, confidence: medium
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-007, src-010): Rolling In The Meep's dislikes list cites fiddly tracking and "many cubes/effects," and Miniature Market retail snippets call setup "a little lengthy" and "a bit heavy," confidence: high
- the-quacks-of-quedlinburg (src-005): a reviewer reports "beginning to notice wear on the chips" after just five plays and calls the plastic chips "a bit expensive" to replace, confidence: high

## Where it works

- the-quacks-of-quedlinburg (src-004): The Board Game Family's review praises the game's "icons that help remind players" of ingredient effects, crediting them alongside variable setup for the game's family-friendly accessibility, confidence: high

No other game in this sample produced a review quote that explicitly praises a component-clarity design choice (icon system, board legibility, or reference-card design) as working well; the remaining evidence in this corpus run is friction-only.

## Where it fails

- ark-nova (src-009): "component and design irks" are cited as the reason the game falls short of being the best it could be, confidence: medium
- bloodborne-the-card-game (src-006): a drab visual palette is flagged as slowing the quick reads a simultaneous, timing-sensitive game depends on, confidence: medium
- dune-imperium (src-006): the rulebook/reference-sheet physical format is "bad for using and reading it," awkward to hold and to fit at the table, confidence: medium
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-006): player-board iconography "requires learning," slowing onboarding for new players, confidence: medium

- the-quacks-of-quedlinburg (src-005): repeated handling wears visible marks into the chips within a handful of plays, confidence: high

## Coverage gaps

Every citable claim here comes from secondary reviews, not from direct component/graphic-design analysis, and only one game (Quacks) offers a genuine praised-design counterexample — the corpus is thin on the "works well" side of this label. Dark Pact carries `component-clarity` in frontmatter this pass with a synthesized Summary-level opportunity (separating permissions/resources/multiplication visually) but that specific bullet has no independent Source/Evidence citation of its own in the source doc, so it was excluded here rather than cited loosely. A game whose reviewers specifically credit iconography or a reference-card redesign for resolving prior complaints (e.g., a revised/second edition) would strengthen the "where it works" side considerably.
