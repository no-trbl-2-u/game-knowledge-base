## Summary

Evidence has grown from one game to two, and the second (Food Chain Magnate) is the sharper case: a design explicitly built around compounding punishment rather than a catch-up mechanic softening it. The Quacks of Quedlinburg suggests a catch-up mechanism can keep trailing players *feeling* competitive through the midgame even while it fails to fully prevent a leader from becoming apparent late — one reviewer praised the catch-up system for keeping players in contention, while another observed that by round 7/8 the eventual winner is usually already clear. Food Chain Magnate offers the opposite design philosophy: the official rules and independent reception both describe a runaway-leader dynamic as intentional (small early production or sales differences can leave a player "hopelessly behind"), but the review also credits the design with making the danger legible — trouble is telegraphed and opponent moves provide readable clues — so the mitigation is informational (see it coming, decide early) rather than mechanical (a rubber-band bonus). Together the two games suggest two distinct, non-exclusive answers to runaway leader: soften the snowball with a catch-up mechanism (Quacks), or keep the snowball but make its warning signs visible early enough for players to route around it (Food Chain Magnate). Still too small a sample to generalize which approach players prefer.

## Where it works

- the-quacks-of-quedlinburg (src-006): reviewer credits the catch-up mechanism
  with keeping trailing players competitive rather than checked out, per the
  reviews.okf.md "Praised design"/"Source-backed facts" claim ("The catch-up
  mechanic does a great job of keeping players competitive").
- food-chain-magnate (src-004): telegraphed threats and legible opponent moves
  let a trailing player see danger coming and route around it, even though the
  underlying compounding punishment is never softened.

## Where it fails

- the-quacks-of-quedlinburg (src-005): the better-if.okf.md doc flags that
  comeback pressure does not hold up deep into the final rounds — by round
  7/8 the likely winner is often already apparent, undercutting the earlier
  catch-up effect.
- food-chain-magnate (src-003, src-004): small early production or sales
  differences can compound into a player being left hopelessly behind, with no
  catch-up mechanism to reverse it once the snowball starts.

## Coverage gaps

This pattern still needs more games before it can be treated as a fully general synthesis, but it now spans two opposite design philosophies (softened snowball vs. legible snowball) rather than one case study. A good WISHLIST candidate would be a competitive area-control or engine-building game with an *explicit* runaway-leader complaint that has *neither* a catch-up mechanism *nor* Food Chain Magnate's telegraphing, to test whether the absence of both mitigations produces a distinctly harsher reviewer reaction.
