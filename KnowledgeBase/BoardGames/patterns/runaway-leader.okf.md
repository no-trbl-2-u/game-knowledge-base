## Summary

This pattern now has two genuinely contrasting data points, and the contrast
is the finding. The Quacks of Quedlinburg's runaway-leader shape is a *late,
visible* one: a built-in catch-up mechanic keeps trailing players feeling
competitive through the midgame, but by round 7/8 the eventual winner is
usually already apparent and the catch-up effect fades — the mechanism
works, it just runs out of runway before the game ends. Food Chain Magnate
is close to the opposite shape: it has no catch-up mechanic at all, and a
single early misstep — "even a slight nudge on the scale" — can produce an
irreversible, hard-to-read snowball; the game's own rules text warns a
mistake can make it "hard or impossible to get back on track." Yet Food
Chain Magnate is not simply worse on this axis — reviewers note the trouble
is "not out of the blue," because players can continuously read opponents'
committed staff/marketing moves, unlike a silent, opaque runaway lead. The
two games therefore diverge on two separable design questions: whether a
catch-up mechanic exists at all (Quacks: yes, but fading; Food Chain
Magnate: none), and whether the leader's rise is legible while it happens
(Quacks: legible only from round 7/8 onward, via visible dice scores; Food
Chain Magnate: legible move-by-move, but the point of no return itself is
easy to cross without noticing — exactly what reviewers ask designers to
fix, by making the cost of committing staff/marketing easier to read before
a player crosses that threshold). With two data points instead of one, this
suggests runaway-leader is not a single problem but at least two
independent axes: catch-up-mechanism presence/durability, and legibility of
the point where a lead becomes irreversible.

## Where it works

- the-quacks-of-quedlinburg (src-006): reviewer credits the catch-up mechanism
  with keeping trailing players competitive rather than checked out, per the
  reviews.okf.md "Praised design"/"Source-backed facts" claim ("The catch-up
  mechanic does a great job of keeping players competitive").
- food-chain-magnate (src-004): although the game has no catch-up mechanic,
  reviewers note the eventual trouble is "not out of the blue" — opponents'
  staff/marketing commitments are continuously readable, so a developing lead
  is legible move-by-move even without a mechanism to reverse it.

## Where it fails

- the-quacks-of-quedlinburg (src-005): the better-if.okf.md doc flags that
  comeback pressure does not hold up deep into the final rounds — by round
  7/8 the likely winner is often already apparent, undercutting the earlier
  catch-up effect.
- food-chain-magnate (src-004): a single "slight nudge on the scale" can
  upset one player's plans and force a "long-term setback," with no catch-up
  mechanic available to reverse it.
- food-chain-magnate (src-003): the rules themselves warn that a mistake can
  make it "hard or impossible to get back on track" — the runaway-leader risk
  is acknowledged in the game's own text, not just in reviews.
- food-chain-magnate (src-003, src-004): reviewers want the cost of
  committing staff/marketing made "easier to read before a player crosses a
  point of no return," since the irreversible threshold itself is currently
  easy to cross unknowingly.

## Coverage gaps

Two data points now exist, and they are usefully opposite in shape: Quacks
has a catch-up mechanism whose effect fades late, while Food Chain Magnate
has no catch-up mechanism at all but keeps opponents' moves legible
throughout. That is still a small sample for a cross-game synthesis. A third
game would help determine whether these two axes (catch-up-mechanism
presence/durability, point-of-no-return legibility) are the whole picture or
whether other runaway-leader shapes exist — for example, a game with neither
a catch-up mechanic nor legible opponent moves, or one where a catch-up
mechanic holds all the way to the final round. A competitive area-control or
engine-building game with an explicit runaway-leader complaint, distinct from
both of these shapes, remains a good WISHLIST candidate.
