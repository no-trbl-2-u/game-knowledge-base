## Summary

Across the current evidence, player interaction friction and its fixes cluster into
four shapes. First, low-interaction euro designs (Dominion, Terra Mystica) get
flagged simply for having little player-facing interaction as a named minor flaw,
without necessarily needing a wholesale redesign — reviewers ask for "one more
consequential interaction channel" rather than a rebuild. Second, euro-style games
that do bolt on confrontation as a discrete subsystem (Ark Nova's attack cards) read
as tacked-on rather than integrated, and even direct take-that conflict (Bloodborne)
is flagged as an audience-fit problem for groups uncomfortable attacking each other's
plans. Third, cooperative/simultaneous games get interaction "for free" from shared
mechanisms — Food Chain Magnate's continuous need to assess opponents, Shadows over
Camelot's accusation/suspicion layer (the reviewer's single favorite element), Heroes
of Terrinoth's aid tokens and enemy handoffs — but that same lack of turn-by-turn
structure can also run too thin (Onirim's two-player cooperation is "slight") or open
quarterbacking or cheating-adjacent private-information risk (Heroes of Terrinoth,
Quacks of Quedlinburg). Fourth, a middle path exists between bolted-on conflict and
pure parallel play: Scythe's factions pursue "direct conflict without elimination,"
maintaining territorial pressure without forcing constant fighting. The throughline:
interaction works best when it is a structural byproduct of shared stakes rather than
an add-on module or an eliminating mechanic, and simultaneous/private mechanisms need
an explicit audit or commitment ritual to stay trustworthy.

## Evidence by game

- ark-nova (src-007): Reviewer says the game's attack cards "seem like a tacked on solution for more player interaction" in an otherwise largely parallel euro; confidence: high
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): Reviewer flags open information in the co-op as a quarterbacking/alpha-player risk; confidence: medium
- mage-knight (src-011): Fan critique argues the competitive mode's interaction is denial-driven — other players taking dice/actions/spells/units or killing targets injects unwanted luck — and calls co-op "not cooperative enough," i.e. "multiplayer solitaire"; confidence: medium
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008): Reviewer praises the rulebook's free-order rule (players may play cards/use potions/activate abilities in any order) as the mechanism enabling tactical co-op collaboration, but flags that this strength needs explicit teaching; confidence: medium
- the-quacks-of-quedlinburg (src-005): Reviewer notes the simultaneous, largely private bag-drawing phase creates "many opportunities to cheat" in what is otherwise "largely solitaire" play; confidence: medium
- bloodborne-the-card-game (src-004): take-that play "will not suit groups uncomfortable with attacking friends' plans," confidence: high (source doc status: needs_followup)
- dominion (src-003): "lower player interaction" is listed among the design's minor flaws, with a better-if wanting "one more consequential interaction channel" without slowing turns, confidence: medium
- food-chain-magnate (src-004): trouble from opponents is "not out of the blue" — players must continuously assess opponents' moves rather than being blindsided, confidence: high
- onirim-second-edition (src-002): two-player cooperation is described as "slight," with a better-if wanting "one more consequential cooperative decision," confidence: medium
- scythe (src-001, src-003): factions pursue "direct conflict without elimination," maintaining territorial pressure rather than fighting constantly, confidence: medium
- shadows-over-camelot (src-007): the accusation/suspicion social layer is the reviewer's highlight of the whole game, confidence: high (source doc status: needs_followup)
- terra-mystica (src-003): "Lower amount of player interaction," confidence: high

## Where it works

- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): Aid tokens and enemy handoffs give players concrete, repeated reasons to coordinate rather than merely play in parallel.
- ark-nova (src-004): The official FAQ lets groups that dislike interactive Poison/Pilfering-style abilities swap in alternate solo-game effects, formalizing interaction as an optional mode instead of a fixed tax on every table.
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008): The rulebook's explicit any-order action rule is called out as the feature that makes cooperative play read as genuine collaboration rather than turn-taking.
- food-chain-magnate (src-004): trouble from other players is "not out of the blue," rewarding continuous opponent-assessment as an organic byproduct of the economic engine rather than a bolted-on module.
- shadows-over-camelot (src-007): the accusation/suspicion social layer is called out as the reviewer's single favorite element of the whole game — the strongest praised example of interaction as core design rather than addition.

## Where it fails

- ark-nova (src-007): Attack/interaction cards feel like a bolted-on answer to "the game needs more interaction," not a system-native one.
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): Fully open information invites one player to dictate the group's choices.
- mage-knight (src-011): Competitive-mode interaction is experienced as denial and added variance from other players' actions rather than meaningful engagement; the co-op mode is described as "multiplayer solitaire."
- the-quacks-of-quedlinburg (src-005): Private simultaneous resolution trades away auditability — the same design that avoids downtime also removes the ability to verify others aren't cheating.
- dominion (src-003): "lower player interaction" is named as one of the design's minor flaws, with reviewers wanting a more consequential interaction channel that doesn't slow turns.
- terra-mystica (src-003): reviewers flag a "lower amount of player interaction" as a shortcoming of the otherwise praised engine.
- onirim-second-edition (src-002): two-player cooperation reads as "slight," with a better-if asking for "one more consequential cooperative decision."
- bloodborne-the-card-game (src-004): take-that play aimed at attacking friends' plans "will not suit" groups uncomfortable with direct confrontation.

## Coverage gaps

Scythe and Bloodborne now contribute direct-conflict evidence, and Shadows over
Camelot contributes a hidden-traitor social-suspicion mechanic, partially closing the
earlier gap that no cited game treated conflict as a core loop. Still missing: a game
whose central mechanic is negotiation or trading (as opposed to territorial conflict
or take-that combat) to test whether that specific interaction shape produces
different reviewer language than the conflict-as-subsystem and conflict-as-core-loop
cases already covered.
