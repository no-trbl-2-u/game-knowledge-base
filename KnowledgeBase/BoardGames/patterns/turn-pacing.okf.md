## Summary

Turn-pacing friction in this corpus splits into three failure modes. The
first is individual-turn slowness: a single player's turn drags because it
demands open-ended analysis (Mage Knight), administrative overhead
(Dark Pact's Action-counting), or is procedurally required but low-agency
(Heroes of Terrinoth's Rest action). The second is aggregate pace-of-play
decay: the whole session lengthens as player count rises (Ark Nova, Spirit
Island), runs well past its advertised time (Twilight Imperium's 5-hour
first game against a 240-360 min estimate), or a flat stretch before a late
climax (Heat). The third, seen in legacy/scenario-based games, is *variance*
in pacing itself — the same haunt or mission can resolve in minutes or drag
on far past expectation (Betrayal Legacy's haunt phases, Nemesis: Lockdown's
five-hour four-player sessions against a shorter box estimate), which is a
distinct complaint from "this game is too slow." The recurring fix reviewers
credit is removing sequential waiting altogether — simultaneous or free-order
action resolution (Heat, Slay the Spire) — offering a solo/lighter mode that
strips the downtime without cutting the decision space (Mage Knight), or
designing explicitly for short sessions (Dominion, Onirim's "quick solo
filler"). Evidence is real but thin per game; most claims are single-review
observations rather than corroborated across sources.

## Evidence by game

- ark-nova (src-007): Board Game Quest says they "won't touch a four player game" because it "would take too long" — pace-of-play degrades as player count rises, confidence: high
- betrayal-legacy (src-005): haunt phases can run "thirty minutes beyond what it should" or "end in victory before a single full round passes", confidence: medium
- dark-pact (src-007, src-008, src-010): multiple reviews describe administrative pressure/sequencing and Action-counting overhead during long turns, confidence: medium
- dominion (src-003): review's Final Thoughts "praises quick turns and the short session length", confidence: high
- gloomhaven-jaws-of-the-lion (src-003): a 90-minute loss followed by a required retry "is not usually enjoyable"; clearer recovery scaffolding is needed, confidence: medium
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-006): Co-op Board Games credits simultaneous card selection with eliminating downtime, confidence: medium
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-007): Shelf Gamer found the race "relatively flat until the climactic final round," a mid-game pacing complaint, confidence: medium
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): a four-player Rest round is described as a turn players would rather not spend their activation on — a low-agency, maintenance-feeling individual turn, confidence: high
- horrified (src-002): the solo appendix only adjusts terror and removes specific cards rather than supplying a separate automated opponent, also flagged as a pacing-improvement surface, confidence: medium
- mage-knight (src-009): "Every round has the potential to take a while, since every move invites analysis" — direct analysis-paralysis turn-length complaint; the same source notes the solo variant avoids "a lot of the downtime," confidence: high
- nemesis-lockdown (src-008): no four-player game finished under five hours, longer than the box estimate, confidence: high
- onirim-second-edition (src-002): especially strong as a quick solo filler, with 10-15 minute solo sessions and an easy teach, confidence: high
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008, src-003): coopgestalt praises the rulebook's free-order action resolution (play cards/potions/abilities in any order) as a pacing strength, though the BGG rulebook file (src-003) confirms a later version (v2.30) cut the optional sequential-turn alternative, confidence: medium
- spirit-island (src-006, src-008): "at 4 player it can take a very long time to play" (src-006), and "higher player counts can slow the game down due to extensive discussion and coordination" (src-008) — pace-of-play degrades with table size, confidence: high
- twilight-imperium-fourth-edition (src-001, src-003): publisher lists 240-360 min official playtime; reviewer reports a five-hour first game; a session aid should set duration expectations before teaching begins, confidence: high

## Where it works

- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-006): simultaneous card selection is explicitly credited with eliminating downtime between turns.
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008): free-order action resolution (no fixed turn sequence) is praised as a rulebook strength for cooperative pacing.
- mage-knight (src-009): the dedicated solo variant is called out as preserving the game's decision crunch "without a lot of the downtime" that the multiplayer round-based structure creates.
- dominion (src-003): quick turns and short overall session length are named directly as a strength, not just an absence of complaint.
- onirim-second-edition (src-002): a deliberately short, easy-to-teach solo session is the design's explicit pitch and is praised as such.

## Where it fails

- mage-knight (src-009): open-ended analysis on every move makes individual rounds slow — the sharpest single-turn-length complaint in the corpus.
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): the Rest action is a mechanically necessary but low-agency turn, felt most acutely at four players.
- ark-nova (src-007): length at four players is severe enough that the reviewer refuses that player count outright.
- spirit-island (src-006, src-008): four-player sessions run long, driven by both raw turn count and extended discussion/coordination time.
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-007): pacing can go flat mid-race, with tension concentrated only at the finish.
- dark-pact (src-007, src-008, src-010): administrative/Action-counting overhead is named across multiple independent reviews as a turn-length drag.
- gloomhaven-jaws-of-the-lion (src-003) and nemesis-lockdown (src-008): both campaign/scenario games report sessions running well past expectation — a 90-minute loss with mandatory retry, and no four-player game finishing under five hours respectively.
- betrayal-legacy (src-005): haunt-phase length is unpredictable in either direction, which is its own distinct complaint from "too slow."
- twilight-imperium-fourth-edition (src-001, src-003): the published time estimate undersells first-game length by roughly two hours, setting the wrong expectation before play even starts.
- horrified (src-002): the solo mode's adjustments don't address pacing directly, leaving it as an open improvement surface rather than a solved problem.

## Coverage gaps

Most cited evidence still describes aggregate session-length pacing (more players, more turns, more administration) rather than pure single-turn duration; Mage Knight, Heroes of Terrinoth, and Dark Pact give the clearest individual-turn-length complaints. Dominion and Onirim now supply genuine "where it works" cases for a lighter, faster-playing design, partially filling the earlier gap. The newer gap is pacing *variance* (Betrayal Legacy, Nemesis: Lockdown) — a scenario/legacy game whose reviewers specifically credit a fix for unpredictable session length (rather than just uniformly long sessions) would be a good WISHLIST target.
