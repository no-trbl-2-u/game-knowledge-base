## Summary

Turn-pacing friction in this corpus takes several distinct shapes. Individual-turn slowness comes from open-ended analysis (Mage Knight) or a procedurally required but low-agency turn (Heroes of Terrinoth's Rest action). Aggregate session-length decay comes from rising player count (Ark Nova, Spirit Island), a flat mid-game before a late climax (Heat), a lack of a catch-up mechanism that lets a leader block until the game simply ends (Terra Mystica), or early player elimination that leaves an eliminated player facing "a long wait" until the game resolves (Nemesis). At the extreme, some designs are unpredictable in both directions at once — Betrayal Legacy can end "before a single full round passes" or run a haunt phase "thirty minutes beyond what it should" — and others post a hard quantified worst case, like Nemesis Lockdown's reviewer-observed "no four-player game under five hours." Against this, the corpus's clearest positive examples are designs that keep the whole session short rather than fixing any one turn: Dominion's roughly 30-minute games with quick turns are explicitly framed as "a strength to preserve," and Onirim's 10-15 minute solo sessions show the same principle at the lighter, solo end of the spectrum. Removing sequential waiting altogether (Heat, Slay the Spire's free-order resolution) or offering a lighter/solo mode (Mage Knight) remain the other recurring fixes reviewers credit. Evidence is real but thin per game; most claims are single-review observations rather than corroborated across sources.

## Evidence by game

- ark-nova (src-007): Board Game Quest says they "won't touch a four player game" because it "would take too long" — pace-of-play degrades as player count rises, confidence: high
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-006): Co-op Board Games credits simultaneous card selection with eliminating downtime, confidence: medium
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-007): Shelf Gamer found the race "relatively flat until the climactic final round," a mid-game pacing complaint, confidence: medium
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): a four-player Rest round is described as a turn players would rather not spend their activation on — a low-agency, maintenance-feeling individual turn, confidence: high
- mage-knight (src-009): "Every round has the potential to take a while, since every move invites analysis" — direct analysis-paralysis turn-length complaint; the same source notes the solo variant avoids "a lot of the downtime," confidence: high
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008, src-003): coopgestalt praises the rulebook's free-order action resolution (play cards/potions/abilities in any order) as a pacing strength, though the BGG rulebook file (src-003) confirms a later version (v2.30) cut the optional sequential-turn alternative, confidence: medium
- spirit-island (src-006, src-008): "at 4 player it can take a very long time to play" (src-006), and "higher player counts can slow the game down due to extensive discussion and coordination" (src-008) — pace-of-play degrades with table size, confidence: high
- betrayal-legacy (src-005): games "sometimes end in victory before a single full round passes," while haunt phases can conversely run "thirty minutes beyond what it should" or end abruptly — pacing swings to both extremes, confidence: medium
- dominion (src-003): "turns are quick" and games run roughly 30 minutes — reviewer frames this as "a strength to preserve", confidence: high
- nemesis (src-006, src-007): early death without the Intruder role can mean "a long wait", confidence: medium
- nemesis-lockdown (src-008): "no four-player game under five hours" in the reviewer's plays, confidence: high
- onirim-second-edition (src-002): "quick 10-15 minute solo sessions", confidence: high
- terra-mystica (src-002, src-004): pacing/catch-up critique — "you build in such a way that it blocks them, and that's about it", confidence: medium

## Where it works

- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-006): simultaneous card selection is explicitly credited with eliminating downtime between turns.
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008): free-order action resolution (no fixed turn sequence) is praised as a rulebook strength for cooperative pacing.
- mage-knight (src-009): the dedicated solo variant is called out as preserving the game's decision crunch "without a lot of the downtime" that the multiplayer round-based structure creates.
- dominion (src-003): "turns are quick" and roughly 30-minute games are explicitly framed as "a strength to preserve" — a multiplayer deckbuilder solving pacing by keeping the whole session short rather than removing waiting turn-by-turn.
- onirim-second-edition (src-002): "quick 10-15 minute solo sessions" show the same short-whole-session principle at the lighter, solo end of the spectrum.

## Where it fails

- mage-knight (src-009): open-ended analysis on every move makes individual rounds slow — the sharpest single-turn-length complaint in the corpus.
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): the Rest action is a mechanically necessary but low-agency turn, felt most acutely at four players.
- ark-nova (src-007): length at four players is severe enough that the reviewer refuses that player count outright.
- spirit-island (src-006, src-008): four-player sessions run long, driven by both raw turn count and extended discussion/coordination time.
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-007): pacing can go flat mid-race, with tension concentrated only at the finish.
- betrayal-legacy (src-005): pacing swings unpredictably to both extremes — games can end before a full round passes, or haunt phases can run thirty minutes long.
- nemesis-lockdown (src-008): the reviewer's plays produced "no four-player game under five hours" — the most extreme quantified runtime complaint in this pattern.
- nemesis (src-006, src-007): a player eliminated early without the Intruder role faces "a long wait" until the game resolves.

## Coverage gaps

Dominion and Onirim now supply the lighter/faster-playing counterexamples the previous pass asked for, giving the "where it works" side genuine variety instead of resting entirely on downtime-elimination mechanics. What's still thin: only Mage Knight and Heroes of Terrinoth give a clean individual-turn-length complaint distinct from aggregate session length; Betrayal Legacy's swing between near-instant loss and a thirty-minutes-over-length haunt phase is reported once, and it's unclear whether that volatility is specific to its haunt-card system or common to legacy/hidden-traitor games generally; and Nemesis Lockdown's "no four-player game under five hours" is the single most extreme quantified figure in this pattern but comes from one reviewer's plays rather than corroborated benchmarks. A second data point on legacy-game pacing volatility, and a corroborating source for Nemesis Lockdown's runtime, would strengthen this pattern considerably.
