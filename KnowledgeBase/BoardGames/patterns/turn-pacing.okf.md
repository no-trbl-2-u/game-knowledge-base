## Summary

Turn-pacing friction in this corpus splits into three failure modes. The first is individual-turn slowness: a single player's turn drags because it demands open-ended analysis (Mage Knight), tracks fiddly accumulated state across a long chain (Dark Pact's Action-counting combos), or is procedurally required but low-agency (Heroes of Terrinoth's Rest action). The second is aggregate pace-of-play decay: the whole session lengthens as player count rises (Ark Nova, Spirit Island, Elder Sign's printed 1-8 range), or a flat stretch precedes a late climax (Heat, Betrayal Legacy's haunt phase running "thirty minutes beyond what it should"). The third, newly evidenced, is content-pacing: narrated or scripted beats (Forgotten Waters' voiceovers) that deliver too little payoff for the time they consume. The recurring fix reviewers credit is removing sequential waiting altogether — simultaneous or free-order action resolution (Heat, Slay the Spire), a visible state tracker that shortens verification time (Dark Pact), or offering a solo/lighter mode that strips the downtime without cutting the decision space (Mage Knight). Evidence is real but thin per game; most claims are single-review observations rather than corroborated across sources.

## Evidence by game

- ark-nova (src-007): Board Game Quest says they "won't touch a four player game" because it "would take too long" — pace-of-play degrades as player count rises, confidence: high
- betrayal-legacy (src-005): the reviewer reports haunt phases running "thirty minutes beyond what it should" or ending before a full round, supporting pacing that resists both overextension and premature resolution, confidence: medium
- dark-pact (src-007, src-008, src-010): multiple reviews describe administrative pressure during long turns from tracking Actions gained and spent across chains, supporting a small visible Action tracker, confidence: medium
- dominion (src-003): Board Game Quest praises quick turns and short session length in its Final Thoughts as a strength to preserve, confidence: high
- elder-sign (src-002, src-004): both reviewers treat the printed 1-8 player range as unrealistic because "there is almost nothing for players to do between turns," worst at higher counts, confidence: high
- forgotten-waters (src-012): the reviewer says some minute-long narrated voiceovers end in only small boosts and suggests cutting 30-90 minutes from the experience, confidence: high
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-006): Co-op Board Games credits simultaneous card selection with eliminating downtime, confidence: medium
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-007): Shelf Gamer found the race "relatively flat until the climactic final round," a mid-game pacing complaint, confidence: medium
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): a four-player Rest round is described as a turn players would rather not spend their activation on — a low-agency, maintenance-feeling individual turn, confidence: high
- mage-knight (src-009): "Every round has the potential to take a while, since every move invites analysis" — direct analysis-paralysis turn-length complaint; the same source notes the solo variant avoids "a lot of the downtime," confidence: high
- scythe (src-003): the reviewer says combat is prominent in the presentation but rare during a typical session, a mismatch between pacing expectation and actual play, confidence: medium
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008, src-003): coopgestalt praises the rulebook's free-order action resolution (play cards/potions/abilities in any order) as a pacing strength, though the BGG rulebook file (src-003) confirms a later version (v2.30) cut the optional sequential-turn alternative, confidence: medium
- spirit-island (src-006, src-008): "at 4 player it can take a very long time to play" (src-006), and "higher player counts can slow the game down due to extensive discussion and coordination" (src-008) — pace-of-play degrades with table size, confidence: high

## Where it works

- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-006): simultaneous card selection is explicitly credited with eliminating downtime between turns.
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008): free-order action resolution (no fixed turn sequence) is praised as a rulebook strength for cooperative pacing.
- mage-knight (src-009): the dedicated solo variant is called out as preserving the game's decision crunch "without a lot of the downtime" that the multiplayer round-based structure creates.
- dominion (src-003): quick, short turns are singled out as the base game's clearest strength to protect when weighing any other change.

## Where it fails

- mage-knight (src-009): open-ended analysis on every move makes individual rounds slow — the sharpest single-turn-length complaint in the corpus.
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): the Rest action is a mechanically necessary but low-agency turn, felt most acutely at four players.
- ark-nova (src-007): length at four players is severe enough that the reviewer refuses that player count outright.
- spirit-island (src-006, src-008): four-player sessions run long, driven by both raw turn count and extended discussion/coordination time.
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-007): pacing can go flat mid-race, with tension concentrated only at the finish.
- elder-sign (src-002, src-004): downtime is the single most consistently reported friction point, worst beyond four or five players.
- betrayal-legacy (src-005): the haunt phase can run substantially over or resolve before a full round, showing pacing variance rather than a controlled climax.
- forgotten-waters (src-012): scripted narrated content can consume significant session time for a disproportionately small mechanical payoff.

## Coverage gaps

Most cited evidence describes aggregate session-length pacing (more players, more turns) rather than pure single-turn duration; Mage Knight, Heroes of Terrinoth, and Dark Pact give the cleanest individual-turn-length complaints. betrayal-legacy and forgotten-waters contribute from `needs_followup` source docs, which is why this pattern's status has stepped back to draft pending re-verification. A lighter, faster-playing game (a filler or family-weight title) with turn-pacing commentary would help separate "this turn itself is slow" from "this game has too many turns" as distinct design problems.
