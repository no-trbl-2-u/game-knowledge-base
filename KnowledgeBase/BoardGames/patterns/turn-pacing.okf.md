## Summary

Turn-pacing friction in this corpus splits into two distinct failure modes. The first is individual-turn slowness: a single player's turn drags because it demands open-ended analysis (Mage Knight) or because it is procedurally required but low-agency (Heroes of Terrinoth's Rest action). The second is aggregate pace-of-play decay: the whole session lengthens as player count rises, through more turns to wait through (Ark Nova, Spirit Island) or a flat stretch before a late climax (Heat). A third shape, newly evidenced here, is administrative pacing drag within an otherwise-fast turn: bookkeeping an Action counter (Dark Pact) or a variable-length special phase that can run far past its intended slot (Betrayal Legacy's haunt). The recurring fix reviewers credit is removing sequential waiting altogether — simultaneous or free-order action resolution (Heat, Slay the Spire) — offering a solo/lighter mode that strips the downtime without cutting the decision space (Mage Knight), or simply keeping turns quick by design (Dominion). Evidence is real but thin per game; most claims are single-review observations rather than corroborated across sources, and Betrayal Legacy's contribution draws on a needs_followup-status doc.

## Evidence by game

- dark-pact (src-007, src-008, src-010): multiple reviews describe sequencing and Action counting as a recurring administrative pressure during long turns, supporting a visible Action tracker so state survives long gain-to-hand chains, confidence: medium
- betrayal-legacy (src-005): the reviewer reports haunt phases running "thirty minutes beyond what it should" or ending before a full round, supporting pacing that resists both premature resolution and unnecessary extension, confidence: medium (source doc status: needs_followup)
- dominion (src-003): Board Game Quest's Final Thoughts specifically praise quick turns and short session length as a strength to preserve when weighing any other change, confidence: high
- elder-sign (src-002, src-004): downtime is the most consistently reported friction, with both reviewers treating the printed 1-8 player range as unrealistic — "any number of players beyond 4 or 5 will suffer significant downtime," confidence: high
- elder-sign (src-002): the reviewer's own mitigation for downtime is social rather than mechanical, locating the pacing gap in the design rather than the group, confidence: medium
- scythe (src-003): combat is prominent in the game's presentation but rare during an ordinary session, a session-rhythm mismatch between how the game markets itself and how it actually plays turn to turn, confidence: medium

- ark-nova (src-007): Board Game Quest says they "won't touch a four player game" because it "would take too long" — pace-of-play degrades as player count rises, confidence: high
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-006): Co-op Board Games credits simultaneous card selection with eliminating downtime, confidence: medium
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-007): Shelf Gamer found the race "relatively flat until the climactic final round," a mid-game pacing complaint, confidence: medium
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): a four-player Rest round is described as a turn players would rather not spend their activation on — a low-agency, maintenance-feeling individual turn, confidence: high
- mage-knight (src-009): "Every round has the potential to take a while, since every move invites analysis" — direct analysis-paralysis turn-length complaint; the same source notes the solo variant avoids "a lot of the downtime," confidence: high
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008, src-003): coopgestalt praises the rulebook's free-order action resolution (play cards/potions/abilities in any order) as a pacing strength, though the BGG rulebook file (src-003) confirms a later version (v2.30) cut the optional sequential-turn alternative, confidence: medium
- spirit-island (src-006, src-008): "at 4 player it can take a very long time to play" (src-006), and "higher player counts can slow the game down due to extensive discussion and coordination" (src-008) — pace-of-play degrades with table size, confidence: high

## Where it works

- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-006): simultaneous card selection is explicitly credited with eliminating downtime between turns.
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008): free-order action resolution (no fixed turn sequence) is praised as a rulebook strength for cooperative pacing.
- mage-knight (src-009): the dedicated solo variant is called out as preserving the game's decision crunch "without a lot of the downtime" that the multiplayer round-based structure creates.
- dominion (src-003): quick turns and a short session are named directly as a strength worth protecting, the corpus's clearest "this is just fast" praise rather than a fix for a specific slowdown.

## Where it fails

- mage-knight (src-009): open-ended analysis on every move makes individual rounds slow — the sharpest single-turn-length complaint in the corpus.
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): the Rest action is a mechanically necessary but low-agency turn, felt most acutely at four players.
- ark-nova (src-007): length at four players is severe enough that the reviewer refuses that player count outright.
- spirit-island (src-006, src-008): four-player sessions run long, driven by both raw turn count and extended discussion/coordination time.
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-007): pacing can go flat mid-race, with tension concentrated only at the finish.
- elder-sign (src-002, src-004): the printed 1-8 player range creates downtime severe enough that both reviewers call it unrealistic beyond four or five players.
- betrayal-legacy (src-005): the haunt's special phase is reported as pacing-unstable in both directions — sometimes far too long, sometimes cut short (source doc status: needs_followup).
- dark-pact (src-007, src-008, src-010): tracking Action state across long combo chains is administrative overhead layered on top of an otherwise fast turn.

## Coverage gaps

Most cited evidence describes aggregate session-length pacing (more players, more turns) rather than pure single-turn duration; only Mage Knight and Heroes of Terrinoth give a clean individual-turn-length complaint. Dark Pact adds a third shape — administrative bookkeeping inside a fast turn — that is distinct from both. A lighter, faster-playing game (a filler or family-weight title) with turn-pacing commentary would help separate "this turn itself is slow" from "this game has too many turns" as distinct design problems; Dominion's quick-turns praise is a first step in that direction but is one data point.
