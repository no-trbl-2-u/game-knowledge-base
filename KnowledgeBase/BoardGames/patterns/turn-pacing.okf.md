## Summary

Turn-pacing friction in this corpus splits into two distinct failure modes. The first is individual-turn slowness: a single player's turn drags because it demands open-ended analysis (Mage Knight), because it is procedurally required but low-agency (Heroes of Terrinoth's Rest action), or because internal turn state (an Action counter accumulated through long combo chains in Dark Pact) becomes hard to track without an external aid. The second is aggregate pace-of-play decay: the whole session lengthens as player count rises, through more turns to wait through (Ark Nova, Spirit Island, Elder Sign) or a flat stretch before a late climax (Heat), or through narrated/app-driven content that outruns its mechanical payoff (Forgotten Waters). The recurring fix reviewers credit is removing sequential waiting altogether — simultaneous or free-order action resolution (Heat, Slay the Spire) — offering a solo/lighter mode that strips the downtime without cutting the decision space (Mage Knight), or simply keeping turns short by design (Dominion's quick turns are praised as a strength worth protecting). Evidence is real but thin per game; most claims are single-review observations rather than corroborated across sources.

## Evidence by game

- ark-nova (src-007): Board Game Quest says they "won't touch a four player game" because it "would take too long" — pace-of-play degrades as player count rises, confidence: high
- betrayal-legacy (src-005): the reviewer reports haunt phases running "thirty minutes beyond what it should" in some sessions or ending before a full round in others — pacing variance in both directions, confidence: medium
- dark-pact (src-007, src-008, src-010): multiple reviews describe administrative pressure from sequencing and Action-counting during long gain-to-hand chains, supporting a visible Action tracker so turn state survives without verbal replay, confidence: medium
- dominion (src-003): Board Game Quest's Final Thoughts single out quick turns and short session length as a core strength to preserve, confidence: high
- elder-sign (src-002, src-004): both reviewers treat the printed 1-8 player range as unrealistic because non-active players have little to do between turns, confidence: high
- forgotten-waters (src-012): the reviewer says some narrated app passages run a minute or more for a small payoff and suggests the campaign could be cut by 30-90 minutes, confidence: high
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-006): Co-op Board Games credits simultaneous card selection with eliminating downtime, confidence: medium
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-007): Shelf Gamer found the race "relatively flat until the climactic final round," a mid-game pacing complaint, confidence: medium
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): a four-player Rest round is described as a turn players would rather not spend their activation on — a low-agency, maintenance-feeling individual turn, confidence: high
- mage-knight (src-009): "Every round has the potential to take a while, since every move invites analysis" — direct analysis-paralysis turn-length complaint; the same source notes the solo variant avoids "a lot of the downtime," confidence: high
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008, src-003): coopgestalt praises the rulebook's free-order action resolution (play cards/potions/abilities in any order) as a pacing strength, though the BGG rulebook file (src-003) confirms a later version (v2.30) cut the optional sequential-turn alternative, confidence: medium
- spirit-island (src-006, src-008): "at 4 player it can take a very long time to play" (src-006), and "higher player counts can slow the game down due to extensive discussion and coordination" (src-008) — pace-of-play degrades with table size, confidence: high

## Where it works

- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-006): simultaneous card selection is explicitly credited with eliminating downtime between turns.
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008): free-order action resolution (no fixed turn sequence) is praised as a rulebook strength for cooperative pacing.
- mage-knight (src-009): the dedicated solo variant is called out as preserving the game's decision crunch "without a lot of the downtime" that the multiplayer round-based structure creates.
- dominion (src-003): short, quick turns are explicitly named as a strength to protect against any interaction or onboarding fix that would slow them down.

## Where it fails

- mage-knight (src-009): open-ended analysis on every move makes individual rounds slow — the sharpest single-turn-length complaint in the corpus.
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): the Rest action is a mechanically necessary but low-agency turn, felt most acutely at four players.
- ark-nova (src-007): length at four players is severe enough that the reviewer refuses that player count outright.
- betrayal-legacy (src-005): pacing is inconsistent in both directions — some haunts overrun, others resolve before a full round completes.
- dark-pact (src-007, src-008, src-010): untracked internal turn state (an Action count built through long chains) forces players to verbally reconstruct a turn to verify it.
- elder-sign (src-002, src-004): non-active-player idle time is the single most consistently reported friction across the corpus's dice-driven cooperative games.
- forgotten-waters (src-012): app-narrated content is reported as running long relative to its mechanical payoff.
- spirit-island (src-006, src-008): four-player sessions run long, driven by both raw turn count and extended discussion/coordination time.
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-007): pacing can go flat mid-race, with tension concentrated only at the finish.

## Coverage gaps

Most cited evidence describes aggregate session-length pacing (more players, more turns) rather than pure single-turn duration; Mage Knight, Heroes of Terrinoth, and now Dark Pact give a clean individual-turn-length complaint. Dominion is the corpus's clearest counter-example (short turns as a protected strength) and should anchor any design guidance that pacing fixes must not sacrifice a game's fast-turn identity. A lighter, faster-playing filler-weight game beyond Dominion would help further separate "this turn itself is slow" from "this game has too many turns" as distinct design problems.
