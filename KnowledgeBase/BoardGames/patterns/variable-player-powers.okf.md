## Summary

Evidence has broadened from 4 to several more games with claims genuinely about
variable/asymmetric powers as a design feature. The core finding holds and sharpens:
reviewers credit character/hero/faction variety directly with replayability and
strategic identity (Marvel Champions, Heroes of Terrinoth, Too Many Bones, Terra
Mystica's 14 factions, Unmatched's per-fighter asymmetry, Star Wars: Rebellion's
thematically-grounded Imperial-vs-Rebel power differential, Scythe's balanced
faction/mat/starting-location variety), but the same asymmetry is what makes the
game harder to learn (each kit is its own lesson in Root) or harder to keep balanced
at scale (one dominant investment path in Dune: Imperium Uprising, per-quest balance
swings in Descent 2E, per-mission/player-count balance pressure in Star Wars:
Imperial Assault). Asymmetry-as-replayability, asymmetry-as-onboarding-tax, and
asymmetry-as-balance-risk appear to be three faces of the same design choice, not
three competing findings.

## Evidence by game

- descent-journeys-in-the-dark-second-edition (src-004): scenario/quest goals drive balance, and "balance varies widely between quests", confidence: medium
- dune-imperium-uprising (src-004): the sandworm reward-doubling mechanic can make "one investment path feel dominant," with the sandworm-investing player pulling "ahead decisively in many ... plays", confidence: medium
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): reviewer's Pros list credits hero variety (alongside upgrades, randomized quest sets, and dice) with the game's replay value, confidence: high
- marvel-champions-the-card-game (src-004): reviewer frames hero/villain combinations as the central replayability signal — "With nearly unlimited combinations of notable heroes and villainous schemes...", confidence: high
- root (src-004): learning Root "demands more than just memorising the general rules" because each faction is effectively its own ruleset to learn, confidence: high
- root (src-006): reaching proficiency requires understanding not just your own faction but every other faction at the table — asymmetry raises the mastery ceiling for everyone, confidence: high
- scythe (src-003): faction, mat, and starting-location balance is a "bright spot"; no faction felt overpowered across the reviewer's games, confidence: medium
- star-wars-imperial-assault (src-003): campaign mission balance and five-player-count scaling are identified as pressure points needing more transparency, confidence: medium
- star-wars-rebellion (src-005, src-006): reviewers praise the asymmetric expression of Imperial grand-army power vs. Rebel scrappy-underdog vulnerability as thematically strong, confidence: high
- terra-mystica (src-003, src-004): reviewers praise 14-faction variety and fully interconnected/deterministic economic systems as strategic depth drivers, confidence: high
- too-many-bones (src-004, src-005): reviewers praise the depth and distinctiveness of Gearloc-specific builds and treat the character-specific dice as tactical identity, but both reviews also flag a real learning curve and complexity that may deter casual players, confidence: medium
- unmatched-battle-of-legends-volume-one (src-003): the character roster and "rules-bendy asymmetry" is called the game's strongest feature, confidence: medium

## Where it works

- marvel-champions-the-card-game (src-004): hero/villain combinatorics are named as the game's central replayability driver, confidence: high
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): hero variety is listed alongside upgrades and randomized content as a source of replay value, confidence: high
- too-many-bones (src-004, src-005): once learned, Gearloc build distinctiveness and character-specific dice are the game's clearest praised strength, confidence: medium
- scythe (src-003): faction/mat/starting-location asymmetry held up under actual play testing, not just on paper — no overpowered combination found.
- star-wars-rebellion (src-005, src-006): the Imperial/Rebel power differential is the praised design itself, asymmetry expressing theme rather than undermining balance.
- terra-mystica (src-003, src-004): 14 factions give the deterministic planning system its variety without reviewers flagging an imbalance.
- unmatched-battle-of-legends-volume-one (src-003): per-fighter asymmetry pairs a fast teach with a real skill ceiling, echoing Root's "learn every faction" depth without the long onboarding tax.

## Where it fails

- root (src-004): onboarding cost scales with faction count — "each faction is its own lesson", not a shared ruleset with variants, confidence: high
- root (src-006): the skill ceiling created by asymmetry means players must learn opposing factions' engines, not just their own, to play well, confidence: high
- too-many-bones (src-004, src-005): the same character-specific build depth that earns praise also produces a learning curve that reviewers say may deter casual or younger players, confidence: medium
- dune-imperium-uprising (src-004): asymmetric leader/path choices can tip into one dominant option rather than genuinely different strategic identities.
- descent-journeys-in-the-dark-second-edition (src-004) and star-wars-imperial-assault (src-003): both campaign games report that asymmetric balance is tuned per-scenario, so a single "is it balanced" verdict doesn't transfer across missions or player counts.

## Coverage gaps

Several strong asymmetric-power designs (Spirit Island, Gloomhaven, Arkham Horror: The Card Game, and Mage Knight) still carry the `variable-player-powers` mechanics tag but have not yielded a claim in their own reception docs clearly, specifically about asymmetric powers rather than campaign length, setup, or rules load in general — their "Praised design" sections gesture at asymmetric spirits/investigators/classes but without a source-attributed quote to cite here, so those games remain left out rather than forced in. Kingdom Death: Monster and Too Many Bones still carry `needs_followup` on their reception docs, which keeps this doc's status at `draft`. The "where it fails" side is now better populated (Root plus three balance-focused cases), but still skews toward onboarding/balance complaints rather than a widely-corroborated, multi-source overpowered-faction controversy.
