## Summary

`semi-cooperative-game` entered the corpus this pass via a batch of nine
horror/survival dossiers, and four of them (Forgotten Waters, Nemesis: Lockdown,
Shadows over Camelot, Dead of Winter: A Crossroads Game) carry a genuine, sourced
definitional claim about what the label means for that specific design — not just the
tag. The recurring shape: players share a catastrophic loss condition or common goal,
but at least one individual layer (a secret objective, a personal ending, an optional
Traitor) creates bounded self-interest that can diverge from the group's. Reviewers
treat this as the design's central tension rather than a flaw to fix, provided the
game is explicit that direct sabotage is limited or prohibited — indirect harm and
self-preserving choices are what "semi-cooperative" cashes out to in practice, not
open betrayal. All four contributing reception docs are still `status: needs_followup`,
so this pattern's own status is `draft` and every claim below should be reweighted once
those docs clear review.

## Evidence by game

- forgotten-waters (src-001, src-003, src-012): the rulebook combines shared
  catastrophic losses with individual constellation qualification and personal
  endings; Bumbling Through Dungeons reports the resulting selfish incentives "keep
  play from feeling fully cooperative," confidence: high (source doc status:
  needs_followup)
- nemesis-lockdown (src-001): standard play is semi-cooperative because players share
  threats but retain secret individual (Personal/Corporate) objectives and
  independent victory; a separate full-cooperative/solo mode is offered alongside it,
  confidence: high (source doc status: needs_followup)
- shadows-over-camelot (src-001): cooperative and semi-cooperative tags both apply
  because the loyal Knights share a team result while a possibly-dealt Traitor serves
  the opposing side; play draws from shared White/Black card piles rather than a
  revealed team split at setup, confidence: high (source doc status: needs_followup)
- dead-of-winter-a-crossroads-game (src-001): the base game is governed by main and
  secret objectives alongside shared survivor, item, crisis, and Crossroads cards,
  with card text taking precedence over the rulebook for those secret-objective
  rulings, confidence: high (source doc status: needs_followup)

## Where it works

- shadows-over-camelot (src-006, src-007): imperfect information and the possibility
  of a hidden Traitor are named by two independent reviewers as the base game's
  strongest fun — suspicion around ordinary moves, not resolved betrayal, is the
  actual draw.
- nemesis-lockdown (src-001): offering both a semi-cooperative standard mode and a
  separate fully-cooperative/solo mode lets a table choose its trust level rather than
  forcing one structure on every group.

## Where it fails

- forgotten-waters (src-012): a reviewer reports the individual-ending incentive
  structure undercuts the feeling of full cooperation even though the rules impose
  shared catastrophic losses — the "semi" half can read as absence of "co-op" rather
  than a deliberate tension.
- nemesis-lockdown (src-008, src-009): reviews (cited in this corpus's
  `rules-ambiguity.okf.md`) report that the same information-dense design that
  supports secret objectives also makes rulebook lookup slow, an indirect cost of the
  semi-cooperative structure's extra bookkeeping (individual objective cards, hidden
  scoring).

## Coverage gaps

Five other semi-cooperative-tagged games in this batch (Betrayal Legacy, Bloodborne:
The Card Game, Dead of Winter: The Long Night, and two games with no better-if content
at all — Battlestar Galactica and The Thing: The Boardgame) did not yield a distinct,
sourced definitional claim about the semi-cooperative structure itself, so they were
left out rather than assumed from the tag. Every contributing reception doc here is
still `needs_followup`; this pattern should be re-verified once those docs clear
review, and a librarian/audit pass on the remaining five tagged games could
substantially deepen this doc's evidence base without requiring new intake.
