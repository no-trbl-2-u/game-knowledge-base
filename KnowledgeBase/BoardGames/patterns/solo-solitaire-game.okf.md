## Summary

Across games tagged `solo-solitaire-game`, only a minority carry reception evidence that actually discusses the dedicated solo mode as its own experience (as opposed to the mechanic simply being listed). Where reviewers do address solo play directly, the same tension recurs: a strong ruleset "just works" alone because it removes multiplayer downtime and negotiation overhead, but the solo variant itself is judged on whether it introduces its own new problems — swinginess from running a single hand, brain burn from multi-handing, an anticlimactic/procedural endgame, oversized physical footprint for one player, a content pool that plateaus in difficulty after a few solo plays (Onirim), or (for a video-game adaptation) simply feeling like a slower manual reenactment of software logic. A recurring variant of "no new problems" is "no dedicated solution at all": Horrified's and Elder Sign's solo modes work but lean on rules deltas (a modified terror track, lenient death penalties) rather than purpose-built solo tension. Evidence is genuinely thin for most co-op titles in this set; several carry the tag with no solo-specific reviewer commentary at all.

## Evidence by game

- ark-nova (src-008): A dedicated solo reviewer (Stidjen Plays Solo) says the large card-stack output randomness makes it hard to plan ahead in solo play, and separately flags the board as "just too big for solo play (or in general, arguably)." confidence: high
- mage-knight (src-009): There Will Be Games credits the solo variant with sidestepping the downtime that afflicts multiplayer turns: "solo version... without a lot of the downtime." confidence: high
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-007, src-008): Rolling In The Meep judges solo play "admirable but less successful" because it feels too much like a physical substitute for the original video game; coopgestalt's own scoring shows solo (8.5/10) trailing co-op (9/10). confidence: medium
- spirit-island (src-009, src-008): Stidjen Plays Solo reports true solo is swingy, multi-handing causes brain burn, and the endgame feels procedural; Punchboard separately calls the game "fantastic... for solo players and small groups." confidence: high
- horrified (src-002): the solo appendix changes the terror track and removes specific cards rather than supplying a dedicated automated opponent, confidence: medium
- elder-sign (src-002, src-004): solo play works without any dedicated automation system but leans on lenient death penalties — a solo player "could simply burn through several characters in a single game" — rather than designed solo tension, confidence: high
- onirim-second-edition (src-002): the reviewer says the base solo game "is not very challenging after several plays," with difficulty scaling relegated to expansion modules rather than available in the base box, confidence: medium

## Where it works

- mage-knight (src-009): the solo mode is praised specifically because it removes the downtime problem that dogs the multiplayer table — the same brain-burning card-efficiency puzzle without waiting on other players.
- spirit-island (src-008): reviewer calls it a fantastic fit for solo players and small groups, treating low player count (including solo) as the format where the design shines brightest.

## Where it fails

- ark-nova (src-008): output randomness from the large card deck undermines forward planning in solo play, and the board's physical footprint is called out as oversized specifically for a single player.
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-007): solo play is the weaker mode in an otherwise praised adaptation, criticized for feeling like a slower manual version of the source video game rather than offering its own tabletop-specific value.
- spirit-island (src-009): true solo (one hand) is swingy, and the multi-handed solo alternative causes brain burn; the same reviewer separately flags a procedural, anticlimactic solo endgame.
- horrified (src-002): the solo mode is a rules delta grafted onto the multiplayer system, not a purpose-built solo/automation layer.
- elder-sign (src-002, src-004): solo tension is undersupported by design and instead masked by forgiving death penalties.
- onirim-second-edition (src-002): the base game's difficulty plateaus for experienced solo players, with no in-box lever to restore challenge.

## Coverage gaps

Most of the corpus's cooperative/campaign titles carry `solo-solitaire-game` in their mechanics list but have no reviewer commentary specifically about the solo mode: aeons-end, arkham-horror-the-card-game, gi-joe-deck-building-game, heat-pedal-to-the-metal, heroes-of-terrinoth, hoplomachus-remastered, legendary-encounters-an-alien-deck-building-game, and marvel-champions-the-card-game all lack a source that discusses solo scaling, solo scoring, or solo bot/variant logic in the docs read for this pass. too-many-bones is excluded here because its reception docs are still `needs_followup`; dark-pact and nemesis also carry the tag but neither's better-if doc yielded a claim distinctly about the solo mode itself, so both were left out rather than stretched. A future pass would benefit from a dedicated solo-focused review for at least one Fantasy Flight LCG-style title (Arkham Horror or Marvel Champions), since both explicitly ship solo rules but the current sources only cover multiplayer/co-op reception.
