## Summary

Onboarding friction across this corpus takes three recurring shapes. Rules-cliff games force the first session to absorb a whole engine at once (Root's faction opacity, Mage Knight's "obtuse" rulebook, Slay the Spire's video-game-literate assumptions). Campaign and construction games front-load a punishing early loss before the deck/character engine that justifies the system opens up (Arkham Horror LCG's weak starter decks, Aeon's End's no-shuffle sequencing, Too Many Bones' walkthrough dependency, Arydia's threat system landing before players have absorbed the component set). And competitive economy-engine games (Terra Mystica, Scythe) punish new players against experienced ones specifically because the action economy or expertise gap is exposed all at once rather than staged. Against these, the corpus's clearest wins are sequencing fixes rather than simplification: a scripted teach round or prologue that defers full complexity (Earthborne Rangers' explicitly-praised prologue), or a genuinely simple core loop that needs no staging at all (Unmatched's five-minute teach, Cthulhu: Death May Die's quick setup, Dominion's accessible base loop). Evidence is broad (21 games) but still shallow per game — most claims are single-review inferences rather than dense player-comment consensus.

## Where it works

- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-005): Board Game Quest characterizes the system as "a deckbuilding game with campaign elements and a progressive narrative" — the upgrade cadence introduces construction gradually rather than all at once.
- gi-joe-deck-building-game (src-005): customer reviews praise that "the base game is easy to learn" while expansions layer in additional elements, letting complexity ramp after the first sessions.
- cthulhu-death-may-die (src-003): "it's easy to teach, it's quicker to set up than most other games of this type" — a horror game the corpus otherwise associates with heavy production keeping first-session teaching light.
- earthborne-rangers (src-005): the reviewer explicitly credits the prologue with teaching the game, giving this pattern a rare example of a scripted first-session structure working as designed.
- unmatched-battle-of-legends-volume-one (src-003): "few fighting systems as simple as this one... fewer than five minutes to teach" — a low-complexity core ruleset that still supports variable-player-power depth.

## Where it fails

- root (src-004, reviews.okf.md player friction): "each faction is its own lesson" — a player can know the shared rules and still not understand the game state because opponents' faction engines stay opaque.
- mage-knight (src-010): the review's own framing — obtuse and most complicated before it clicks — shows the rulebook itself as the barrier, independent of the underlying design's quality.
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008): rulebook assumptions about video-game familiarity leave non-video-game players stranded at specific junctures (deck-shuffling exceptions, post-Act-III progression).
- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-006, via better-if.okf.md): "The player decks provided are poor, unfocused," making early losses feel punishing before the deck-construction engine that justifies the system opens up.
- too-many-bones (src-004, src-005): the learning curve is steep enough that reviewers treat an external walkthrough as a near-required onboarding aid.
- terra-mystica (src-003, src-004): "steep learning curve... a full game may be needed before players feel comfortable," with better-if asking to expose the action economy earlier "with less front-loaded explanation."
- scythe (src-003): "New players will suffer against seasoned vets," with a better-if call for early-game expertise gaps to be "made less punishing."
- arydia-the-paths-we-dare-tread (src-004, src-005): wants "a staged first scenario that introduces the threat system before the full component burden" before the full campaign apparatus lands on new players.
- food-chain-magnate (src-003, src-004): better-if asks to "strengthen onboarding around early warning signals and recovery decisions" so early mistakes don't compound unrecoverably.

## Coverage gaps

Nearly every citation here comes from a single review's framing rather than dense multi-source consensus, and the sample skews toward heavy campaign/deckbuilding games — ark-nova and kingdom-death-monster carry the onboarding label in their frontmatter but their fetched text didn't yield an onboarding-specific claim distinct from general weight/scale, so they were excluded. The "Where it works" side now has genuine variety (a narrative prologue, a fast-teaching fighting-card system, a quick-setup horror game) rather than resting on two deckbuilders, but arydia-the-paths-we-dare-tread's evidence remains low-confidence (needs_followup underlying doc) and should be revisited once that record is verified.
