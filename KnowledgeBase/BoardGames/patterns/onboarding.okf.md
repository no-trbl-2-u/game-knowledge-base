## Summary

Onboarding friction shows up in three shapes across this corpus: rules-cliff games where the first session must teach a whole engine at once (Root's faction opacity, Mage Knight's "obtuse" rulebook, Slay the Spire's video-game-literate assumptions), campaign games where the friction is staged deck/character construction meeting a punishing early loss (Arkham Horror LCG's weak starter decks, Aeon's End's no-shuffle sequencing, Too Many Bones' walkthrough dependency), and — newly evidenced this pass — games whose rulebook length is itself the barrier even when live teaching is fast (Shadows over Camelot: a 10-15 minute oral teach undercut by "two beautiful books" longer than the game needs) or whose core loop is legible but a second subsystem intrudes before players are ready (Earthborne Rangers' praised prologue vs. its overlapping tabletop-RPG/dynamic-storytelling layers; Nemesis's "intimidating amount of material" that still reviews as eventually intuitive). The recurring fix reviewers point to is not simplification but sequencing: an official first-game path, a scripted teach round, a mode-by-mode reference, or a gradual-unlock structure that defers full complexity past session one. Evidence is broad (23 games) but shallow per game — most claims are single-review inferences rather than dense player-comment consensus.

## Evidence by game

- aeons-end (src-004): review frames deck-order mistakes as costly ("it wasn't a bad draw, it was a bad choice"), supporting a better-if call for onboarding that teaches no-shuffle sequencing through controlled first scenarios, confidence: high
- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-003): the official Learn to Play PDF itself directs new players to the introductory scenario and pre-constructed decks before customization, confidence: high
- arydia-the-paths-we-dare-tread (src-004, src-005): reviews praise depth but describe a large tactile system and unusual threat-driven combat, supporting a staged first scenario that introduces the threat system before the full component burden arrives, confidence: low
- cthulhu-death-may-die (src-002): the four-part turn sequence spans pp. 12-17 of the official rulebook and the printed rules summary compresses it onto p. 20, supporting a compact phase aid that keeps the turn and Mythos/summoning check order visible, confidence: medium
- dead-of-winter-the-long-night (src-004): reviewer reports "rules themselves are pretty long" and that the experience "needs a team ... familiarity with the rules," confidence: medium
- dominion (src-002, src-003): the rulebook presents many Kingdom-card choices while the review says new players can choose how much strategy to apply, supporting more explicit first-game guidance on the action/economy balance, confidence: medium
- dune-imperium (src-009): Geeky Guide labels the teaching curve "Procedural" and social dynamics "High Friction," supporting a call for a scripted teach round, confidence: medium
- earthborne-rangers (src-004, src-005): Space-Biff praises the contextual card ecosystem while Gideon's Gaming reports the tabletop-RPG, prewritten-story, and dynamic-storytelling subsystems can step on each other's toes even as it separately praises the prologue's teaching, confidence: medium
- gi-joe-deck-building-game (src-005): customer reviews describe the base game as easy to learn with expansions adding depth later, confidence: medium
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-006, src-007): reviewers note player-board iconography "requires learning" and multi-phase turns "can be tricky for non-gamers," confidence: medium
- horrified (src-002, src-003): the official rules split common phases across pp. 5-9 with monster-specific setup/reference following, and the reviewer notes later pages carry component pictures and monster-specific detail, supporting a single quick-reference that separates the common turn skeleton from monster exceptions, confidence: medium
- hoplomachus-remastered (src-004): the rulebook's structure (shared core plus multiple mode-specific setup/turn-order/objective chapters) implies a mode-onboarding burden the design could stage more explicitly, confidence: medium
- legendary-encounters-an-alien-deck-building-game (src-003): scenario-specific locations, objectives, and hazard/event triggers layered onto the core deckbuilder suggest onboarding would benefit from separating core decisions from movie-specific exceptions, confidence: medium
- mage-knight (src-010): review calls the game "obtuse" and "most complicated" though rewarding once the rulebook is understood, confidence: high
- nemesis (src-005, src-008): reviewers describe a twenty-step setup and an intimidating amount of material despite the game reviewing as eventually intuitive, supporting a short mode-specific teach that foregrounds action-card costs, noise escalation, and endgame gates before the full rulebook, confidence: medium
- oathsworn-into-the-deepwood (src-004): review says the game "can seem pretty complex at first," supporting staged rules/component introduction, confidence: medium
- root (src-004): "Learning how to play Root demands more than just memorising the general rules, it requires time," supporting a teach-the-spine-first approach, confidence: high
- shadows-over-camelot (src-006): the reviewer could teach the game orally in roughly ten to fifteen minutes but considered the two accompanying rulebooks much longer and more detailed than necessary, and specifically warned readers not to miss the Quest-book rules, confidence: high
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008): reviewer found moving forward after Act III "very unclear," relying on video-game familiarity to fill the gap, confidence: high
- spirit-island (src-006): review calls the game "a little fiddly, and lacking a little guidance here and there," confidence: high
- star-wars-imperial-assault (src-004): review presents the game as a multi-role tactical campaign with substantial scenario and role structure, supporting teaching the Imperial and Rebel roles through a short guided mission, confidence: medium
- tainted-grail-the-fall-of-avalon (src-003): community review metadata labels complexity high, supporting a staged onboarding sequence for entering the campaign, confidence: medium
- too-many-bones (src-004, src-005): both reviews identify a substantial learning curve and point to a third-party walkthrough as the practical bridge into play, confidence: medium

## Where it works

- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-005): Board Game Quest characterizes the system as "a deckbuilding game with campaign elements and a progressive narrative" — the upgrade cadence introduces construction gradually rather than all at once.
- gi-joe-deck-building-game (src-005): customer reviews praise that "the base game is easy to learn" while expansions layer in additional elements, letting complexity ramp after the first sessions.
- earthborne-rangers (src-005): the prologue's teaching is praised on its own terms, even though the reviewer separately flags friction once the larger subsystems layer on top of it.
- shadows-over-camelot (src-006): the game's live, oral teach runs ten to fifteen minutes — the design's core loop is not the barrier; its two-book rules presentation is.

## Where it fails

- root (src-004, reviews.okf.md player friction): "each faction is its own lesson" — a player can know the shared rules and still not understand the game state because opponents' faction engines stay opaque.
- mage-knight (src-010): the review's own framing — obtuse and most complicated before it clicks — shows the rulebook itself as the barrier, independent of the underlying design's quality.
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008): rulebook assumptions about video-game familiarity leave non-video-game players stranded at specific junctures (deck-shuffling exceptions, post-Act-III progression).
- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-006, via better-if.okf.md): "The player decks provided are poor, unfocused," making early losses feel punishing before the deck-construction engine that justifies the system opens up.
- too-many-bones (src-004, src-005): the learning curve is steep enough that reviewers treat an external walkthrough as a near-required onboarding aid.
- shadows-over-camelot (src-006): the two accompanying rulebooks are reported as much longer and more detailed than the live teach requires, with a specific warning that a rules subsection (the Quest book) is easy to miss.
- nemesis (src-005, src-008): a twenty-step setup and a comprehensive, intimidating ruleset front-load complexity before the "eventually intuitive" payoff reviewers report.

## Coverage gaps

Nearly every citation here comes from a single review's (or, for cthulhu-death-may-die and horrified, the official rulebook's) framing rather than dense multi-source consensus, and the sample skews toward heavy campaign/deckbuilding games — ark-nova and kingdom-death-monster carry the onboarding label in their frontmatter but their fetched text didn't yield an onboarding-specific claim distinct from general weight/scale, so they were excluded; dead-of-winter-a-crossroads-game, descent-journeys-in-the-dark-second-edition, elder-sign, forgotten-waters, nemesis-lockdown, and onirim-second-edition carry the same exclusion this pass despite touching adjacent labels. The corpus would benefit from a lighter, widely-praised "teaches itself" gateway game (something reviewers credit for solving onboarding well, not just avoiding the problem by being simple) to give the "Where it works" side more than four data points.
