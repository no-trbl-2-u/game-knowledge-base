## Summary

Onboarding friction shows up in two distinct shapes across this corpus: rules-cliff games where the first session must teach a whole engine at once (Root's faction opacity, Mage Knight's "obtuse" rulebook, Slay the Spire's video-game-literate assumptions), and campaign games where the friction is staged deck/character construction meeting a punishing early loss (Arkham Horror LCG's weak starter decks, Aeon's End's no-shuffle sequencing, Too Many Bones' walkthrough dependency). A newer cluster of evidence — one limited-communication co-op (The Crew), several campaign-RPG dossiers (Shadows over Camelot, Arydia, Tainted Grail, Star Wars: Imperial Assault, Cthulhu: Death May Die), and a market deck-builder (Dominion) — reinforces the same fix reviewers point to: not simplification but sequencing. Shadows over Camelot's Tom Vasel could teach the whole game orally in ten to fifteen minutes despite two dense rulebooks, the sharpest evidence yet that legible rules organization, not raw rules volume, is the actual onboarding lever. The recurring fix reviewers point to is not simplification but sequencing: an official first-game path, a scripted teach round, or a gradual-unlock structure that defers full complexity past session one. Evidence is broad (21 games) but shallow per game — most claims are single-review inferences rather than dense player-comment consensus, and several of the newer entries (Arydia, Tainted Grail) draw on reception docs still marked `needs_followup`.

## Where it works

- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-005): Board Game Quest characterizes the system as "a deckbuilding game with campaign elements and a progressive narrative" — the upgrade cadence introduces construction gradually rather than all at once.
- gi-joe-deck-building-game (src-005): customer reviews praise that "the base game is easy to learn" while expansions layer in additional elements, letting complexity ramp after the first sessions.
- shadows-over-camelot (src-006): a ten-to-fifteen-minute oral teach for the whole game, independent of two long companion rulebooks, is the strongest "onboarding solved" data point in the corpus — proof that rules organization can decouple from rules volume.
- earthborne-rangers (src-005): the prologue is credited with successful teaching in isolation, even though the same review flags friction once its several subsystems run concurrently.

## Where it fails

- root (src-004, reviews.okf.md player friction): "each faction is its own lesson" — a player can know the shared rules and still not understand the game state because opponents' faction engines stay opaque.
- mage-knight (src-010): the review's own framing — obtuse and most complicated before it clicks — shows the rulebook itself as the barrier, independent of the underlying design's quality.
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008): rulebook assumptions about video-game familiarity leave non-video-game players stranded at specific junctures (deck-shuffling exceptions, post-Act-III progression).
- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-006, via better-if.okf.md): "The player decks provided are poor, unfocused," making early losses feel punishing before the deck-construction engine that justifies the system opens up.
- too-many-bones (src-004, src-005): the learning curve is steep enough that reviewers treat an external walkthrough as a near-required onboarding aid.
- dead-of-winter-the-long-night (src-004): "rules themselves are pretty long" and the experience "needs a team ... familiarity with the rules" before it lands (source doc status: needs_followup).
- cthulhu-death-may-die (src-002): a four-part turn plus a distinct Mythos/summoning check sequence is spread across six rulebook pages with only a partial one-page summary.

## Coverage gaps

Nearly every citation here comes from a single review's framing rather than dense multi-source consensus, and the sample skews toward heavy campaign/deckbuilding games — ark-nova and kingdom-death-monster carry the onboarding label in their frontmatter but their fetched text didn't yield an onboarding-specific claim distinct from general weight/scale, so they were excluded. Arydia and Tainted Grail contribute only needs_followup-status, low/medium-confidence inferences and should be reweighted once their reception docs clear that backlog. The corpus now has one strong "onboarding solved" anchor (Shadows over Camelot) rather than the two-data-point gap noted previously; the next-most-useful addition would be a lighter, non-campaign gateway game reviewed specifically on first-play teachability, to test whether the "organization over volume" lesson holds outside cooperative/campaign designs.
