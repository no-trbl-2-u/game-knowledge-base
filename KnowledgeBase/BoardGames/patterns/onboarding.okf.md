## Summary

Onboarding friction shows up in three distinct shapes across this corpus: rules-cliff games where the first session must teach a whole engine at once (Root's faction opacity, Mage Knight's "obtuse" rulebook, Terra Mystica's interlocking systems, Slay the Spire's video-game-literate assumptions), campaign games where the friction is staged deck/character construction meeting a punishing early loss (Arkham Horror LCG's weak starter decks, Aeon's End's no-shuffle sequencing, Too Many Bones' walkthrough dependency), and veteran-vs-newcomer asymmetry where the rules are learnable but experienced players still have a structural edge (Scythe's "new players will suffer against seasoned vets"). The recurring fix reviewers credit is not simplification but sequencing: an official first-game path, a scripted teach round, or a gradual-unlock structure that defers full complexity past session one — Unmatched's sub-five-minute teach and The Crew's training-mission campaign are this pass's clearest "where it works" anchors, addressing last pass's gap of having no widely-praised "teaches itself" example. Evidence is broad (23 games) but shallow per game — most claims are single-review inferences rather than dense player-comment consensus.

## Where it works

- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-005): Board Game Quest characterizes the system as "a deckbuilding game with campaign elements and a progressive narrative" — the upgrade cadence introduces construction gradually rather than all at once.
- gi-joe-deck-building-game (src-005): customer reviews praise that "the base game is easy to learn" while expansions layer in additional elements, letting complexity ramp after the first sessions.
- unmatched-battle-of-legends-volume-one (src-003): a sub-five-minute teach time is the corpus's clearest "taught fast" example, achieved by keeping the shared ruleset tiny and pushing asymmetry into card text rather than exceptions.
- the-crew (src-004): the 50-mission campaign is structured so early missions double as the tutorial, staging complexity mission-by-mission instead of teaching it all up front.
- dominion (src-003): reviewers credit quick ~30-minute sessions with making the choice space easy to pick up through repetition rather than upfront explanation.

## Where it fails

- root (src-004, reviews.okf.md player friction): "each faction is its own lesson" — a player can know the shared rules and still not understand the game state because opponents' faction engines stay opaque.
- mage-knight (src-010): the review's own framing — obtuse and most complicated before it clicks — shows the rulebook itself as the barrier, independent of the underlying design's quality.
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008): rulebook assumptions about video-game familiarity leave non-video-game players stranded at specific junctures (deck-shuffling exceptions, post-Act-III progression).
- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-006, via better-if.okf.md): "The player decks provided are poor, unfocused," making early losses feel punishing before the deck-construction engine that justifies the system opens up.
- too-many-bones (src-004, src-005): the learning curve is steep enough that reviewers treat an external walkthrough as a near-required onboarding aid.
- scythe (src-003): new players are explicitly called out as disadvantaged against "seasoned vets," a veteran-knowledge asymmetry distinct from raw rules complexity.
- terra-mystica (src-003, src-004): two independent reviews agree the interlocking systems need a full game or two before they click.
- shadows-over-camelot (src-006): short oral teaching is contrasted against two full-length rulebooks as the only fallback reference.

## Coverage gaps

Nearly every citation here comes from a single review's framing rather than dense multi-source consensus, and the sample skews toward heavy campaign/deckbuilding games — ark-nova and kingdom-death-monster carry the onboarding label in their frontmatter but their fetched text didn't yield an onboarding-specific claim distinct from general weight/scale, so they were excluded; descent-journeys-in-the-dark-second-edition and nemesis-lockdown carry the label too but their current reception docs likewise yield no dedicated onboarding claim. The "where it works" side is no longer limited to two thin data points, but every entry there is still a single-review citation — the corpus would benefit from a second independent source corroborating any one of them.
