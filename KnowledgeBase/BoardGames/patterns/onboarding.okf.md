## Summary

Onboarding friction shows up in two distinct shapes across this corpus: rules-cliff games where the first session must teach a whole engine at once (Root's faction opacity, Mage Knight's "obtuse" rulebook, Slay the Spire's video-game-literate assumptions), and campaign games where the friction is staged deck/character construction meeting a punishing early loss (Arkham Horror LCG's weak starter decks, Aeon's End's no-shuffle sequencing, Too Many Bones' walkthrough dependency). The recurring fix reviewers point to is not simplification but sequencing: an official first-game path, a scripted teach round, or a gradual-unlock structure that defers full complexity past session one. Evidence is broad (17 games) but shallow per game — most claims are single-review inferences rather than dense player-comment consensus.

## Evidence by game

- aeons-end (src-004): review frames deck-order mistakes as costly ("it wasn't a bad draw, it was a bad choice"), supporting a better-if call for onboarding that teaches no-shuffle sequencing through controlled first scenarios, confidence: high

- arydia-the-paths-we-dare-tread (src-004, src-005): reviews praise campaign depth but describe a large tactile system and unusual threat-driven combat, supporting a staged first scenario that introduces the threat/AI-card system before the full component burden arrives (source doc status: needs_followup), confidence: low
- cthulhu-death-may-die (src-002): the four-part turn and Mythos/summoning check order spans rulebook pp. 12-17, supporting a call for a compact phase aid for first-time players, confidence: medium
- dune-imperium (src-009): Geeky Guide labels the teaching curve "Procedural" and social dynamics "High Friction," supporting a call for a scripted teach round, confidence: medium
- gi-joe-deck-building-game (src-005): customer reviews describe the base game as easy to learn with expansions adding depth later, confidence: medium
- heat-pedal-to-the-metal (src-006, src-007): reviewers note player-board iconography "requires learning" and multi-phase turns "can be tricky for non-gamers," confidence: medium

- legendary-encounters-an-alien-deck-building-game (src-003): scenario-specific locations, objectives, and hazard/event triggers layered onto the core deckbuilder suggest onboarding would benefit from separating core decisions from movie-specific exceptions, confidence: medium

## Where it works

- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-005): Board Game Quest characterizes the system as "a deckbuilding game with campaign elements and a progressive narrative" — the upgrade cadence introduces construction gradually rather than all at once.
- gi-joe-deck-building-game (src-005): customer reviews praise that "the base game is easy to learn" while expansions layer in additional elements, letting complexity ramp after the first sessions.

## Where it fails

- root (src-004, reviews.okf.md player friction): "each faction is its own lesson" — a player can know the shared rules and still not understand the game state because opponents' faction engines stay opaque.
- mage-knight (src-010): the review's own framing — obtuse and most complicated before it clicks — shows the rulebook itself as the barrier, independent of the underlying design's quality.
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008): rulebook assumptions about video-game familiarity leave non-video-game players stranded at specific junctures (deck-shuffling exceptions, post-Act-III progression).
- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-006, via better-if.okf.md): "The player decks provided are poor, unfocused," making early losses feel punishing before the deck-construction engine that justifies the system opens up.
- too-many-bones (src-004, src-005): the learning curve is steep enough that reviewers treat an external walkthrough as a near-required onboarding aid.

## Coverage gaps

Nearly every citation here comes from a single review's framing rather than dense multi-source consensus, and the sample skews toward heavy campaign/deckbuilding games — ark-nova and kingdom-death-monster carry the onboarding label in their frontmatter but their fetched text didn't yield an onboarding-specific claim distinct from general weight/scale, so they were excluded. descent-journeys-in-the-dark-second-edition also carries the label but its better-if doc's body claims are about quest balance and setup-driven dungeon mystery, not onboarding specifically, so it was excluded rather than forced in. The corpus would benefit from a lighter, widely-praised "teaches itself" gateway game (something reviewers credit for solving onboarding well, not just avoiding the problem by being simple) to give the "Where it works" side more than two data points.
