## Summary

Onboarding friction still splits into the same two shapes now that the sample has grown to 28 games, and a third fix pattern has hardened into the clearest cross-game signal: sequencing beats simplification. Rules-cliff games front-load a whole engine at once (Root's faction opacity, Mage Knight's "obtuse" rulebook, Terra Mystica's interlocking systems, Scythe's veteran-vs-new-player expertise gap); campaign/mission games stage deck or character construction into a punishing early loss (Arkham Horror LCG's weak starter decks, Too Many Bones' walkthrough dependency, Tainted Grail's high-complexity opening). The newest evidence adds a third recurring shape — heavy component/system reveal: dense rulebooks and large physical/monster-specific exception sets (Nemesis's twenty-step setup, Horrified's monster-specific exceptions, Cthulhu: Death May Die's four-part turn, Shadows over Camelot's two "beautiful but long" rulebooks) that reviewers say could teach in minutes if the first session isolated the common skeleton from the exceptions. Even confidently fast-teaching designs (Unmatched's under-five-minute rules) still draw a request to make asymmetric identity explicit before the first real decision. The recurring fix reviewers credit is not simplification but sequencing: an official first-game path, a scripted teach round, a compact phase-order aid, or a gradual-unlock structure that defers full complexity past session one. Evidence remains broad but shallow per game — most claims are single-review inferences rather than dense player-comment consensus.

## Where it works

- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-005): Board Game Quest characterizes the system as "a deckbuilding game with campaign elements and a progressive narrative" — the upgrade cadence introduces construction gradually rather than all at once.
- gi-joe-deck-building-game (src-005): customer reviews praise that "the base game is easy to learn" while expansions layer in additional elements, letting complexity ramp after the first sessions.
- unmatched-battle-of-legends-volume-one (src-003): a fewer-than-five-minute teach is treated as a genuine strength, not just a simplicity trade-off, because the asymmetric roster still gives each fighter a distinct identity.
- shadows-over-camelot (src-006): the base rules can be taught orally in ten to fifteen minutes despite the two long printed rulebooks, showing the core loop itself is not the barrier.

## Where it fails

- root (src-004, reviews.okf.md player friction): "each faction is its own lesson" — a player can know the shared rules and still not understand the game state because opponents' faction engines stay opaque.
- mage-knight (src-010): the review's own framing — obtuse and most complicated before it clicks — shows the rulebook itself as the barrier, independent of the underlying design's quality.
- slay-the-spire-the-board-game (src-008): rulebook assumptions about video-game familiarity leave non-video-game players stranded at specific junctures (deck-shuffling exceptions, post-Act-III progression).
- arkham-horror-the-card-game (src-006, via better-if.okf.md): "The player decks provided are poor, unfocused," making early losses feel punishing before the deck-construction engine that justifies the system opens up.
- too-many-bones (src-004, src-005): the learning curve is steep enough that reviewers treat an external walkthrough as a near-required onboarding aid.
- nemesis (src-005, src-008): a twenty-step setup and comprehensive rules read as intimidating even to reviewers who found the game intuitive once running.
- dead-of-winter-the-long-night (src-004): long rules combined with a need for group-wide familiarity is named as a direct barrier to a smooth first session.
- terra-mystica (src-003, src-004): interlocking action-economy and faction systems take a full game or two before they click for new players.

## Coverage gaps

Nearly every citation here comes from a single review's framing rather than dense multi-source consensus, and the sample skews toward heavy campaign/deckbuilding and dice-driven co-op games — ark-nova and kingdom-death-monster carry the onboarding label in their frontmatter but their fetched text didn't yield an onboarding-specific claim distinct from general weight/scale, so they were excluded. Several new-game contributions (arydia, dead-of-winter-the-long-night, tainted-grail, shadows-over-camelot) come from `needs_followup` source docs and should be treated as provisional pending re-verification. The corpus would benefit from a lighter, widely-praised "teaches itself" gateway game (something reviewers credit for solving onboarding well, not just avoiding the problem by being simple) to give the "Where it works" side more data points beyond the two now cited.
