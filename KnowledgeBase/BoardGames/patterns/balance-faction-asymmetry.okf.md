## Summary

Evidence for this pattern was thin through the last pass (only Root supplied strong, well-cited evidence); three new games move it past "one aside" for the first time. Descent (2nd Edition) and Star Wars: Imperial Assault — both grid-movement dungeon/campaign games from the same design lineage — independently draw scenario/mission-balance complaints (quest-to-quest balance variance, campaign scaling that assumes a stable player count), and Dark Pact adds a deckbuilding-market angle (uneven difficulty across its thirteen alternate "Pact" victory conditions). None of these rise to Root's "widely-corroborated controversy" bar — each still rests on one or two reviews — but the shape is now corroborated across genres (area-control, dungeon-crawl campaign, deckbuilder) rather than resting on Root alone. The clearest signal remains Root, where reviewers treat faction asymmetry as the game's best-in-class strength and its main onboarding/low-player-count cost in the same breath — asymmetry that changes the whole board, not just one player's options, is what reviewers reward, but it also means the design is only "on" when enough differentiated factions are at the table. Ark Nova carries this label in its own corpus tags but its sourced complaints are about bolted-on interaction cards, not power/faction imbalance, so it was excluded rather than forced in.

## Evidence by game

- dark-pact (src-007, src-009): reviews raise uneven difficulty across the game's thirteen alternate Pact victory conditions as a balance risk, questioning whether the paths are actually equivalent, confidence: medium
- descent-journeys-in-the-dark-second-edition (src-004): a reviewer reports that quest goals drive balance and that balance varies widely between individual quests, confidence: medium
- dune-imperium (src-010): A reviewer flagged that leader Glossu Rabban may be overpowered in the conflict phase, able to recruit soldiers easily and become "close to unstoppable" — a single, low-confidence outlier concern rather than a broad consensus, confidence: low
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): A reviewer found some introductory quests nearly damage-free and effectively automatic wins, i.e. the challenge undershoots what the hero roster's danger is meant to be tuned for, confidence: medium
- root (src-006): A reviewer called Root's asymmetry "some of the best... I've ever seen" with a "high skill ceiling," while also noting that "without expansions, the experience at lower player counts is much more muted," confidence: high
- star-wars-imperial-assault (src-003): campaign mission balance and the reviewer's stated five-person ideal are both named as pressure points, confidence: medium

## Where it works

- root (src-006): Reviewers praise faction asymmetry as the defining strength — "some of the best asymmetry I've ever seen" — because opposing factions are different machines competing over the same board, not palette-swapped variants, confidence: high

No other game in this set produced praised-design evidence tied specifically to power/faction balance (as opposed to interaction, theme, or general replayability).

## Where it fails

- dark-pact (src-007, src-009): thirteen distinct victory-condition Pacts are questioned as equivalent difficulty rather than assumed balanced by design, confidence: medium
- descent-journeys-in-the-dark-second-edition (src-004): quest-to-quest balance variance means the campaign's difficulty is uneven by scenario rather than tuned to a consistent curve, confidence: medium
- dune-imperium (src-010): possible single-leader power outlier (Glossu Rabban) in the conflict phase, confidence: low
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): easy introductory quests can be near-automatic wins, undershooting the tension the hero powers are balanced against, confidence: medium
- root (src-006): faction asymmetry is diluted at low player counts without expansions, so the "different machines" experience the design is praised for depends on table size and ownership of extra content, confidence: high
- star-wars-imperial-assault (src-003): campaign balance is reported as a pressure point tied to assuming a specific (five-person) table size, confidence: medium

## Coverage gaps

Root remains the only game with strong, well-cited evidence on both sides of this pattern; the three new games (Dark Pact, Descent 2E, Imperial Assault) each rest on one or two reviews, same as Dune: Imperium and Heroes of Terrinoth before them. The corpus would still benefit from a game with a well-documented, widely-corroborated overpowered-faction or overpowered-Pact/mission controversy (multiple independent sources converging on the same specific imbalance, not several different one-review asides) to test whether Root's asymmetry-as-strength pattern holds when balance actually breaks down rather than merely varies scenario-to-scenario.
