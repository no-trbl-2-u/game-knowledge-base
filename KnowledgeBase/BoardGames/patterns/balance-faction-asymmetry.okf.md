## Summary

Evidence for this pattern was thin at last pass: only 3 of the 4 candidate games yielded claims actually about power/faction balance, and one of those (Dune: Imperium) rests on a single low-confidence reviewer aside. The clearest signal comes from Root, where reviewers treat faction asymmetry as the game's best-in-class strength and its main onboarding/low-player-count cost in the same breath — asymmetry that changes the whole board, not just one player's options, is what reviewers reward, but it also means the design is only "on" when enough differentiated factions are at the table. Two newer campaign-scenario games (Descent, Star Wars: Imperial Assault) add a distinct failure mode: balance that varies by which scenario or mission a group happens to draw, rather than by faction identity — the complaint is inconsistency across content, not a single overpowered option. Dark Pact contributes a third shape: thirteen alternate victory conditions ("Pacts") whose relative difficulty reviewers say has never been validated against each other. Ark Nova carries this label in its own corpus tags but its sourced complaints are about bolted-on interaction cards, not power/faction imbalance, so it was excluded rather than forced in.

## Evidence by game

- dune-imperium (src-010): A reviewer flagged that leader Glossu Rabban may be overpowered in the conflict phase, able to recruit soldiers easily and become "close to unstoppable" — a single, low-confidence outlier concern rather than a broad consensus, confidence: low
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): A reviewer found some introductory quests nearly damage-free and effectively automatic wins, i.e. the challenge undershoots what the hero roster's danger is meant to be tuned for, confidence: medium
- root (src-006): A reviewer called Root's asymmetry "some of the best... I've ever seen" with a "high skill ceiling," while also noting that "without expansions, the experience at lower player counts is much more muted," confidence: high
- descent-journeys-in-the-dark-second-edition (src-004): a search-result review reports that quest goals drive balance and that balance "varies widely between quests," a content-level rather than faction-level imbalance, confidence: medium
- star-wars-imperial-assault (src-003): review discussion identifies campaign balance and a five-person ideal as pressure points, supporting more transparent mission balance and player-count scaling, confidence: medium
- dark-pact (src-007, src-009): reviews question equivalence across the game's thirteen alternate-victory Pacts, supporting a difficulty indicator or recommended pairing/draft method grounded in playtesting rather than an assumption that all thresholds are equivalent, confidence: medium

## Where it works

- root (src-006): Reviewers praise faction asymmetry as the defining strength — "some of the best asymmetry I've ever seen" — because opposing factions are different machines competing over the same board, not palette-swapped variants, confidence: high

No other game in this set produced praised-design evidence tied specifically to power/faction balance (as opposed to interaction, theme, or general replayability).

## Where it fails

- dune-imperium (src-010): possible single-leader power outlier (Glossu Rabban) in the conflict phase, confidence: low
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): easy introductory quests can be near-automatic wins, undershooting the tension the hero powers are balanced against, confidence: medium
- root (src-006): faction asymmetry is diluted at low player counts without expansions, so the "different machines" experience the design is praised for depends on table size and ownership of extra content, confidence: high
- descent-journeys-in-the-dark-second-edition (src-004): balance is scenario-dependent rather than consistent across the campaign, so the same roster of powers can feel tuned differently quest to quest.
- star-wars-imperial-assault (src-003): campaign balance and an implicit "best at five players" ceiling are named as pressure points independent of any single power or faction.
- dark-pact (src-007, src-009): thirteen distinct alternate-victory Pacts are questioned as balance-equivalent without playtesting evidence to support that assumption.

## Coverage gaps

Root remains the only game with strong, well-cited evidence on both sides of this pattern; Dune: Imperium's contribution is a single low-confidence aside and Heroes of Terrinoth's is an adjacent difficulty-tuning claim rather than a direct power-balance complaint. Descent and Star Wars: Imperial Assault broaden the pattern to scenario/mission-level balance rather than faction-level balance, and Dark Pact broadens it further to alternate-victory-condition balance — none of the three yet has the multi-source depth Root has. The corpus would still benefit from a well-documented, widely-corroborated overpowered-faction controversy (multiple independent sources, not one aside) to test whether Root's asymmetry-as-strength pattern holds when balance actually breaks down rather than merely thins out or varies by content.
