## Summary

Evidence for this pattern used to be thin (3 of 4 candidate games); it is now broader. The clearest signal still comes from Root, where reviewers treat faction asymmetry as the game's best-in-class strength and its main onboarding/low-player-count cost in the same breath — asymmetry that changes the whole board, not just one player's options, is what reviewers reward, but it also means the design is only "on" when enough differentiated factions are at the table. Descent 2E and Star Wars: Imperial Assault add a campaign-specific variant: an asymmetric one-vs-many role split (Overlord/Imperial player against heroes) is praised as a distinct-role strength, but both games separately draw complaints that scenario-to-scenario or mission-to-mission balance is uneven and can depend on player count. Dark Pact shows the same tension inside a single deck-builder: variable win-condition Pacts are the strategic-variety draw, but reviewers question whether all Pacts are equally hard to complete. Ark Nova carries this label in its own corpus tags but its sourced complaints are about bolted-on interaction cards, not power/faction imbalance, so it was excluded rather than forced in.

## Evidence by game

- dark-pact (src-007, src-009): reviews credit variable Pacts with creating substantially different build goals, while separately questioning whether all Pacts demand equivalent effort — an uneven-difficulty concern rather than a broad-consensus imbalance, confidence: medium
- descent-journeys-in-the-dark-second-edition (src-004): a review reports that quest-to-quest balance "falls down to the goals in the quests" and is "all over the place," an asymmetric-role balance complaint tied to scenario design rather than the Overlord/hero split itself, confidence: medium
- dune-imperium (src-010): A reviewer flagged that leader Glossu Rabban may be overpowered in the conflict phase, able to recruit soldiers easily and become "close to unstoppable" — a single, low-confidence outlier concern rather than a broad consensus, confidence: low
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): A reviewer found some introductory quests nearly damage-free and effectively automatic wins, i.e. the challenge undershoots what the hero roster's danger is meant to be tuned for, confidence: medium
- root (src-006): A reviewer called Root's asymmetry "some of the best... I've ever seen" with a "high skill ceiling," while also noting that "without expansions, the experience at lower player counts is much more muted," confidence: high
- star-wars-imperial-assault (src-003): a review argues campaign balance depends on player count, with a five-participant table (four Rebel heroes) reported as the strongest configuration, confidence: medium

## Where it works

- root (src-006): Reviewers praise faction asymmetry as the defining strength — "some of the best asymmetry I've ever seen" — because opposing factions are different machines competing over the same board, not palette-swapped variants, confidence: high
- descent-journeys-in-the-dark-second-edition (src-001, src-003): the Overlord-versus-heroes role split is foregrounded by both BGG and the publisher as the campaign's defining asymmetric structure, confidence: high
- star-wars-imperial-assault (src-004): giving one player a distinct opposing (Imperial) role against individual Rebel heroes is treated as a successful, central design choice rather than a bolted-on feature, confidence: high
- dark-pact (src-007, src-009, src-010): variable Pacts creating substantially different build goals is the corpus's clearest example of asymmetric win conditions read as a strength rather than an imbalance, confidence: high

## Where it fails

- dune-imperium (src-010): possible single-leader power outlier (Glossu Rabban) in the conflict phase, confidence: low
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): easy introductory quests can be near-automatic wins, undershooting the tension the hero powers are balanced against, confidence: medium
- root (src-006): faction asymmetry is diluted at low player counts without expansions, so the "different machines" experience the design is praised for depends on table size and ownership of extra content, confidence: high
- descent-journeys-in-the-dark-second-edition (src-004): quest-to-quest balance is reported as "all over the place," depending heavily on individual scenario goals rather than the role split itself.
- star-wars-imperial-assault (src-003): the best-balanced campaign experience is reported to require a specific, stable five-person group, making balance sensitive to table size.
- dark-pact (src-007, src-009): reviewers question whether all thirteen Pacts require equivalent effort to complete, an unconfirmed difficulty-tuning concern.

## Coverage gaps

Root remains the strongest single-game evidence base, but Descent 2E, Star Wars: Imperial Assault, and Dark Pact now corroborate the same shape: asymmetric roles/powers are praised as the design's core strength, while balance across those roles (scenario-to-scenario, mission-to-mission, or Pact-to-Pact) is the recurring complaint. Dune: Imperium's contribution remains a single low-confidence aside and Heroes of Terrinoth's is an adjacent difficulty-tuning claim rather than a direct power-balance complaint. The corpus would still benefit from a game with a well-documented, widely-corroborated overpowered-faction controversy (multiple independent sources, not one aside) to test whether this asymmetry-as-strength pattern holds when balance actually breaks down rather than merely thins out.
