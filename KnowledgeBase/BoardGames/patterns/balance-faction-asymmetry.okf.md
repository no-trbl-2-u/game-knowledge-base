## Summary

Evidence for this pattern has grown from a single strong case to seven
games, and the throughline sharpens: reviewers reward asymmetry when they
can point to a concrete instance where it produced a fair, differentiated
experience, and punish it when win conditions, campaign scenarios, or
matchup outcomes are visibly uneven — with the unevenness usually traced to
scenario/mission/quest design rather than a faction's raw power level. Root
remains the strongest praised case, treating faction asymmetry as the
game's best-in-class strength (though diluted at low player counts). Scythe
now supplies a comparable positive data point: "no specific faction had
felt too overpowered," with faction/mat/starting-location balance called
"a bright spot" — a rare instance where reviewers explicitly rule out an
imbalance complaint rather than simply not raising one. Set against these,
three campaign- or mission-driven games show the same failure shape from
different angles: Dark Pact's alternate win conditions are questioned for
demanding unequal effort across paths; Descent 2e's balance "falls down to
the goals in the quests" and is "all over the place"; and Imperial
Assault's campaign balance is "sensitive to mission design and player
count," with reviewers asking for more transparency in how missions and
player counts scale. Read together, these suggest asymmetry itself is not
what breaks balance — it is the surrounding scenario/mission/win-condition
design, not the faction's inherent power, that becomes the uncontrolled
variable reviewers notice and flag. Dune: Imperium's and Heroes of
Terrinoth's contributions remain single, narrower asides rather than
broad patterns.

## Evidence by game

- dune-imperium (src-010): A reviewer flagged that leader Glossu Rabban may be overpowered in the conflict phase, able to recruit soldiers easily and become "close to unstoppable" — a single, low-confidence outlier concern rather than a broad consensus, confidence: low
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): A reviewer found some introductory quests nearly damage-free and effectively automatic wins, i.e. the challenge undershoots what the hero roster's danger is meant to be tuned for, confidence: medium
- root (src-006): A reviewer called Root's asymmetry "some of the best... I've ever seen" with a "high skill ceiling," while also noting that "without expansions, the experience at lower player counts is much more muted," confidence: high
- dark-pact (src-007, src-009): Reviewers question whether all alternate-win requirements demand equivalent effort, implying asymmetric win paths may not be tuned to comparable difficulty, confidence: medium
- descent-journeys-in-the-dark-second-edition (src-004): Reviewer finds "balance ultimately falls down to the goals in the quests" and describes quest-to-quest balance as "all over the place," confidence: medium
- scythe (src-003): Reviewer reports "no specific faction had felt too overpowered," naming faction/player-mat/starting-location balance "a bright spot" of the design, confidence: medium
- star-wars-imperial-assault (src-003): Reviewer finds campaign balance and outcomes "sensitive to mission design and player count," confidence: medium
- star-wars-imperial-assault (src-003): The same review's better-if angle asks that "mission balance and player-count scaling were more transparent," confidence: medium

## Where it works

- root (src-006): Reviewers praise faction asymmetry as the defining strength — "some of the best asymmetry I've ever seen" — because opposing factions are different machines competing over the same board, not palette-swapped variants, confidence: high
- scythe (src-003): Reviewer reports no faction "had felt too overpowered," explicitly naming faction/mat/starting-location balance a strength rather than merely not flagging a problem, confidence: medium

## Where it fails

- dune-imperium (src-010): possible single-leader power outlier (Glossu Rabban) in the conflict phase, confidence: low
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): easy introductory quests can be near-automatic wins, undershooting the tension the hero powers are balanced against, confidence: medium
- root (src-006): faction asymmetry is diluted at low player counts without expansions, so the "different machines" experience the design is praised for depends on table size and ownership of extra content, confidence: high
- dark-pact (src-007, src-009): alternate win conditions are questioned as possibly unequal in the effort/difficulty they demand, confidence: medium
- descent-journeys-in-the-dark-second-edition (src-004): quest-level balance is reported as inconsistent ("all over the place"), with the quests' own goals — not faction/hero design — identified as the source, confidence: medium
- star-wars-imperial-assault (src-003): campaign balance and outcomes are reported as overly sensitive to mission design and player count, with reviewers asking for more transparency in that scaling, confidence: medium

## Coverage gaps

Scythe now joins Root as a second, independently-sourced strong case for
"asymmetry as a bright spot," which strengthens confidence that
well-tuned asymmetric factions read as a design strength rather than a
risk. The three new campaign/mission-driven games (Dark Pact, Descent 2e,
Imperial Assault) converge on a distinct sub-pattern — balance problems
attributed to scenario/quest/mission design rather than faction power
itself — that this doc hadn't previously captured; a future pass should
watch for whether that sub-pattern holds in other campaign games. The
corpus still lacks a well-documented, widely-corroborated overpowered-
faction controversy (multiple independent sources agreeing a specific
faction is broken, not merely thin at low player counts or campaign-
scenario-dependent) to test whether the asymmetry-as-strength pattern
holds when balance actually breaks down at the faction level.
