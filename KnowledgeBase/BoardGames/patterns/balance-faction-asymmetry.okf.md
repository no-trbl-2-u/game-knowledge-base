## Summary

Evidence has grown from one well-cited game (Root) plus thin asides to a
genuine cross-game pattern. The recurring finding: asymmetry is praised when
it produces *different strategic identities* at a comparable power level
(Root's opposing factions, Scythe's faction/mat/starting-location balance,
Star Wars: Rebellion's thematically-grounded Imperial-power-vs-Rebel-scrappiness
asymmetry), and criticized when one path, leader, or investment inside that
asymmetric system reliably outperforms the others (Dune: Imperium's Glossu
Rabban, Dune: Imperium Uprising's sandworm-doubling, Descent 2E's
quest-to-quest swings, Dark Pact's unresolved question over whether its
thirteen Pact victory conditions are equivalent). Scenario/campaign games add
a distinct axis: balance is tuned per-mission or per-quest rather than once
for the whole game, so "is it balanced" has to be asked scenario-by-scenario
(Descent 2E, Star Wars: Imperial Assault).

## Evidence by game

- dark-pact (src-007, src-009): reviewers question whether the thirteen Pact victory conditions are balanced/equivalent, confidence: medium
- descent-journeys-in-the-dark-second-edition (src-004): scenario/quest goals drive balance, and "balance varies widely between quests", confidence: medium
- dune-imperium (src-010): A reviewer flagged that leader Glossu Rabban may be overpowered in the conflict phase, able to recruit soldiers easily and become "close to unstoppable" — a single, low-confidence outlier concern rather than a broad consensus, confidence: low
- dune-imperium-uprising (src-004): the sandworm reward-doubling mechanic lets one investment path pull "decisively ahead," and ignoring sandworms "can feel like a mistake", confidence: medium
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): A reviewer found some introductory quests nearly damage-free and effectively automatic wins, i.e. the challenge undershoots what the hero roster's danger is meant to be tuned for, confidence: medium
- root (src-006): A reviewer called Root's asymmetry "some of the best... I've ever seen" with a "high skill ceiling," while also noting that "without expansions, the experience at lower player counts is much more muted," confidence: high
- scythe (src-003): faction, mat, and starting-location balance is a "bright spot"; no faction felt overpowered across the reviewer's games, confidence: medium
- star-wars-imperial-assault (src-003): campaign mission balance and five-player-count scaling are identified as pressure points needing more transparency, confidence: medium
- star-wars-rebellion (src-005, src-006): reviewers praise the asymmetric expression of Imperial grand-army power vs. Rebel scrappy-underdog vulnerability as thematically strong, confidence: high

## Where it works

- root (src-006): Reviewers praise faction asymmetry as the defining strength — "some of the best asymmetry I've ever seen" — because opposing factions are different machines competing over the same board, not palette-swapped variants, confidence: high
- scythe (src-003): independent reviewer testing across factions/mats/starting locations found no overpowered combination — asymmetry held up under play, not just on paper.
- star-wars-rebellion (src-005, src-006): the Imperial/Rebel power differential is itself the praised design — asymmetry expressing theme (overwhelming force vs. underdog scrappiness) rather than undermining balance.

## Where it fails

- dune-imperium (src-010): possible single-leader power outlier (Glossu Rabban) in the conflict phase, confidence: low
- dune-imperium-uprising (src-004): the sandworm-doubling mechanic recreates the same "one path pulls ahead" shape one game later, suggesting a recurring design tendency in this sub-series rather than an isolated balance miss.
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): easy introductory quests can be near-automatic wins, undershooting the tension the hero powers are balanced against, confidence: medium
- root (src-006): faction asymmetry is diluted at low player counts without expansions, so the "different machines" experience the design is praised for depends on table size and ownership of extra content, confidence: high
- dark-pact (src-007, src-009): an unresolved reviewer question over whether thirteen separately-designed victory conditions are actually equivalent is a harder balance claim to verify than a single overpowered faction.
- descent-journeys-in-the-dark-second-edition (src-004) and star-wars-imperial-assault (src-003): both campaign games tune balance per-scenario rather than once, so a single "is this game balanced" verdict doesn't transfer across missions or player counts.

## Coverage gaps

Root is no longer the only well-cited case — Scythe and Star Wars: Rebellion now supply corroborated "where it works" evidence, and the Dune Imperium family supplies a repeating "dominant path" failure shape across two games. The remaining gap is a widely-corroborated, multi-source overpowered-faction *controversy* (as opposed to a single reviewer's aside or an unresolved question) to test whether Root's asymmetry-as-strength pattern holds when balance actually, verifiably breaks down.
