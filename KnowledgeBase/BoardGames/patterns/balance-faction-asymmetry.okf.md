## Summary

Evidence for this pattern was thin at the last pass (3 of 4 candidate games), but two new campaign games — Descent 2E and Star Wars: Imperial Assault, both dual-role Fantasy Flight designs where one player controls the "enemy" side — add a distinct failure mode: quest/mission-level balance that varies scenario to scenario rather than a single overpowered faction or leader. The clearest signal remains Root, where reviewers treat faction asymmetry as the game's best-in-class strength and its main onboarding/low-player-count cost in the same breath — asymmetry that changes the whole board, not just one player's options, is what reviewers reward, but it also means the design is only "on" when enough differentiated factions are at the table. Ark Nova carries this label in its own corpus tags but its sourced complaints are about bolted-on interaction cards, not power/faction imbalance, so it was excluded rather than forced in.

## Evidence by game

- descent-journeys-in-the-dark-second-edition (src-004): scenario objectives are reported as driving balance, and that balance "varies widely between quests" — an inconsistency at the mission-design level rather than a single dominant faction, confidence: medium
- dune-imperium (src-010): A reviewer flagged that leader Glossu Rabban may be overpowered in the conflict phase, able to recruit soldiers easily and become "close to unstoppable" — a single, low-confidence outlier concern rather than a broad consensus, confidence: low
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): A reviewer found some introductory quests nearly damage-free and effectively automatic wins, i.e. the challenge undershoots what the hero roster's danger is meant to be tuned for, confidence: medium
- root (src-006): A reviewer called Root's asymmetry "some of the best... I've ever seen" with a "high skill ceiling," while also noting that "without expansions, the experience at lower player counts is much more muted," confidence: high
- star-wars-imperial-assault (src-003): campaign discussion identifies mission balance and a five-person "ideal" player count as pressure points, tying faction/role balance to group-size scaling rather than a single overpowered side, confidence: medium

## Where it works

- root (src-006): Reviewers praise faction asymmetry as the defining strength — "some of the best asymmetry I've ever seen" — because opposing factions are different machines competing over the same board, not palette-swapped variants, confidence: high

No other game in this set produced praised-design evidence tied specifically to power/faction balance (as opposed to interaction, theme, or general replayability).

## Where it fails

- dune-imperium (src-010): possible single-leader power outlier (Glossu Rabban) in the conflict phase, confidence: low
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): easy introductory quests can be near-automatic wins, undershooting the tension the hero powers are balanced against, confidence: medium
- root (src-006): faction asymmetry is diluted at low player counts without expansions, so the "different machines" experience the design is praised for depends on table size and ownership of extra content, confidence: high
- descent-journeys-in-the-dark-second-edition (src-004): quest-to-quest balance inconsistency means the overlord-vs-heroes matchup can feel fairer in some scenarios than others, independent of player skill.
- star-wars-imperial-assault (src-003): campaign balance and an "ideal" five-player recommendation both point at scaling as an unresolved pressure point in the Imperial-vs-Rebel structure.

## Coverage gaps

Root remains the only game with strong, well-cited evidence on both sides of this pattern; Dune: Imperium's contribution is a single low-confidence aside, Heroes of Terrinoth's is an adjacent difficulty-tuning claim, and the two new campaign games (Descent 2E, Star Wars: Imperial Assault) describe *quest-level* balance variance rather than a single overpowered faction or leader — a related but distinct failure mode from Root's or Dune's. The corpus would still benefit from a game with a well-documented, widely-corroborated overpowered-faction controversy (multiple independent sources, not one aside) to test whether Root's asymmetry-as-strength pattern holds when balance actually breaks down at the faction level rather than the scenario level.
