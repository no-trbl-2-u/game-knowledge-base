## Summary

Evidence was thin (3 games) and has grown to 8. The clearest signal still comes from
Root, where reviewers treat faction asymmetry as the game's best-in-class strength
and its main onboarding/low-player-count cost in the same breath — asymmetry that
changes the whole board, not just one player's options, is what reviewers reward, but
it also means the design is only "on" when enough differentiated factions are at the
table. The new evidence sharpens a split within that reward: Terra Mystica and Scythe
are both explicitly praised for *achieved* balance across many factions (14 and
several respectively), while Star Wars: Imperial Assault's asymmetric 1-vs-many
structure is praised as a design choice even as its campaign-level balance draws
separate complaints, and Dark Pact and Descent (2e) both report balance complaints
tied not to the factions/powers themselves but to uneven alternate-objective or
quest-goal design layered on top of them. Ark Nova carries this label in its own
corpus tags but its sourced complaints are about bolted-on interaction cards, not
power/faction imbalance, so it was excluded rather than forced in.

## Evidence by game

- dune-imperium (src-010): A reviewer flagged that leader Glossu Rabban may be overpowered in the conflict phase, able to recruit soldiers easily and become "close to unstoppable" — a single, low-confidence outlier concern rather than a broad consensus, confidence: low
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): A reviewer found some introductory quests nearly damage-free and effectively automatic wins, i.e. the challenge undershoots what the hero roster's danger is meant to be tuned for, confidence: medium
- root (src-006): A reviewer called Root's asymmetry "some of the best... I've ever seen" with a "high skill ceiling," while also noting that "without expansions, the experience at lower player counts is much more muted," confidence: high
- dark-pact (src-007, src-009): reviews question whether all alternate-win requirements demand equivalent effort, treating this as an open balance question rather than a resolved complaint, confidence: medium
- descent-journeys-in-the-dark-second-edition (src-004): a review reports that campaign balance "falls down to the goals in the quests" and is "all over the place," locating the imbalance in quest design rather than in the asymmetric Overlord-vs-heroes structure itself, confidence: medium
- scythe (src-003): the reviewer calls faction and home-base balance "a bright spot," saying no specific faction had felt overpowered across their games, confidence: medium
- star-wars-imperial-assault (src-003, src-004): the review frames the 1-Imperial-vs-1–4-Rebel-heroes structure as a design strength, while a separate discussion raises campaign-balance concerns tied to the five-person ideal and uneven campaign outcomes, confidence: medium
- terra-mystica (src-003, src-004): Board Game Quest calls the game "low-luck and high-strategy" across 14 factions with fully deterministic victory parameters, treating broad faction balance as achieved rather than aspirational, confidence: high

## Where it works

- root (src-006): Reviewers praise faction asymmetry as the defining strength — "some of the best asymmetry I've ever seen" — because opposing factions are different machines competing over the same board, not palette-swapped variants, confidence: high
- scythe (src-003): explicit praise that no faction had felt overpowered is the corpus's clearest "balance achieved, not just attempted" claim.
- terra-mystica (src-003, src-004): deterministic victory parameters across 14 factions are praised as evidence the asymmetry was engineered to stay fair at scale.
- star-wars-imperial-assault (src-004): the asymmetric one-vs-many structure itself is praised as a design choice, independent of the campaign-balance concerns raised elsewhere about the same game.

## Where it fails

- dune-imperium (src-010): possible single-leader power outlier (Glossu Rabban) in the conflict phase, confidence: low
- heroes-of-terrinoth (src-005): easy introductory quests can be near-automatic wins, undershooting the tension the hero powers are balanced against, confidence: medium
- root (src-006): faction asymmetry is diluted at low player counts without expansions, so the "different machines" experience the design is praised for depends on table size and ownership of extra content, confidence: high
- dark-pact (src-007, src-009): whether alternate-win paths are truly equivalent in required effort remains an open, unresolved question across reviews.
- descent-journeys-in-the-dark-second-edition (src-004): campaign balance is reported as inconsistent quest-to-quest, a complaint about scenario design rather than the core asymmetric structure.
- star-wars-imperial-assault (src-003): campaign-level balance concerns and a five-person "ideal" player count are raised alongside praise for the core asymmetric structure.

## Coverage gaps

Root, Scythe, and Terra Mystica now supply strong, well-cited evidence on both sides of this pattern. Dune: Imperium's contribution remains a single low-confidence aside, and Dark Pact's and Descent (2e)'s complaints are about quest/objective design layered onto asymmetric structures rather than the asymmetric powers themselves — a genuinely corroborated overpowered-*faction* (not overpowered-*quest*) controversy is still missing from this pattern.
