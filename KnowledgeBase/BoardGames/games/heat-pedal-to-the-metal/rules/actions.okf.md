## Summary

Core player agency is expressed through gear selection, card commitment, Heat spending, cooling, boosting, slipstreaming, and choosing when to accept corner risk.

## Source-backed facts

- Claim: A player may stay in gear, shift up one, or shift down one for free; shifting two positions costs Heat.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: Gear rules allow staying, shifting up 1, shifting down 1, or shifting up/down 2 by paying 1 Heat.
  Confidence: high
- Claim: Current gear determines exactly how many cards must be played: one in first gear through four in fourth gear.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: The gear table maps 1st to 1 card, 2nd to 2, 3rd to 3, and 4th to 4.
  Confidence: high
- Claim: Heat cards normally cannot be played from hand.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: Play Cards rules say players may play all card types "except Heat cards."
  Confidence: high
- Claim: Stress cards add uncertainty by forcing draws until a valid speed value is revealed.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: Stress resolution draws from the deck until a basic speed card is revealed and adds that value.
  Confidence: high

## Rules / Mechanics

The action economy makes speed mandatory rather than optional: a fourth-gear player must commit four playable cards if possible. Heat is the license to break tempo, but spent Heat later pollutes the deck and can deny options.
