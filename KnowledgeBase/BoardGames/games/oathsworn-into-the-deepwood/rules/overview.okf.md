## Summary

The game is structured as a persistent campaign: each chapter has a Story part and an Encounter part, while a recurring Free Company of Oathsworn develops across chapters.

## Source-backed facts

- Claim: Oathsworn: Into the Deepwood is a cooperative game for 1-4 players. An Oathsworn campaign is divided into chapters, and each chapter has two parts: the Story (narrative, played with the Story Book and City Maps, ~30-60 minutes) and the Encounter (tactical miniature combat, played with the Game Board and miniatures, ~90-120 minutes).
  Source: src-008, src-009
  Evidence: "Oathsworn: Into the Deepwood is a cooperative game of epic dark fantasy for 1-4 players... An Oathsworn campaign is divided into chapters, with each chapter made up of two parts" (Story Rule Book p.3, identical text in Encounter Rule Book p.3).
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Progress is saved between play sessions using per-character Save Bags and a Free Company Save Bag, so a chapter's Story and Encounter parts can be split across separate sessions.
  Source: src-008
  Evidence: "Your progress is saved between sessions using Save Bags." (Story Rule Book p.3); full save-bag contents list (Story Rule Book p.15).
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Every rule in both rulebooks is written assuming Might Cards are drawn, but a set of Might Dice can be substituted for cards at any time, per-draw, and the two can even be mixed within a single draw.
  Source: src-008, src-009
  Evidence: "Included in the game is a set of Might Dice that can, at any time, be used instead of drawing cards from the Might Decks... You can even combine the use of cards and dice at the same time" (Story Rule Book p.3, identical text in Encounter Rule Book p.3).
  Confidence: high

## Open questions

The total number of chapters in the base campaign and the ultimate campaign-victory condition are not stated in either rules PDF (they describe ongoing chapter/leveling structure, not a terminal chapter count); that detail lives in the Story Book/Encounter Book scenario content, which is out of scope for this corpus's copyright posture, so it stays UNKNOWN.
