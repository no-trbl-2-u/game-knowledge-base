## Summary

Actions resolve movement, attacks, conditions, looting, card loss, forced movement, and elemental augmentation on a hex map.

## Source-backed facts

- Claim: Move abilities spend movement points through valid hexes, while attacks are melee or ranged and require line of sight.
  Source: src-002
  Evidence: PDF pp. 7-9 define movement limits, melee/ranged targeting, and line-of-sight through walls.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Every attack draws one attack-modifier card per target; Advantage and Disadvantage draw two and retain the better or worse result.
  Source: src-002
  Evidence: PDF p. 8 defines modifier draws, doubling/null cards, and Advantage/Disadvantage.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Basic actions let any card become Attack 2 or Move 2, while lost actions move the card to the lost pile and reduce future longevity.
  Source: src-002
  Evidence: PDF p. 15 describes the basic-action icons and lost-action timing.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Long rests occur on initiative 99, return discarded cards except one chosen lost card, heal two, and refresh spent items.
  Source: src-002
  Evidence: PDF p. 22 describes the long-rest turn and its effects.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Element tokens move from Strong to Waning to Inert and can be consumed to augment abilities.
  Source: src-002
  Evidence: PDF pp. 25-26 describe infusion at end of turn, waning at end of round, and consumption from Strong or Waning.
  Confidence: high

## Rules / Mechanics

The card system offers a fallback basic action but makes powerful effects costly through loss, timing, and hand exhaustion.

## Player friction

The official rules layer target selection, modifier decks, conditions, rest timing, and element timing; the review calls the resulting card management a source of careful planning and mental overhead.
