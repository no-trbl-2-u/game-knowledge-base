# Better-if opportunities grounded in reviews

## Strategic depth

- Claim: The standalone game would better sustain repeat play if its strong bluffing and risk-management core had a broader base-game encounter, upgrade, and Final Boss pool.
  Source: src-005, src-006
  Evidence: "Both reviews praise the core while reporting that limited content becomes familiar quickly; this opportunity concerns the standalone box, not importing expansion rules into this corpus."
  Confidence: high

## Randomness

- Claim: Players seeking skill-dominant combat would be better served by more ways to manage catastrophic burst rerolls without erasing the push-your-luck threat.
  Source: src-005, src-006
  Evidence: "The reviewers identify extreme damage chains and upgrade-reveal luck as friction while still valuing the resulting tension."
  Confidence: medium

## Player interaction

- Claim: The game is a better fit when onboarding states plainly that promises are nonbinding and direct interference is intentional.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: "Ars praises bluffing and betrayal but warns that take-that play will not suit groups uncomfortable with attacking friends' plans."
  Confidence: high

## Component clarity

- Claim: Higher-contrast presentation would better support quick reading of cards and board tracks in the game's simultaneous, timing-sensitive environment.
  Source: src-006
  Evidence: "Meeple Mountain specifically criticizes the drab visual palette; the contrast recommendation is a bounded synthesis from that attributed complaint."
  Confidence: medium
