# Independent reception

## Praised design

- Claim: The simultaneous commitment system creates bluffing, negotiation, betrayal, and readable counterplay rather than off-turn inactivity.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: "The reviewer describes squinting at opponents, tracking their used cards, and anticipating counters; almost all complexity comes from playing opponents rather than the rules overhead."
  Confidence: high

- Claim: The fight-or-bank decision successfully creates dread around risking accumulated Blood Echoes.
  Source: src-004, src-005
  Evidence: "Ars calls a greedy risk exhilarating; RPGFan praises planning, prediction, and choosing when to press luck or enter the Dream."
  Confidence: high

- Claim: The base game is easy to teach and brisk across its supported player range.
  Source: src-004, src-006
  Evidence: "Ars calls it easy to learn and simple to play; Meeple Mountain says simultaneous play keeps five-player games brisk."
  Confidence: high

## Reported friction

- Claim: Dice damage can produce abrupt outcomes even from the weakest die, and this luck can frustrate players expecting a skill-dominant Bloodborne translation.
  Source: src-005, src-006
  Evidence: "RPGFan says dice rolls may provoke outrage; Meeple Mountain notes an unlimited burst reroll chain can kill a full-health hunter."
  Confidence: high

- Claim: The standalone game's limited upgrade and boss content reduces replay depth after only a few sessions.
  Source: src-005, src-006
  Evidence: "RPGFan says the limited content left its group wanting; Meeple Mountain says the 32-card upgrade deck and five bosses expose most content quickly."
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Direct interference and take-that effects make enjoyment group-dependent.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: "Ars says the nastiness worked for the reviewer but explicitly warns players who cannot stand being mean to friends."
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Visual and token production drew criticism from at least one independent review.
  Source: src-006
  Evidence: "Meeple Mountain criticizes the drab palette and game-render card art; this is an attributed review judgment, not a corpus inference."
  Confidence: high
