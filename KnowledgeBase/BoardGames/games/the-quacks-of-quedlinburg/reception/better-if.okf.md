## Summary

Quacks would be better if its physical chips resisted wear, its randomness was more clearly framed for luck-sensitive players, its private simultaneous draws were harder to abuse, and its late-game catch-up remained plausible without invalidating early performance.

## Better-if opportunities

- component-clarity: Upgrade chip durability or provide sleeves/capsules by default.
  Source: src-005
  Evidence: "After even just 5 games I was beginning to notice wear on the chips"; "plastic chips... a bit expensive".
  Confidence: high
- randomness: Teach the game as a risk spectacle first and a strategy game second.
  Source: src-005
  Evidence: "the random nature of the game means that I wouldn’t recommend buying this without playing it or seeing it played first if you don’t like luck".
  Confidence: high
- player-interaction: Add optional public checkpoints or reveal rituals to reduce cheating anxiety in simultaneous brewing.
  Source: src-005
  Evidence: "many opportunities to cheat" during "largely solitaire" brewing.
  Confidence: medium
- runaway-leader: Preserve comeback pressure deeper into the final rounds.
  Source: src-005
  Evidence: "from round 7/8... it’s usually pretty clear who is going to win" and others may be unable to affect it.
  Confidence: medium
- accessibility: Support younger or new players with guided first-play help.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: "it might be helpful to let them have an adult buddy the first play through".
  Confidence: medium
- setup-teardown: Keep module selection guided because mismatched ingredient books can damage balance.
  Source: src-006
  Evidence: "make sure all the books are from that set" or the game can become "wildly unbalanced".
  Confidence: medium

## Design implications for SomberSoft

If Axiomancer uses bag/deck pollution and bust risk, make mitigation visible: odds estimates, durable digital state, clear comeback bands, and a final-round catch-up curve that creates hope without softening judgment into mush.
