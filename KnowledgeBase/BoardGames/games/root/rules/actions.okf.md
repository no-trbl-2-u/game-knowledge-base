## Summary

Root’s shared actions are movement, cards/crafting, and battle, but action access varies by faction. Permission, not raw availability, is the heart of the system.

## Source-backed facts

- Claim: Dicebreaker identifies moving, using/crafting cards, and battling as common backbone mechanics across factions.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: "there are three general mechanics that all factions need to perform and are the backbone of the game: moving, using/crafting cards and battling."
  Confidence: high
- Claim: Movement uses roads between clearings and requires rule of the origin or destination clearing.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: "the clearing they are coming from must be connected by a road" and "must rule either the clearing they are coming from or the one they are going to."
  Confidence: high
- Claim: The official rules library says actions, abilities, and persistent effects cannot be interrupted unless explicitly allowed.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: "You cannot interrupt an action ... ability ... or persistent effect ... with another effect unless it explicitly allows it."
  Confidence: high
- Claim: Cards have suits, with bird functioning as a special flexible suit in Dicebreaker's summary.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: "cards have suits" and "bird suit ... can be used as any of the other three."
  Confidence: high

## Rules / Mechanics

Root’s action economy is partly topological. Moving and battling are not abstract menu items; they are conditioned by rule, adjacency, faction permission, and local board state.
