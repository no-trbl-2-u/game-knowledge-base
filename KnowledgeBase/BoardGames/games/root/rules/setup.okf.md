## Summary

Root setup combines a shared board setup with faction-specific setup. This matters because asymmetry starts before the first turn: faction boards and advanced setup choices define initial positions and strategic pressure.

## Source-backed facts

- Claim: Leder provides official Learning to Play and Walking Through Root files in addition to the Law of Root.
  Source: src-002
  Evidence: "Learning to Play" and "Walking Through Root."
  Confidence: high
- Claim: The rules library advanced setup sequence includes choosing starting hands: each player keeps three of five cards, returns two, then the shared deck is shuffled.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: "Each player chooses three cards in their hand to keep and puts the other two cards face down on the shared deck. When finished, shuffle the shared deck."
  Confidence: high
- Claim: The setup sequence places score markers on the zero space.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: "Each player places their faction's score marker on the '0' space on the score track."
  Confidence: high
- Claim: Dicebreaker emphasizes that factions have their own setup listed on their faction boards.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: "factions also have their own setup described on their faction board."
  Confidence: high

## Rules / Mechanics

Setup is not merely component placement. It assigns factions, initializes score, establishes starting hands, and follows faction-specific instructions that create initial asymmetry.
