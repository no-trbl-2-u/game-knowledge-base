## Summary

Root is a medium-heavy asymmetric war and adventure game in which woodland factions compete through radically different procedures on one shared map. Its value to the corpus is the clean separation between shared rules and faction-specific engines: the board state is common, but each player’s operational grammar differs.

## Rules / Mechanics

The shared layer is territorial: clearings, paths, rule, movement, battle, cards, crafting, and victory points. The faction layer defines how each player converts phase permissions into board pressure.

## Player friction

Root’s burden is not one ruleset but many partial rulesets in collision. Players must understand their faction enough to play and other factions enough to police them.

## Praised design

The design achieves high replayability through faction asymmetry without abandoning a shared contested board. Each faction changes both its controller’s incentives and the opponent’s threat model.

## Better-if opportunities

- onboarding: asymmetric games need faction-facing procedure aids and a staged first game.
- rules-ambiguity: a formal rules library reduces table-law disputes for dense edge cases.
- balance-faction-asymmetry: faction mix and player count affect the pressure economy.
