## Summary

Root would be better for more tables if the onboarding path reduced cross-faction opacity, if table aids made policing other factions easier, and if low-player-count faction ecology were less dependent on expansions.

## Better-if opportunities

- onboarding: Teach by common spine first, then faction exception. Dicebreaker’s note that learning demands more than memorizing general rules supports staged learning.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: "Learning how to play Root demands more than just memorising the general rules, it requires time."
  Confidence: high
- rules-ambiguity: Dense asymmetric systems benefit from a searchable official rules library with precedence rules.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: "The term cannot is absolute" and "the player taking their turn chooses" unclear simultaneous order.
  Confidence: high
- strategic-depth: The game asks players to understand their own faction and opponent factions; mastery burden is part of the attraction and the barrier.
  Source: src-006
  Evidence: "to become proficient at the game, you not only have to understand your own faction, but every other faction at the table."
  Confidence: high
- balance-faction-asymmetry: Lower player counts and faction mix can mute the experience.
  Source: src-006
  Evidence: "without expansions, the experience at lower player counts is much more muted."
  Confidence: high
- expansion-bloat: Expansion factions open the system but can create a perceived completeness pressure.
  Source: src-006
  Evidence: "having them all is the ideal experience" and "The Law of Root booklet itself even heightens the feeling of incompleteness."
  Confidence: medium

## Player friction

The core friction is epistemic. A player can know the rules of Root and still not understand the game state because the other players’ faction engines remain opaque.

## Praised design

The same faction asymmetry that burdens onboarding creates the highest praise: opponents are not variants on the same deck or board, but different machines competing over the same forest.

## Open questions

- Which player aids best reduce opponent-faction opacity without flattening discovery.
