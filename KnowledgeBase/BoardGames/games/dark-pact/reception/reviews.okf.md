## Summary

Reviewers consistently identify the Pacts, grimoire, multiplier play, and gain-to-hand timing as the game's distinctive tactical layer. Criticism concentrates on Action bookkeeping, sequences that run long without a proportionate payoff, familiar deck-builder scaffolding, and potentially uneven Pact difficulty.

## Praised design

- Claim: Variable Pacts create substantially different build goals from game to game.
  Source: src-007, src-009, src-010
  Evidence: Reviews emphasize alternate victory requirements as the main source of strategic variety.
  Confidence: high
- Claim: The grimoire supplies flexible reserved tools without simply adding them to the normal draw cycle.
  Source: src-007, src-010
  Evidence: Reviews praise access to Silver, Inscription, and the extra multiplier as a tactical reserve.
  Confidence: medium
- Claim: Gain-to-hand purchases support opportunistic same-turn pivots.
  Source: src-007, src-009
  Evidence: Reviews highlight immediate use of acquired cards as a major tactical distinction.
  Confidence: high

## Player friction

- Claim: Long effect chains and Action counting can create sequencing overhead.
  Source: src-007, src-008, src-010
  Evidence: Reviews describe extended turns and the need to track whether further Actions remain legal.
  Confidence: medium
- Claim: Some elaborate combinations feel less rewarding than their execution time suggests.
  Source: src-008, src-010
  Evidence: Critical accounts contrast combo length with comparatively modest payoff.
  Confidence: medium
- Claim: Pact difficulty may be uneven.
  Source: src-007, src-009
  Evidence: Reviewers question whether all alternate-win requirements demand equivalent effort.
  Confidence: medium
- Claim: The core market deck-building structure can feel derivative despite its novel overlays.
  Source: src-008, src-009
  Evidence: Reviews compare the base loop to established market deck-builders while distinguishing its Pacts and multipliers.
  Confidence: medium
