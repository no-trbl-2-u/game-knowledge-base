## Summary

Full review retrieved and read (Meeple Mountain, reviewer Abram Towle). The reviewer praises the streamlined turn structure, the dual Health/Threat resource tension, and the accessible onboarding via prebuilt starter decks; the main complaints are occasional deck-driven bad luck and post-game component sorting.

## Praised design

- Hero and villain combinations appear to be the central replayability signal.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: "With nearly unlimited combinations of notable heroes and villainous schemes..."
  Confidence: high
- Turn structure is streamlined with little fiddly per-turn management.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: "Players go, and then the Villain goes. There isn't a lot of fiddly management of components or resetting things each turn besides readying and drawing cards."
  Confidence: high
- Dual Health/Threat tracks create meaningful decision tension.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: "Having dual clocks – Health and Threat – forces you to make tough decisions as a player about which lowercase threat is more immediate."
  Confidence: high
- Onboarding for new players is well designed via prebuilt starter decks and a separate Learn to Play booklet.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: "Marvel Champions designed their introduction well, packaging up prebuilt Spider-Man and Captain Marvel decks for a two-player game against Rhino." / "Marvel Champions is one of the easier card games to get into..."
  Confidence: high
- The hero-pack expansion model (buy only the heroes you want) is praised rather than seen as bloat.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: "Selling so many heroes in individual Hero packs is genius; I don't want to have to buy a huge box when I have my eye on a single hero." / "I could play with just the core game and be perfectly content...That's a major strength of the game, because it doesn't make you feel obligated to necessarily go buy more boxes."
  Confidence: high

## Player friction

- Deck-driven variance can occasionally feel unfair.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: "you are set up for failure simply in how the decks present themselves" / "spinning your wheels while you delay the inevitable"
  Confidence: high
- Post-game component sorting is a chore: character-specific cards must be manually resorted back into their owner's stack.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: "When you're packing up the game you need to remember to remove all of the character-specific cards and store them together in the box." / "If you don't like organizing, you may find yourself in a world of hurt trying to hunt down Black Panther's Obligations that somehow ended up with a stack of Captain Marvel's cards."
  Confidence: high
