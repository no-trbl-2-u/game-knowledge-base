## Summary

Gaia Project is a 2017 space-themed economic strategy game in which asymmetric factions colonize a variable galaxy, upgrade buildings, and climb research tracks. The packet is scoped to the German first-edition base game and publishes only claims supported by the official product page, official rulebook, and two independent reviews.

## Source-backed facts

- Claim: The official edition presents Gaia Project as a space adaptation of the Terra Mystica play principle and lists 1–4 players with a 60–150 minute duration.
  Source: src-001
  Evidence: The official product page describes the move into the galaxy and lists “1 – 4” and “60 – 150 Minuten.”
  Confidence: high

- Claim: The game gives each player one of 14 factions with different capabilities.
  Source: src-002
  Evidence: Rulebook p. 1: “In Gaia Project führt jeder von euch eines von 14 verschiedenen Völkern.”
  Confidence: high

- Claim: The game uses a variable board assembled from space sectors, six rounds, research tracks, technologies, and endgame scoring tiles.
  Source: src-002
  Evidence: Rulebook pp. 2, 8, 10, and 17–18 describe ten sectors, six rounds, the research board, technology tiles, and two randomly selected endgame tiles.
  Confidence: high

## Rules / Mechanics

Players build an income engine by placing mines, upgrading buildings, converting transdimensional planets through Gaia projects, forming alliances, and advancing research. Spatial reach and planet habitability constrain expansion; the action phase is a repeated one-action-at-a-time cycle in which players may pass and leave the round.

## Player friction

The game has many coupled tracks: power circulation, planetary reach, terrain conversion costs, building upgrades, alliances, research, income, boosters, and round/endgame tiles. The packet does not claim an exhaustive component or technology inventory.

## Praised design

The independent reception evidence treats the game as an evolved, highly variable successor to Terra Mystica. Its variable galaxy, asymmetric factions, and changing final scoring conditions are repeatedly presented as sources of replayability.
