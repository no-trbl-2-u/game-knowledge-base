## Summary

Ark Nova scores through appeal and conservation tracks moving toward each other. Endgame triggers when the counters meet or pass in the same scoring area, then final scoring cards and endgame card effects modify the result.

## Source-backed facts

- Claim: Appeal affects income and is advanced primarily through animals; conservation points are necessary to win and may grant bonuses.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: Rulebook p. 18 section "Appeal and Conservation Points" and p. 3 introduction describe appeal generating revenue and conservation bonuses.
  Confidence: high
- Claim: Endgame triggers at end of turn or during a break when Conservation and Appeal counters are in the same scoring area or have passed one another.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: Rulebook p. 19: "If at the end of your turn or during a break your Conservation counter and Appeal counter are ever in the same scoring area, or if they have passed one another, the end of the game is triggered."
  Confidence: high
- Claim: If triggered at the end of a player's turn, all other players take one more turn; if triggered during a break, all players take one more turn.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: Rulebook p. 19: "all other players (but not you) will take one more turn"; "during a break, all players (including you) will take one more turn."
  Confidence: high
- Claim: Final scoring cards can add 1–4 conservation points and cards with endgame icons also score.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: Rulebook p. 19: "Final Scoring card(s)"; "cards with an end-of-game icon"; "gain 1–4 additional Conservation Points".
  Confidence: high
- Claim: A reviewer praised the game but found endgame scoring inelegant enough to require checking the rulebook.
  Source: src-007
  Evidence: "Endgame scoring is not elegant... I have to pull out the rule book".
  Confidence: medium

## Rules / Mechanics

Endgame sequence:
1. Check whether appeal and conservation counters meet/pass in a scoring area at end of turn or during break.
2. Assign final turns depending on trigger timing.
3. Score final scoring cards and endgame card icons.
4. Determine final victory points by final marker relation.

## Player friction

The visual race is clear; final arithmetic and trigger timing are less clear. The distinction between end-of-turn and break triggers changes who receives final turns.

## Praised design

Opposed tracks make the end visible throughout the game. The race can be accelerated by any player pursuing crossing rather than by a shared depletion clock.

## Better-if opportunities

- scoring/endgame: player aid with exact final-turn and scoring protocol.
- rules ambiguity: table examples for end-of-turn vs break trigger.
- accessibility: visual scoring area highlights on track.

## Design implications for SomberSoft

If using opposing tracks, make the current net score and final-turn rights immediately calculable. Let the drama be strategic, not arithmetic.
