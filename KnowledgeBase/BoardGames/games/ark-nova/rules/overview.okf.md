## Summary

Ark Nova's objective is to create the most successful zoo by advancing appeal and conservation on opposing tracks. The game's practical engine is an action row of five cards; the rightmost cards are stronger, and the used card cycles back to the weakest slot.

## Rules / Mechanics

Core loops:
- Draw/select cards.
- Build the zoo map with enclosures, kiosks, pavilions, and special structures.
- Play animals that satisfy cost, icon, enclosure, and adjacency requirements.
- Use association work for reputation, partner zoos, universities, conservation projects, and donations.
- Trigger breaks for hand limit, worker recall, market refresh, and income.
- Race until appeal and conservation counters meet or pass.

## Player friction

Heavy icon language, many card effects, and spatial prerequisites produce a learning cliff.

## Praised design

The action-row makes tempo a visible resource. The opposing scoring tracks create a race without a traditional victory-point pile.

## Better-if opportunities

- onboarding: guided first turns and prerequisite overlays.
- component clarity: icon tooltips/reference integration.
- scoring/endgame: persistent endgame preview.

## Design implications for SomberSoft

When a game asks players to parse many cards, the action economy must be simple and ritualistic. Ark Nova proves that a five-card conveyor belt can tame a broad ruleset.
