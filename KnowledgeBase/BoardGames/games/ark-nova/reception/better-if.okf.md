## Summary

Better-if opportunities are not theoretical ornament. They are field reports. Ark Nova is loved, yet its complaints are sharp enough to extract design weapons.

## Better-if opportunities

### 1. More deck churn and planning valves

- Labels: randomness, strategic depth, onboarding
- Source-backed complaint: Stidjen says the large card stack makes the game tactical and can cause strategic plans or conservation goals not to pan out; the reviewer wanted "either less cards, or more ways to churn through the deck."
  Source: src-008
  Evidence: "output randomness is so large"; "can't really plan ahead"; "less cards, or more ways to churn through the deck."
  Confidence: high
- Source-backed complaint: Tabletop Gaming reports criticism that the 212-card zoo deck causes "waiting for cards to come that never do."
  Source: src-009
  Evidence: "212 zoo cards"; "waiting for cards to come that never do."
  Confidence: high
- SomberSoft implication: If a design has a large card corpus, provide discard-for-filter, tag search, market cycling, goal wildcards, or card-type guarantees.

### 2. Cleaner endgame scoring ceremony

- Labels: scoring/endgame, rules ambiguity, accessibility
- Source-backed complaint: Board Game Quest says endgame scoring is not elegant and requires pulling out the rulebook.
  Source: src-007
  Evidence: "Endgame scoring is not elegant... pull out the rule book".
  Confidence: high
- SomberSoft implication: The scoring ritual must be executable from the board state and a single player aid. A brilliant race loses force if the finish requires accountancy.

### 3. Shorter / better-scaled high-player-count experience

- Labels: downtime, turn pacing, accessibility
- Source-backed complaint: Board Game Quest would not play at four players because it would take too long.
  Source: src-007
  Evidence: "Won't touch a four player game – it would take too long."
  Confidence: high
- Source-backed signal: BGG community best player count is 2 and recommends 1–3.
  Source: src-001
  Evidence: "Best: 2 · Recommended: 1–3."
  Confidence: high
- SomberSoft implication: Scale action count, market pressure, and simultaneous planning aids by player count. Do not merely permit four players; earn them.

### 4. Better-integrated interaction / attack effects

- Labels: player interaction, accessibility, balance/faction asymmetry
- Source-backed complaint: Board Game Quest says attack cards feel like a tacked-on solution for more interaction.
  Source: src-007
  Evidence: "Attack cards seem like a tacked on solution for more player interaction."
  Confidence: high
- Source-backed mitigation: Official FAQ allows groups disliking Poison/Pilfering-style interactive abilities to use alternate solo-game effects instead.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: "If your group dislikes interactive abilities... use the alternate solo-game effects."
  Confidence: high
- SomberSoft implication: If interaction is optional in spirit, formalize it as a mode. Do not hide social compatibility behind card exceptions.

### 5. Smaller solo footprint / board ergonomics

- Labels: setup/teardown, accessibility, component clarity, solo/co-op automation
- Source-backed complaint: Stidjen says the game board is too big for solo play and arguably in general.
  Source: src-008
  Evidence: "the game board... is just too big for solo play (or in general, arguably)."
  Confidence: high
- SomberSoft implication: Solo modes deserve dedicated layout compression. A solo player should not need the full ceremonial table of multiplayer if the system does not demand it.

### 6. Better graphic/component clarity

- Labels: component clarity, accessibility, onboarding
- Source-backed complaint: Tabletop Gaming concludes component and design irks prevent perfection.
  Source: src-009
  Evidence: "component and design irks veer towards ugly duckling".
  Confidence: medium
- SomberSoft implication: Heavy icon systems need ruthless visual hierarchy, not merely icon density.

## Player friction

Top friction: large-deck variance, four-player length, endgame scoring, and interaction fit.

## Praised design

Even the complaint sources praise the central action-card engine and replayability. The lesson is not to make Ark Nova smaller; it is to make its frictions more governable.

## Open questions

- Would a modular market variant preserve replayability while reducing deck frustration?
