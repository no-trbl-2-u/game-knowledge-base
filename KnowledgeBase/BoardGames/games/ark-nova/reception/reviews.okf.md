## Summary

Reception is strongly positive: BGG rank and rating are exceptional, and the sampled reviews praise the action-slot engine, tactical card adaptation, replayability, theme integration, and zoo growth arc. Criticism concentrates on player count length, endgame scoring elegance, large-deck variance, slow opening turns, table footprint, interaction cards, and graphic/component issues.

## Player friction

- Long at 4 players and best received at 2.
- Large deck increases variance and tactical adaptation burden.
- Early card requirements can slow new-player momentum.
- Endgame scoring can require rulebook reference.
- Table footprint is large, especially for solo.
- Interaction/attack effects may feel bolted onto a mostly parallel euro.

## Praised design

- Action-slot timing is the cited engine.
- Card variety creates replayability and combo discovery.
- Theme works better than many euros because spatial zoo growth and conservation goals are legible.
- Opposing appeal/conservation tracks give visible race pressure.

## Better-if opportunities

- downtime: discourage or redesign 4-player length burden.
- randomness: more market churn or deck sculpting.
- scoring/endgame: cleaner final scoring aid.
- player interaction: make attack cards feel systemic or provide official non-attack alternates.

## Design implications for SomberSoft

Ark Nova's reception says the market will forgive complexity when the central action engine is memorable and tactile. It will not forgive opacity at the finish line or dead time at higher player counts.
