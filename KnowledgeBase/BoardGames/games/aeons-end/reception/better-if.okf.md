## Summary

*A better-if reading of Aeon's End* is not that the core should be softer. Its strength is severity. The improvement pressure is around approachability: faster setup, clearer onboarding into deterministic deck order, and accessibility for heavy card text.

## Better-if opportunities

- Better if setup were faster to prepare and reset.
  Source: src-005
  Evidence: "El setup del juego es muy tedioso".
  Confidence: medium
- Better if onboarding taught no-shuffle sequencing through controlled first scenarios.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: "If you bloat your deck even a bit, you will pay for it"; "it wasn’t a bad draw, it was a bad choice."
  Confidence: high
- Better if the market offered more perceived variety without losing strategic readability.
  Source: src-005
  Evidence: "La variedad del mercado es algo limitada."
  Confidence: medium
- Better if high card-text dependence were reduced through reference aids, icons, or localized support.
  Source: src-001
  Evidence: "Extensive use of text - massive conversion needed to be playable."
  Confidence: high
- Better if difficulty warnings and Nemesis recommendations were explicit enough to keep first losses instructive rather than demoralizing.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: "I lost my first 15 games"; "No matter who you fight, the Nameless are punishing."
  Confidence: high

## Player friction

- Setup-teardown friction.
- Onboarding burden around deck-order planning.
- Random turn-order volatility can feel brutal even when bounded.
- Card-text accessibility.

## Praised design

- The no-shuffle deck is worth preserving because it produces accountable losses.
- High difficulty supports co-op table tension when the teaching path is sound.
