# Better-if opportunities

Each label below is carried by retrieved reviewer wording, not inferred from
the rulebook alone.

## turn-pacing

- Claim: Downtime is the most consistently reported friction, and both reviewers treat the printed 1-8 player range as unrealistic because of it.
  Source: src-002, src-004
  Evidence: "There is almost nothing for players to do between turns so any number of players beyond 4 or 5 will suffer significant downtime"; "This can be a particular problem when there are seven other players and you do little while waiting to roll the dice again."
  Confidence: high

- Claim: The design would be better if it gave non-active players something to resolve or decide during another investigator's adventure.
  Source: src-002
  Evidence: The reviewer's mitigation is social rather than mechanical — "Luckily, social interaction and planning can make the delay between turns more bearable" — which locates the gap in the design, not the group.
  Confidence: medium

## randomness

- Claim: Task resolution is fully dice-gated, so players who want deterministic planning have limited recourse beyond consumable rerolls.
  Source: src-001, src-002
  Evidence: The official rules resolve adventures by matching rolled symbols to printed tasks with dice removed as tasks are completed; RPGnet records the standing criticism that this reads as "glorified Yahtzee with custom dice."
  Confidence: high

- Claim: The game would be better if a bad roll sequence did not simultaneously consume the resources needed to recover from it.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: "a few bad rolls of the dice can cause a failure while simultaneously using up all of one's resources. If a player has no extra toys to help with investigations it can be a difficult job to complete a location - especially if there are no 'easier' ones in play."
  Confidence: high

## strategic-depth

- Claim: A dominant no-risk strategy exists: hard adventures can simply be ignored, with no penalty for avoidance.
  Source: src-002
  Evidence: "there are no penalties for simply avoiding particularly difficult adventures, and stashing the tough monsters there to be left undisturbed."
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Thematic engagement, rather than mechanical depth, carries the experience — so analytically-minded groups get less from it.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: "If the players are willing to put forth a bit of effort to imagine and flesh out the actions and events occurring in the game, Elder Sign can provide a nice moody challenge. If your players tend toward the dry, analytical types, the game can be entertaining but it will lose a bit of its luster."
  Confidence: high

## solo-coop-automation

- Claim: Solo play works without a separate automation system, but leans on lenient death penalties rather than designed solo tension.
  Source: src-002, src-004
  Evidence: RPGnet reports enjoying it "both in solo efforts and with others"; Opinionated Gamers notes "the penalties for dying are not very harsh and thus a solo player could simply burn through several characters in a single game."
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Player-count balance is uneven in a way a scaling rule could address, with more investigators reported as easier.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: "the game does seem to be slightly easier with larger number of players... I suspect some of this advantage for more players comes from having more characters over which to spread damage along with the special powers each character possesses."
  Confidence: medium
