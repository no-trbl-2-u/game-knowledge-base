## Summary

The strongest evidence-backed improvement pressures are onboarding clarity, campaign/progression communication, and combat-resolution timing. These are analytical classifications, not claims that the publisher endorses them.

## Better-if opportunities

- Claim: The game would be better if first-time players had a compact phase aid that made the four-part turn and the Mythos/summoning check order visible.
  Source: src-002
  Evidence: The turn sequence spans pp. 12–17 and the rules summary compresses it on p. 20.
  Confidence: medium
- Claim: The game would be better if the ritual-disruption objective and each Episode's two special actions were surfaced in a single setup reference.
  Source: src-002
  Evidence: Episode cards define the ritual and special actions (pp. 5, 14, 18).
  Confidence: medium
- Claim: The game would be better if the distinction between investigator progression and Elder One progression were reinforced at the table.
  Source: src-002
  Evidence: Skill levels advance at sanity thresholds while the Elder One advances from Mythos summoning symbols and Stage reveals (pp. 8, 17–18).
  Confidence: medium
- Claim: The game would be better if crowded map tiles had a documented stacking or token-substitution convention, because the miniature footprint can exceed the space a tile provides.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: "You will run out of room for miniatures on some of the tiles."
  Confidence: medium
- Claim: The game would be better if a mitigation lever damped the swing of dice and card randomness, which the reviewer reports can decide a game outright.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: "You can get completely ruined by dice and card randomness in this game."
  Confidence: medium
