## Summary

Arkham would be better if the path from first box to meaningful deck construction were made clearer, cheaper, and better scaffolded without muting the campaign/deckbuilding depth that gives it its force.

## Better-if opportunities

- Claim: Better onboarding should preserve the official first-game path: starter decks and introductory scenario first, customization later.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: New players are advised to use "the introductory scenario" and "pre-constructed decks."
  Confidence: high
- Claim: Better product access matters because revised-core support was partly a response to reprint scarcity and onboarding strain.
  Source: src-005
  Evidence: "lack of reprints and scarcity" strained the system's ability to bring in new players.
  Confidence: medium
- Claim: Better beginner deck support matters because weak early decks can make losing feel punishing before the deck-construction engine opens up.
  Source: src-006
  Evidence: "The player decks provided are poor, unfocused" and "losing with bad decks with few options sucks."
  Confidence: medium
- Claim: Better campaign storage and collection management matters because expanded play asks players to maintain scenarios, decks, upgrades, and consequences.
  Source: src-007
  Evidence: Outcomes have "lasting implications" and experience enables players to "purchase better cards for your deck."
  Confidence: medium

## Player friction

- Entry cost and card-pool expansion pressure.
- Rules-reference timing complexity.
- Scenario setup and campaign-state bookkeeping.
- Beginner decks may fail to show why the larger system is admired.

## Praised design to preserve

- Campaign upgrade cadence introduces deck construction gradually.
- Scenario identity changes deck priorities.
- Cooperative action pressure turns deck choices into table tactics.

## Open questions

- Compare revised-core onboarding claims against original-core complaints with more direct review sampling.
