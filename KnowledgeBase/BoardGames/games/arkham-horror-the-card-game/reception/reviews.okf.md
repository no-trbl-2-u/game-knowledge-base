## Summary

Reception evidence praises Arkham's campaign deck-construction engine and condemns its entry barriers: card-pool cost, product-line sprawl, unforgiving early decks, and rules/collection overhead.

## Praised design

- Claim: Board Game Quest characterizes the system as campaign deckbuilding with progressive narrative.
  Source: src-005
  Evidence: "This is a deckbuilding game with campaign elements and a progressive narrative."
  Confidence: medium
- Claim: There Will Be Games strongly praises Arkham as a deck construction/deck-building system.
  Source: src-006
  Evidence: "Arkham Horror The Card Game by Fantasy Flight Games is the best deck construction/deck building game I have played."
  Confidence: medium
- Claim: Co-op Board Games emphasizes campaign consequences and deck improvement.
  Source: src-007
  Evidence: Completing a scenario "enables you to purchase better cards for your deck" while scars may also persist.
  Confidence: medium

## Player friction

- Claim: There Will Be Games describes the game's entry barrier as severe and expensive.
  Source: src-006
  Evidence: "getting there is not easy" and is "staggeringly expensive."
  Confidence: medium
- Claim: There Will Be Games criticizes the original core entry for weak starter deck experience and limited deck possibilities.
  Source: src-006
  Evidence: "The player decks provided are poor, unfocused" and beginners lack "the player card sideboard to handle challenges."
  Confidence: medium
- Claim: Co-op Board Games flags player-count/card-pool friction in the base game.
  Source: src-007
  Evidence: "If you wish to play with more than two characters, you will need another copy of the base game."
  Confidence: medium
- Claim: Board Game Quest frames the revised/updated core set as a response to scarcity and onboarding strain.
  Source: src-005
  Evidence: "Due to a lack of reprints and scarcity, the system was strained by continuing forward while losing the ability to bring in new players."
  Confidence: medium

## Better-if opportunities

- Onboarding: better starter decks, guided upgrades, and first-campaign sequencing.
- Setup-teardown: campaign and encounter storage that reduces scenario assembly friction.
- Expansion-bloat: product-line clarity and reprint availability preserve access to the engine.
