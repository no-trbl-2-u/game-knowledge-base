## Summary

Each round follows a five-part cadence: Spirit Phase, Fast Power Phase, Invader Phase, Slow Power Phase, and Time Passes. The turn is designed around precommitment, visible threat, and delayed consequence.

## Rules / Mechanics

- Spirit Phase: decide growth and card commitment.
- Fast Power Phase: prevent, move, defend, destroy, or set immediate effects before enemy action.
- Invader Phase: fear effects and invader ravage/build/explore engine advance.
- Slow Power Phase: resolve delayed powers after enemy action; often best for repositioning against the next threat.
- Time Passes: cleanup, discard played cards, clear temporary effects/damage per rules.

## Player friction

- Slow powers are strategically rich but counterintuitive for new players because they cannot stop the immediate ravage unless timed from a prior turn.
- Simultaneous decisions reduce formal downtime but increase table negotiation.

## Praised design

- The cadence makes the future readable without making it trivial.
- Fast/slow timing creates tactical identity among cards and spirits.

## Better-if opportunities

- rules ambiguity: highlight what slow powers can still usefully affect after invader action.
- turn pacing: digital threat previews can shorten table calculus.

## Design implications for SomberSoft

Split actions by timing windows. Give delayed actions enough value that foresight feels like mastery, not punishment.
