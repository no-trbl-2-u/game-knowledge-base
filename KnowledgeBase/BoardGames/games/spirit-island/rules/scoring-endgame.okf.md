## Summary

There is no point scoring in the conventional sense. The endgame is victory or defeat. Fear lowers the required board-clearing threshold; blight, spirit destruction, and invader-deck exhaustion are the principal defeat clocks.

## Source-backed facts

- Claim: At game start, victory requires destroying every explorer, town, and city; as terror rises, victory becomes easier.
  Source: src-001
  Evidence: "At game start, winning requires destroying every last explorer, town and city"; "as you frighten the Invaders more and more, victory becomes easier".
  Confidence: high
- Claim: The rulebook states players win by driving away the invaders.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: "Win by driving away the Invaders."
  Confidence: high
- Claim: Players lose if blight runs out, any spirit has no presence left, or the invader deck is empty when an explore is needed.
  Source: src-003

  Confidence: high
- Claim: Fear deck exhaustion can also produce victory in reception summaries.

  Confidence: medium

## Rules / Mechanics

- Terror Level I: strictest victory condition.
- Higher Terror Levels: fewer invader types need to be eliminated.
- Fear cards create intermediate rewards and expose Terror Level dividers.
- Loss clocks are multi-axis: ecological damage, spirit survival, and time.

## Player friction

- Some reviews report that when the outcome is already decided, final turns can feel procedural or anticlimactic.

## Praised design

- Dynamic victory conditions align theme and mechanics: fear changes what victory means.

## Better-if opportunities

- scoring/endgame: add concession/auto-resolve support or endgame acceleration when victory/loss certainty is high.
- turn pacing: reveal outcome certainty carefully; do not drain drama with bookkeeping.

## Design implications for SomberSoft

Avoid static victory ladders. Let morale/fear/reputation reshape the win condition over time.
