## Summary

Player actions are principally growth choices, card play, power resolution, presence placement, fear generation, defense/damage/movement, and Dahan support. The action system is indirect: a spirit often wins by preventing a future build or ravage rather than by killing the present threat.

## Rules / Mechanics

- Growth actions define economy and future reach: energy, presence, cards, reclaim.
- Card play defines this-turn capabilities and elemental threshold access.
- Range and sacred-site/presence targeting govern where powers apply.
- Movement effects can prevent future builds/ravages by breaking invader adjacency or population.
- Defense prevents land/Dahan damage; surviving Dahan retaliate after ravage.
- Fear generation both advances victory threshold and earns fear cards.

## Player friction

- The action vocabulary is broad; new players may overvalue direct damage and undervalue push/gather/defend timing.
- Range/targeting/presence restrictions are a likely rules-check burden.

## Praised design

- Element icons make card selection a deckbuilding puzzle layered atop board tactics.
- Each spirit's action profile expresses theme and encourages table role differentiation.

## Better-if opportunities

- component clarity: explicit threat overlays for lands where one push prevents build/ravage.
- onboarding: teach indirect prevention as a first-class move, not an advanced tactic.

## Design implications for SomberSoft

Make non-damage actions visually consequential. If prevention is optimal, show the catastrophe it erased.
