## Summary

Reception is positive but not frictionless. The two independent reviews praise the spy system and the standalone polish, while Ryan Board Games reports concerns about sandworm-driven balance, six-player downtime, and added complexity.

## Source-backed facts

- Claim: Dune News Net reports that Uprising adds sandworms and spies while retaining a deck-building/agent-placement structure.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: The review identifies Uprising as standalone, names sandworms and spies as additions, and describes cards determining where agents can go.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Ryan Board Games calls spies a genuine improvement because they can mitigate blocked spaces through infiltration or be recalled for card draw.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: The review says, “The spy mechanic is a genuine improvement to the formula,” and explains both choices.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Ryan Board Games reports that sandworm reward doubling can feel unbalanced, that six-player play has downtime, and that Uprising is more complex than the original.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: Its “What Doesn't” section names all three criticisms.
  Confidence: medium

## Player friction

The reception evidence points to a familiar trade: the same added systems that create more strategic texture also create more prerequisites and a steeper teach.

## Praised design

Spies are the clearest shared positive signal in the retrieved reviews. The standalone format and solo/two-player support are also presented favorably by Ryan Board Games.
