# Better-if Analysis

- Claim: **Onboarding** is the clearest witnessed pressure: Vasel could teach in roughly ten to fifteen minutes but considered the two beautiful books much longer and more detailed than necessary and warned readers not to miss the Quest-book rules.
  Source: src-006
  Evidence: Review question 10 contrasts short oral teaching with two long rulebooks.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: **Player interaction** should preserve imperfect communication, uncertain Traitor presence, and costly accusation rather than making cooperation fully transparent; both reviewers identify suspicion around ordinary moves as the base game's strongest fun.
  Source: src-006, src-007
  Evidence: Vasel questions 3, 7, and 8; Quinns's accusation example and closing evaluation.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: **Strategic depth** rests in choosing the least damaging evil progression and allocating limited hands across multiple pressures, but Quinns says these functional quest mechanics are eclipsed by the social Traitor system; revisions should not mistake more quest arithmetic for the witnessed core appeal.
  Source: src-006, src-007
  Evidence: Vasel questions 2 and 11 describe dual evil/heroic choices; Quinns calls quest mechanics functional and interesting but eclipsed by the Traitor.
  Confidence: medium
