# Independent Review Witnesses

- Claim: In a release-year review after multiple plays, Tom Vasel praised variable evil choices, replay variation, suspicion even when no Traitor was dealt, broad group appeal, artwork/components, and roughly hour-long play after teaching.
  Source: src-006
  Evidence: Review questions 2-3 and 9-13; witness says the negative-event choice changes focus, traitor uncertainty drives interaction, components are high quality, and his groups enjoyed it.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Vasel also found the separate Rules and Quest books beautiful and detailed but overlong, warned that both must be read to avoid missing rules, and reported only one loyal victory in nearly ten plays across eight mostly first-time groups.
  Source: src-006
  Evidence: Review questions 10 and 12.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Quinns praised the constant multi-Quest panic, Arthurian theme, simple functional quest mechanics, and especially the accusation/no-guaranteed-Traitor social layer; he characterized the base game as solid rather than dazzling.
  Source: src-007
  Evidence: Review paragraphs surrounding the Quest overview, sword end state, and traitor accusation example.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Quinns did not play Merlin's Company; his expansion remarks are excluded from this base-only reception record.
  Source: src-007
  Evidence: Review closing explicitly says the expansion was not played.
  Confidence: high
