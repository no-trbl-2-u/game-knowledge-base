# Reception reviews

## Summary
Reception is strongly positive. The dominant praise is that the adaptation justifies itself through cooperative play, physical production, and a faithful-but-streamlined deckbuilding system. The dominant reservations are cost, size, fiddliness, setup burden, rulebook assumptions, and solo value versus the original video game.

## Praised design
- Cooperative multiplayer creates a new reason for the adaptation to exist.
- Shared defeat condition makes aid and coordination meaningful.
- Physical production, storage, and included sleeves receive repeated praise.
- Numerical compression preserves identity while reducing handling overhead.
- Flexible action order reduces downtime and supports tactical collaboration.

## Player friction
- setup/teardown: heavy box, large footprint, lengthy setup.
- rules ambiguity: rulebook may rely on video-game familiarity.
- solo/co-op automation: solo play can feel redundant beside the digital original.
- component clarity: many decks, tokens, sleeves, and status markers require organization.

## Design implications for SomberSoft
A digital-to-tabletop or tabletop-to-digital adaptation must earn its body. Here the body is earned by co-op coordination, tactile rewards, and shared tactical planning. Faith alone is insufficient; the new medium must claim new territory.
