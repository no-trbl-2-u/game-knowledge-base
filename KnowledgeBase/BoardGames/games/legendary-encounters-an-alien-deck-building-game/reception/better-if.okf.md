## Summary

Better-if opportunities center on reducing the cost of entry without dulling the pressure. The design's virtue is stress; the improvements should clarify setup, timing, and scenario handling rather than remove danger.

## Better-if opportunities

- Better if setup had scenario trays, labeled packs, or a first-game checklist.
  Source: src-005
  Evidence: Product summaries describe a 600-card set and inserts, implying a large sorting burden.
  Confidence: medium
- Better if timing windows for objective completion, hive spawning, and event/hazard triggers were summarized in one reference.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: The review says timing between objective completion and hive spawning "can be an issue".
  Confidence: medium
- Better if difficulty scaling by player count were more transparent at setup.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: The review says the game is "brutally hard, especially at higher player counts" and notes more Alien drones are added for each player.
  Confidence: medium
- Better if onboarding separated core deckbuilder decisions from movie-specific exceptions.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: The review reports scenario-specific locations/objectives and hazards/events tied to objectives/locations.
  Confidence: medium

## Player friction

- Component sorting and setup-teardown.
- Rules ambiguity around timing collisions.
- Swingy tension when hidden hazards/events emerge.
- Accessibility burden from text-heavy cards and licensed knowledge.

## Praised design to preserve

- Hidden threat movement.
- Cooperative deckbuilder economy under a shared clock.
- Scenario identity mapped to familiar movie arcs.
