## Type definition

Players must collectively complete a mission. Mission success permits a second-stage comparison of personal scores, influence, or achievements; one player then wins the individual contest.

Representative implementations identified in the sweep:

- *Churchill*: the Allies must defeat the Axis, after which one power becomes “victor of the peace.”
- *Legendary: Marvel*: defeat the Mastermind, then compare victory points.

## Source-backed facts

- Claim: *Churchill* formally separates winning the common war from winning the postwar political contest.
  Source: src-001
  Evidence: GMT’s description frames the individual result as determining the “victor of the peace” after the Allied struggle.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: A weak common opponent makes the cooperative gate ceremonial and leaves only the personal scoring race with real force.
  Source: src-002
  Evidence: the review criticizes the base *Legendary* Mastermind as too easy for experienced groups.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Some groups preserve *Legendary*’s villain fight but ignore or subordinate its competitive scoring.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: the title is discussed within cooperative deckbuilders and the common challenge is treated as the meaningful victory experience.
  Confidence: medium

- Claim: Personal ranking after a satisfying shared victory can feel like contamination of the cooperative ending.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: the review describes the tension between defeating the villain together and then comparing individual victory points.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Other players experience the same ending as a group accomplishment with one best performer, which can soften the sting of losing the ranking.
  Source: src-005
  Evidence: indexed discussion excerpt characterized the result as collective success plus a standout contributor.
  Confidence: medium

- Claim: Individual scoring can create incentives that conflict with the fictional common mission.
  Source: src-006
  Evidence: the discussion asks whether a player’s best move may be not to win the war, or not to win it too decisively.
  Confidence: medium

## Distinct strengths

- The common mission supplies a meaningful shared accomplishment even to players who lose the ranking.
- The two-stage ending can make rivalry legible: first survive or succeed, then determine supremacy.
- The structure fits rival governments or institutions whose common strategic goal masks incompatible visions of the aftermath.

## Distinct dangers

- Players may house-rule away the personal ranking, evidence that the mission works but the sole-winner layer does not.
- If the mission is too easy, cooperation is theater; if too hard, personal strategy is theater.
- Scoring incentives that recommend weakening the shared mission can violate the fiction even when they are mathematically sound.
