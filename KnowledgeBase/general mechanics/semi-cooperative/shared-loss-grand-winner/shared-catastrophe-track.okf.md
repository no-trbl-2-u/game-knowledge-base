## Type definition

Players manage pollution, unrest, bankruptcy, invasion, or another global system. Crossing its terminal threshold ends the contest in collective defeat; staying below it preserves the individual victory race.

Representative implementations identified in the sweep:

- *CO₂*: prevent ecological collapse; if the group succeeds, highest score wins.
- *Archipelago*: manage rebellion while pursuing private scoring objectives.
- *The Republic of Rome*: preserve Rome while competing for political supremacy.

## Source-backed facts

- Claim: A catastrophe track can function as an ongoing cost imposed on the players merely to keep the individual contest alive.
  Source: src-001
  Evidence: the reviewer calls rebellion management a “tax” on continuing the game and notes that players with stronger positions have more to lose.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Hidden scoring makes the burden of paying that tax uncertain because the table cannot perfectly identify who benefits most from continued survival.
  Source: src-001
  Evidence: the review praises uncertainty around private scoring goals and the ambiguous motives behind apparently irrational actions.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: *CO₂* aligns private profit-seeking with a shared ecological failure condition, producing moral and strategic pressure around greed.
  Source: src-002
  Evidence: the review emphasizes balancing profitable development against the possibility of planetary failure.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: The same climate-collapse structure is praised when its theme makes selfish optimization consequential rather than abstract.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: review discussion links the competitive economy to the need to avert the common environmental loss.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Some players reject the “cooperative” label when the only cooperation is shared punishment and players lack meaningful positive assistance.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: community discussion argues that everybody suffering for collective inefficiency does not by itself create cooperation.
  Confidence: medium

- Claim: A long personal plan erased by other players’ failure to control the shared track can feel outside the player’s control and render the session wasted.
  Source: src-005
  Evidence: the archived review criticizes extensive planning being nullified by collective pollution failure.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: The subtype fits political systems in which rival factions all depend upon the survival of the polity they seek to dominate.
  Source: src-006
  Evidence: the review frames *The Republic of Rome* around preserving Rome amid competitive senatorial ambitions.
  Confidence: medium

## Distinct strengths

- The common meter gives negotiations a concrete object: who pays, when, and in what proportion.
- The leader can be restrained organically because the player with the most valuable position often has the most to lose from collapse.
- Greed damages the system that makes victory possible, so selfish play gains an internal limit.

## Distinct dangers

- If the safe boundary is opaque or random, brinkmanship becomes accidental suicide.
- If one player can move the meter too sharply, inexperience or spite can erase everyone’s session.
- If the track is toothless, the game is ordinary competition wearing cooperative language.
- If the track is overwhelming, personal scoring becomes decorative.
