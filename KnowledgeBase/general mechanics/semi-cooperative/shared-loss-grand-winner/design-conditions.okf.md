## Synthesis status

The following are **design inferences**, not direct rule claims. They distill the reception evidence preserved in the subtype, praise, and friction records.

## Conditions for success

### 1. Collective failure is credible but legible

- Claim: Players need to estimate the common danger well enough to choose brinkmanship rather than stumble into it.
  Source: src-001
  Evidence: reception of *Archipelago* ties the appeal to pressure from rebellion and the defect to its delicate, derail-prone balance.
  Confidence: medium

Operational implication: expose the current threat, the plausible next-step range, and which actions can materially change it. Hidden information may obscure motives; it should not make the terminal boundary unknowable.

### 2. Every player retains a plausible path back into contention

- Claim: A player who cannot imagine personal victory has weak reason to prefer shared survival over common ruin.
  Source: src-002
  Evidence: indexed community testimony warns that the structure makes it easy to throw the game so nobody wins.
  Confidence: medium

Operational implication: preserve comeback paths, secondary leverage that can convert into score, or a formal outcome better than collective defeat. Mere participation is not enough once victory is mathematically dead.

### 3. Contributions are flexible and compensable

- Claim: The survival burden works best when players can divide costs, trade compensation, and alter who pays rather than face a binary volunteer-or-lose demand.
  Source: src-001
  Evidence: rebellion operates as a recurring shared tax under unequal incentives.
  Confidence: medium

Operational implication: permit split payments, enforceable exchanges, deferred obligations, or several different contribution channels. The system should create negotiation, not only coercion.

### 4. Betrayal creates advantage without trivially ending the session

- Claim: Selfish play is most interesting when timing matters and reckless sabotage can hurt the saboteur without providing an immediate “nobody wins” switch.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: *Cutthroat Caverns* is praised because betraying too early can cause the whole party to die.
  Confidence: high

Operational implication: make defection local, reversible, costly, or contestable before making it terminal. Preserve room for response.

### 5. The theme justifies conflicted loyalties

- Claim: Corporations, governments, politicians, and desperate adventurers support the mechanic better than fiction built around unconditional solidarity.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: *CO₂* reception praises the pressure between private profit and shared ecological survival.
  Confidence: high

Operational implication: state openly why the characters need one another and why only one can prevail. Do not ask players to violate heroic fiction merely because the scoring track says so.

## Two terminal failure states

### Toothless common threat

- Claim: If the group nearly always survives, the game is ordinary competition wearing cooperative language.
  Source: src-005
  Evidence: *Legendary* reception shows players subordinating personal scoring when the shared villain fight is the experience they value.
  Confidence: medium

### Weaponized common threat

- Claim: If one noncontender can easily trigger failure, the game gives that player a button marked “nobody wins.”
  Source: src-002
  Evidence: the cited forum concern is precisely the ease of throwing the entire game to deny all winners.
  Confidence: medium

## Design verdict

The mechanic’s strength and defect are the same: **cooperation is never innocent**. It succeeds when survival is a visible, negotiable constraint upon rivalry. It fails when survival is either ceremonial or hostage to the player least invested in completing the contest.

## Test questions

- Can every player explain the next plausible common-loss event?
- Does a trailing player have a credible route from contribution to renewed contention?
- Can survival costs be split or compensated?
- Can betrayal be answered before it ends the session?
- Does the fiction explain both mutual dependence and sole victory?
- Are hidden objectives numerous and balanced enough to resist rote inference?
- Does teaching explicitly say that collective success is a prerequisite, not a shared formal win?
