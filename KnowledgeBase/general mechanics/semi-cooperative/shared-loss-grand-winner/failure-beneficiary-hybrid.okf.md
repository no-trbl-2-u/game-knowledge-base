## Type definition

Most players share a failure condition, but a hidden separatist, rebel, or opportunist may instead qualify for victory when the common system collapses. This is a hybrid between grand-winner semi-cooperation and a hidden-traitor structure.

Representative implementations identified in the sweep:

- *Archipelago*: rebellion defeats everyone except a qualifying Separatist.
- *The Republic of Rome*: particular rebel situations can convert Rome’s collapse into that rebel’s victory.

## Source-backed facts

- Claim: A hidden failure-beneficiary makes ordinary reluctance to contribute harder to interpret because selfish conservation and active sabotage can look alike.
  Source: src-001
  Evidence: the review’s hidden-goal and separatist structure makes apparently irrational actions ambiguous.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Political-collapse themes can support a role whose personal victory no longer depends on preserving the polity shared by the other factions.
  Source: src-002
  Evidence: rebel incentives sit against the normal senatorial need to preserve Rome.
  Confidence: medium

- Claim: Hidden objectives lose tension when experienced players can infer them too readily from a small or predictable objective set.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: the *Nemesis* review argues that predictable objectives weaken dynamic diplomacy and suspicion.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Objective draws can create uneven burdens when one player’s goal aligns with normal cooperation while another’s requires severe sabotage.
  Source: src-003
  Evidence: review discussion contrasts objectives compatible with survival against goals that push players toward destructive behavior.
  Confidence: medium

## Distinct strengths

- Suspicion emerges from observable behavior without requiring every selfish player to be a formal traitor.
- The hybrid can justify dramatic reversals when a player’s true relationship to the common threat is revealed.
- Hidden motives amplify player-reading and bluffing around who believes they benefit from survival.

## Distinct dangers

- Objective memorization can collapse uncertainty into deduction by rote.
- Unequally hostile objectives can make the draw more decisive than negotiation or strategic play.
- A failure-beneficiary can make common ruin too easy to weaponize unless sabotage is costly, legible, and contestable.

## Boundary note

Evidence from *Nemesis* is used only for adjacent hidden-objective dynamics. The research track did not classify every *Nemesis* configuration as a strict one-winner/shared-loss implementation.
