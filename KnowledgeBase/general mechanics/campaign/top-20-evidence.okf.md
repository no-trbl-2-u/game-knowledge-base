# Campaign mechanics — twenty-game evidence pass

This synthesis extracts only campaign-mechanic evidence from Bathcat's twenty-game campaign-RPG intake. Canonical identity, complete sources, rules, reception, and visuals remain in each game's regular directory.

## Working model

A campaign is a contract between sessions. It must answer four questions clearly: **what persists, what resets, what unlocks, and what becomes unreachable**. The strongest designs make those answers visible in character state, deck/loadout state, world infrastructure, scenario access, or irreversible legacy residue.

## Character and party progression

- **Pandemic Legacy: Season 1** (`src-001`): Decisions, named locations, character consequences, and introduced components can carry forward into subsequent games in the same copy. Confidence: high.
- **Gloomhaven: Jaws of the Lion** (`src-002`): Character class progression, retirement/life-goal effects, unlocked content, and scenario outcomes persist through the campaign. Confidence: medium.
- **The 7th Continent** (`src-010`): The save system allows the group to interrupt and resume later, while the card-based continent, inventory, experience, and curse progress form the persistent adventure state. Confidence: high.
- **Too Many Bones: Undertow** (`src-011`): Persistence is expressed through continuing Gearloc development, acquired loot, skills, professions, stats, and Progress Points across the adventure. Confidence: high.
- **Descent: Legends of the Dark** (`src-013`): Campaign persistence is established by the sixteen-quest Blood and Flame arc and recurring heroes, but the exact carry-forward state was not verified from the blocked rule source. Confidence: medium.
- **Sword & Sorcery: Immortal Souls** (`src-015`): Heroes grow across the story through Soul points, restored legendary status, multiple powers, magic, soul weapons, and artifacts. Confidence: high.
- **Mice and Mystics** (`src-016`): The suggested campaign rules preserve story achievements and abilities, allow one carried search card, and reset starting equipment, party items, individual achievements, and cheese between chapters. Confidence: high.
- **Stuffed Fables** (`src-019`): The overarching story branches through player choices and outcomes, and independent review reports replayable chapters with branching paths; persistence is primarily narrative and discovery-based rather than heavy character bookkeeping. Confidence: medium.

## World, map, or infrastructure state

- **Frosthaven** (`src-003`): Character advancement, unlocked classes/content, settlement development, calendar/scenario state, and party resources persist between sessions. Confidence: low.
- **Sleeping Gods** (`src-007`): A journey log records the party's position and progress so a session can stop and resume later, and players can swap in or out across the campaign. Confidence: high.
- **The Lord of the Rings: Journeys in Middle-earth** (`src-008`): Victories and defeats alter the campaign path, while heroes retain inventory, knowledge, experience, skills, and upgraded equipment between adventures. Confidence: high.
- **ISS Vanguard** (`src-009`): Mission logs preserve narrative progress and planetary records preserve changes to explored worlds for later sessions; crew can be healed or memorialized after missions. Confidence: high.

## Deck and loadout growth

- **Clank! Legacy: Acquisitions Incorporated** (`src-004`): Map, company, cards, characters, and story-facing changes persist across the campaign, while the official page presents the campaign as continuing beyond individual adventures. Confidence: high.
- **Earthborne Rangers** (`src-014`): The next session resumes at the map location where the party rested, while the deck is augmented with improved equipment, refined skills, and journey memories. Confidence: high.
- **Middara: Unintentional Malum – Act 1** (`src-018`): Campaign persistence is explicit: encounters are not repeated, win/loss outcomes alter the continuing story, hidden decks unlock campaign content, and XP advances characters. Confidence: high.
- **Pathfinder Adventure Card Game: Rise of the Runelords – Base Set** (`src-020`): Scenario rewards advance characters by adding cards, abilities, and powers to their decks, and the reviewer explicitly identifies ongoing campaign play as the game's intended core. Confidence: high.

## Save, scenario, and narrative continuity

- **Pandemic Legacy: Season 0** (`src-005`): Team identities, discoveries, rule changes, and campaign consequences persist across the linked operations in the same copy. Confidence: low.

## Irreversible legacy mutation

- **Pandemic Legacy: Season 2** (`src-006`): Permanent changes include writing, stickers, opened materials, scratched or destroyed components, while board state, hands, and incidents reset between games; a failed month gets one further attempt before the campaign advances. Confidence: high.
- **Aeon's End: Legacy** (`src-012`): Legacy Game and Scenario / Mission / Campaign Game are explicit BGG mechanism tags, establishing persistent campaign structure; exact reset and carry-forward rules remain unverified. Confidence: high.
- **Aeon Trespass: Odyssey** (`src-017`): Decisions affect future sessions and campaigns, while alternative branches and light legacy mechanics make outcomes persistent and replayable. Confidence: high.

## Cross-game friction

- **Pandemic Legacy: Season 1** (`src-001`): The main friction is irreversible or spoiler-sensitive campaign state, plus the possibility that the fixed campaign arc limits replay value after completion.
- **Gloomhaven: Jaws of the Lion** (`src-002`): The review identifies long scenario commitment and the sting of replaying a failed lengthy scenario as meaningful friction.
- **Frosthaven** (`src-003`): The likely friction surface is the unusually large rules, component, setup, and campaign-state burden, but this dossier does not treat that as independently verified reception evidence.
- **Clank! Legacy: Acquisitions Incorporated** (`src-004`): The structural friction is the tension between shared campaign story and competitive incentives to race, hoard advantage, and let other adventurers absorb danger; this is a design interpretation, not a fetched review verdict.
- **Pandemic Legacy: Season 0** (`src-005`): The principal expected friction is campaign irreversibility and spoiler sensitivity, but a fetched independent review is required before treating that as reception evidence.
- **Pandemic Legacy: Season 2** (`src-006`): The legacy format imposes one-way, destructive state changes and a fixed campaign sequence, creating replay and commitment friction for groups that want resettable or drop-in play.
- **Sleeping Gods** (`src-007`): The same breadth creates long sessions, a large table footprint, substantial reading and bookkeeping, and a high commitment to campaign continuity.
- **The Lord of the Rings: Journeys in Middle-earth** (`src-008`): The app is a dependency rather than an optional aid, and procedural scenario generation plus skill-deck tests can make outcomes feel less transparent or less narratively authored to some players.
- **ISS Vanguard** (`src-009`): The same scope produces a heavy rules and component burden, substantial setup and teardown, long campaign commitment, and bookkeeping across crew, logs, decks, and planetary state.
- **The 7th Continent** (`src-010`): The action deck doubles as the group's life force, so repeated failure and long exploration can create attrition pressure, while the enormous card system demands careful sorting, storage, and bookkeeping.
- **Too Many Bones: Undertow** (`src-011`): No independent friction claim was safely extracted because the fetched review had no accessible transcript; friction is unknown rather than absent.
- **Aeon's End: Legacy** (`src-012`): No review-text friction claim was extracted from the fetched video page; friction is unknown, not disproven.
- **Descent: Legends of the Dark** (`src-013`): No review-text friction claim was extracted from the fetched video page; app dependence, setup load, and long sessions are research leads rather than verified complaints here.
- **Earthborne Rangers** (`src-014`): Space-Biff reports long-campaign wear, daunting stacks of conditional triggers, occasional shuffling that outlasts bypassing a destination, and repeated wilderness scenery.
- **Sword & Sorcery: Immortal Souls** (`src-015`): No review-text friction claim was extracted from the fetched video page; rules burden, scenario balance, and campaign length remain unknown in this batch.
- **Mice and Mystics** (`src-016`): The chapter-by-chapter setup, initiative maintenance, and dense special-case story rules create meaningful onboarding and table-management load.
- **Aeon Trespass: Odyssey** (`src-017`): The documented friction is exceptional scale: an 80-page rulebook, a very large campaign, and a rules/reference burden that required substantial distillation by an experienced reviewer.
- **Middara: Unintentional Malum – Act 1** (`src-018`): The main operational friction is campaign commitment: 3–4 hour weekly sessions over six to eight months, a 500-page Adventure Book, and a large reading/component footprint.
- **Stuffed Fables** (`src-019`): The review identifies a narrow audience window, rules too complex for very young children, low challenge for experienced adult gamers, and no clean way to save mid-chapter.
- **Pathfinder Adventure Card Game: Rise of the Runelords – Base Set** (`src-020`): Documented friction includes a difficult-to-reference rulebook, fiddly rules, weak one-shot appeal, and ongoing replay that can require buying additional adventure packs.

## Design judgment

- **Legibility beats volume.** More persistent variables do not create a stronger campaign if players cannot audit them at re-entry.
- **Progression must change decisions.** Numerical growth without altered tactical or narrative choices becomes bookkeeping.
- **Failure needs continuity law.** Replay, branch, consequence, or recovery must be explicit before a long scenario begins.
- **Irreversibility must earn trust.** Permanent mutation is powerful only when rules authority and recovery procedure are unambiguous.
- **Session boundaries are mechanics.** Setup, save, teardown, recap, and next-scenario selection belong to the campaign system, not outside it.
