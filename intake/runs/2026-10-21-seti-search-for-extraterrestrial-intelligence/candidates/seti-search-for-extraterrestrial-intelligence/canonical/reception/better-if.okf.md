## Better-if opportunities

- Claim: SETI would be better if its first teach staged the action menu, resource conversions, and the solar-system rotation before introducing alien-specific variation.
  Source: src-003, src-004
  Evidence: What Board Game emphasizes the many viable opening paths; Tabletop Gaming calls the game complex and notes analysis-paralysis concerns.
  Confidence: medium
  Label: onboarding

- Claim: SETI would be better if player aids made the card-use modes and recurring-income consequences easier to compare at a glance.
  Source: src-002, src-004
  Evidence: The rulebook gives cards separate main-action, free-action, mission, and income functions; Tabletop Gaming praises card variety while describing a dense euro system.
  Confidence: medium
  Label: accessibility

- Claim: SETI would be better if turn-planning support reduced analysis paralysis without removing the meaningful choice among probes, scans, data, cards, and research.
  Source: src-003, src-004
  Evidence: What Board Game describes many interconnected paths; Tabletop Gaming explicitly flags analysis-paralysis concerns.
  Confidence: medium
  Label: downtime

- Claim: SETI would be better if onboarding materials showed how to plan around rotating discs and changing probe routes.
  Source: src-002, src-004
  Evidence: The rulebook makes rotation a recurring rules event; Tabletop Gaming identifies waiting for favorable rotation as a tactical opportunity.
  Confidence: medium
  Label: strategic-depth
