## Summary

These are evidence-led improvement opportunities, not mechanics prescriptions. The strongest pressure is runaway-leader perception: the official rules and independent reception both describe compounding punishment, while the review also says the signals are visible and countermeasures exist.

## Better-if opportunities

- Claim: A better-if pass could strengthen onboarding around early warning signals and recovery decisions without hiding the game's competitive teeth.
  Source: src-003, src-004
  Evidence: The rules provide an altered introductory game for learning, while the review says trouble is telegraphed but mistakes can leave a player hopelessly behind.
  Confidence: medium

- Claim: A better-if pass could make the cost of committing staff and marketing capacity easier to read before a player crosses a point of no return.
  Source: src-003, src-004
  Evidence: The rules make marketeers busy for campaign duration and require salaries, while the review describes small production or sales differences causing long-term setbacks.
  Confidence: medium

- Claim: A better-if pass should preserve visible opponent-driven counterplay rather than replace it with opaque catch-up bonuses.
  Source: src-004
  Evidence: The independent article praises the game's telegraphed threats and says opponent moves provide clues about the market.
  Confidence: medium
