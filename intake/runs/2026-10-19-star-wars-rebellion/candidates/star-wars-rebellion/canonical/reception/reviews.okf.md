## Summary

Independent reception is strongly positive about the theme, faction asymmetry, hidden-base chase, and mission stories. The same reviews warn that the game is long, rules-heavy, table-hungry, and at its weakest when late combat becomes a slow resolution burden.

## Praised design

- Claim: Reviewers praise the game's asymmetric expression of Imperial power and Rebel vulnerability.
  Source: src-005, src-006
  Evidence: Ars Technica describes the Empire as a grand galactic army and the Rebels as scrappy underdogs; Geeks Under Grace calls the game fully asymmetric and links the factions' different resources and tactics to the theme.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Mission cards and the hidden-base search are repeatedly identified as the strongest thematic systems.
  Source: src-005, src-006
  Evidence: Ars Technica praises the thematic missions and hidden-base race; Geeks Under Grace calls mission timing and the cat-and-mouse search mechanically exciting and appealing.
  Confidence: high

## Player friction

- Claim: Three-to-four-hour sessions and large table requirements are recurring reception friction.
  Source: src-005, src-006
  Evidence: Ars Technica reports the box's three-to-four-hour commitment and two-board footprint; Geeks Under Grace lists a 2–3 hour length and says a large table is required.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Rules lookup and combat complexity create learning and pacing friction.
  Source: src-005, src-006
  Evidence: Ars Technica reports detailed rules, scattered exceptions, and combat with repeated dice and tactic-card steps; Geeks Under Grace calls combat clunky and describes late-game slugfests.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: The two-player configuration is the reviewers' preferred experience.
  Source: src-005, src-006
  Evidence: Ars Technica says the game works best with veteran pairs; Geeks Under Grace lists “2–4 (but really 2)” and says team play reduces individual agency.
  Confidence: high
