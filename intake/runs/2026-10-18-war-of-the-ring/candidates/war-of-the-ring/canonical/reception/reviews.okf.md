## Summary

Reception is strongly positive about thematic integration and agonizing faction-specific choices, while repeatedly warning that the game is long, rules-dense, and table-hungry.

## Praised design

- Claim: Reviewers praise the way hidden Fellowship movement and event-card timing make the Lord of the Rings conflict feel mechanically present.
  Source: src-002, src-003
  Evidence: Meeple Mountain calls the thematic integration top-notch and describes the Fellowship Hunt as a strong mechanical expression; Gaming Respawn presents the asymmetrical Ring and military routes as the game's central appeal.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Reviewers value the memorable battles and faction asymmetry enough to rate the game as a keeper despite its length.
  Source: src-002, src-008
  Evidence: Meeple Mountain gives an enthusiastic final verdict; Fuzzy Llama describes the game as deep and compelling while recording substantial complexity.
  Confidence: medium

## Player friction

- Claim: Long playtime and intensive setup/teardown are recurring friction points.
  Source: src-002, src-003
  Evidence: Meeple Mountain reports sessions of roughly three hours plus setup and teardown; Gaming Respawn says a complete game often exceeds the advertised two hours.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: New players face analysis paralysis because the dice icons and their multiple uses create a large first-game decision surface.
  Source: src-008
  Evidence: Fuzzy Llama explicitly describes the action choices as overwhelming and identifies analysis paralysis as a downside.
  Confidence: high

- Claim: Map legibility and piece differentiation can create component-clarity friction.
  Source: src-002, src-008
  Evidence: The reviews discuss crowded regions, many miniatures, covered location names, and difficulty distinguishing factions or nations at a glance.
  Confidence: high
