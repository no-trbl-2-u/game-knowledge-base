## Better-if opportunities

- Claim: War of the Ring would be easier to onboard if its action-die icon vocabulary and first-round decision priorities were easier to teach than the full reference pages.
  Source: src-008
  Evidence: Fuzzy Llama reports that the many dice actions overwhelm first-time players and can create analysis paralysis even with full-page references.
  Confidence: high
  Interpretation: A staged teaching aid could preserve the deep system while reducing first-session search cost.

- Claim: War of the Ring would be easier to operate if setup, teardown, and table-space demands were reduced or better signposted.
  Source: src-002, src-003
  Evidence: Meeple Mountain describes intensive setup and teardown; Gaming Respawn describes games commonly running longer than two hours.
  Confidence: high
  Interpretation: The burden is operational rather than a claim that the core conflict model fails.

- Claim: War of the Ring would be clearer at a glance if faction and nation pieces were easier to distinguish and map locations were less likely to be covered.
  Source: src-002, src-008
  Evidence: Both reviews discuss crowded map areas, covered location names, and similar-looking miniatures.
  Confidence: high
  Interpretation: This is a component-clarity opportunity, not a proposal to flatten faction asymmetry.

- Claim: War of the Ring may be better for players who prefer tactical uncertainty when its randomness is framed explicitly during onboarding.
  Source: src-002
  Evidence: Meeple Mountain distinguishes input randomness from output randomness and notes that hunt draws, combat rolls, and card draws can frustrate players who prefer deterministic strategy.
  Confidence: medium
  Interpretation: The evidence supports expectation-setting; it does not establish that randomness should be removed.
