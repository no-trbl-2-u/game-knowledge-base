# Axiomancer Labyrinth reference-mining pattern

Use this reference when T discusses the literal Act II Labyrinth, especially the blend of *Maze: Solve the World's Most Challenging Puzzle*, the David Bowie *Labyrinth* film, and Mörk Borg.

## Core doctrine

The Axiomancer Labyrinth is literal: a continental maze-world, roughly "the size of Russia," and it is Act II rather than the whole campaign container.

Design sentence:

> a nation-sized puzzle hell with fairy-tale rules and occult teeth.

It should not behave like a generic procedural dungeon. It should behave like a hostile symbolic architecture where geography is interpreted, not merely traversed.

- `https://inventwithpython.com/mazewebsite/` — playable web version.

## What to extract from Maze

Steal method, not content:

- rooms/regions are riddle-objects;
- visible exits are chosen by interpretation, not stats alone;
- art/environmental details carry routing clues;
- narration may hint, lie, joke, or misdirect;
- the correct path is a chain of meanings;
- wrong paths teach the maze grammar rather than merely wasting time;
- the architect/guide may be hostile, theatrical, divine, comic, or unreliable.

Useful canonical Maze principles from the website directions:

- The challenge is a shortest path through room numbers, not simple exploration completion.
- "Anything in this book might be a clue."
- "Not all clues are necessarily trustworthy."
- Clues can belong to a single room or a chain of rooms.
- The center contains a further riddle whose answer is hidden along the shortest path.

## What to extract from Labyrinth film

- theatrical impossible spaces;
- fairy-tale menace;
- trickster social logic;
- surreal inhabitants with comic threat;
- coming-of-age pressure beneath spectacle.

## What to extract from Mörk Borg

- hostile occult texture;
- decaying world-feel;
- grotesque places and creatures;
- terse doom-laden presentation;
- swingy danger and bodily/spiritual cost.

Tie this to Axiomancer's existing difficulty doctrine: greater-good sacrifice can make later experience harsher, selfishness easier.

## Axiomancer Act II implications

The Labyrinth transforms:

- Boy → Man;
- Girl → Woman;
- adventure → pilgrimage;
- romance → burden;
- curiosity → interpretation;
- map navigation → puzzle theology;
- danger → moral weather.

Preferred encounter families:

- Revelation: murals, prophecies, false histories, symbolic chambers.
- Dilemma: lesser-of-two-evils route choices and sacrifices.
- Hazard: cursed architecture, weather, exhaustion, traps.
- Forage: survival inside impossible biomes.
- Sanctuary: rare rests that expose the relationship.
- Parley: failed pilgrims, tricksters, maze-born citizens, gatekeepers.
- Gambit: wagers with goblin logic, dice, cards, tolls, bargains.
- Combat: dangerous, but not the only pressure.

## Working workflow for future sessions

1. When T provides a new external inspiration, inspect the source if accessible.
2. Distill portable design grammar instead of copying lore/content.
3. Patch durable Axiomancer notes or repo-local doctrine immediately when the idea is settled enough.
4. If the source is a repo/site, record both the human-facing URL and machine-usable repo URL.
5. Keep the output compact: design thesis first, implementation implications second.

## Pitfalls

- Do not reduce the Labyrinth to a metaphor. T explicitly corrected that it is literal.
- Do not reduce it to a map-completion dungeon or roguelike maze.
- Do not treat external references as content to copy. Extract structural rules and emotional grammar.
- Do not let Mörk Borg overwhelm the fairy-tale/theatrical side; the blend needs wonder and cruelty together.
