## Plot

- The gang buys powerful Colt revolvers. Glanton tests one by killing animals and disputes the price until soldiers arrive (PDF pp. 63–64).
- Judge Holden defuses the confrontation through Spanish, elaborate ceremony, and payment, then the armed company departs (pp. 64–66).
- Tension grows between the Black and white men both named John Jackson; other gang members observe and speculate rather than intervene (pp. 63, 66–67).
- The gang accepts a traveling family of performers seeking protection to Janos (pp. 67–69).
- At camp, the performers tell fortunes for Black Jackson, the kid, and Glanton; the judge interprets selectively, while Glanton nearly shoots the fortune-teller before the judge restrains him (pp. 69–74).
- In Janos, Glanton kills an elderly Indigenous woman who is already captive and orders McGill to scalp her for payment (pp. 74–75).
- That night the performers stage their show, with Black Jackson appearing as part of it (pp. 75–76).

## Character Evidence

- **Glanton:** Tests tools through gratuitous killing, bargains through intimidation, tolerates no unwelcome interpretation, and converts a helpless captive into a bounty token (pp. 63–64, 73–75).
- **Judge Holden:** Uses language, erudition, ceremony, and money to transform confrontation into apparent legitimacy; he also withholds or reshapes meaning when interpreting fortunes (pp. 64–65, 70–74).
- **Black John Jackson:** Resists humiliating contact and demands the content of the judge's account; he participates solemnly in the fortune ritual (pp. 64–65, 70–71).
- **White John Jackson:** Repeatedly invades Black Jackson's space with racist provocation while the company declines to intervene (pp. 63, 66).
- **Toadvine:** Refuses Bathcat's invitation to wager on the Jackson conflict, indicating caution about an unstable situation (pp. 66–67).
- **The juggler and family:** Trade performance, divination, and adaptability for passage while remaining exposed to Glanton's violence (pp. 68–76).
- **McGill:** Obeys Glanton's order to scalp the murdered woman and prepares the body as a payable trophy (pp. 74–75).

## Voice & Narrative Techniques

- Detailed weapon mechanics connect technological novelty to immediate misuse.
- The judge's multilingual performance demonstrates how procedure and scholarship can manufacture authority.
- Prophecy is made ambiguous through translation, interruption, lost information, and competing interpretations.
- The traveling show mirrors the gang: both perform identities before witnesses, but only one controls lethal force.
- The chapter contrasts public spectatorship with nonintervention, especially around racial harassment and murder.
- Foreshadowing appears through both explicit future narration and divinatory uncertainty.

## SDLC Translation Candidates

> The following are design inferences, not claims about software in the novel.

- **Inference — Judge Holden:** **Operational behavior:** mediate across languages and restore a stalled negotiation. **Danger:** mediation becomes manipulation when one actor controls translation, context, and payment. **Guardrail:** use independent interpreters, shared minutes, transparent terms, and conflict-of-interest review.
- **Inference — Glanton:** **Operational behavior:** stress-test new tooling and negotiate acquisition cost. **Danger:** testing without ethical scope or a safe environment destroys unrelated assets. **Guardrail:** require sandboxing, test plans, authorized targets, and explicit success criteria.
- **Inference — Black Jackson:** **Operational behavior:** challenge an opaque representation made about him. **Danger:** the organization treats demands for transparency as disruption. **Guardrail:** guarantee access to records, correction rights, and an appeal path.
- **Inference — gang observers:** **Operational behavior:** monitor an escalating interpersonal hazard. **Danger:** passive observation allows predictable violence. **Guardrail:** impose anti-harassment rules, intervention duties, and escalation ownership.
- **Inference — Glanton's bounty process:** **Operational behavior:** produce a contractually legible artifact. **Danger:** artifact-based metrics erase provenance and incentivize fraudulent substitution. **Guardrail:** reject harmful proxy metrics; require chain-of-custody and rights review.
