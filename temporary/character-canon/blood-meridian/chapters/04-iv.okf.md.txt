## Plot

- Captain White's forty-six-man expedition crosses into Mexico, hunts for food, and advances with wagons and uneven equipment (PDF pp. 35–36).
- Disease kills four men; failing wagons and animals force repairs, rationing, and night travel across a pumice desert (pp. 36–38).
- Hayward prays for rain, which arrives briefly; the company later reaches a remote homestead and water source (pp. 38–39).
- White's men drag a terrified old resident from hiding, dismiss his speech, repair equipment, and continue through ruins (pp. 39–40).
- A large herd conceals hundreds of Comanche fighters. White misreads the approach as a small group of thieves and anticipates easy combat (pp. 40–41).
- The Comanches overwhelm the expedition; the kid is unhorsed amid the massacre (pp. 41–43).

## Character Evidence

- **Captain White:** Maintains vigilance rituals but interprets ambiguous evidence through contempt and overconfidence, leading his company into a disastrous encounter (pp. 39–43).
- **Sergeant Trammel:** Demonstrates disciplined weapons maintenance and long-range marksmanship, while also sharing White's mistaken assessment of the herd (pp. 35, 40–41).
- **Candelario:** Serves as guide but gives an uncertain identification; he cannot be found when the concealed force becomes apparent (pp. 36, 40–41).
- **Hayward:** Voices a modest, situational prayer for rain, which the company collectively endorses (pp. 38).
- **The kid:** Carries unreliable equipment, fires during the attack, and attempts to reload after being unhorsed (pp. 35, 42).

## Voice & Narrative Techniques

- Technical descriptions of firearms, hunting, and wagon repair make operational competence visible.
- Environmental scale steadily reduces the expedition's apparent power.
- Disease is personified as an unseen follower, connecting logistics and mortality.
- Celestial and geological imagery frames human plans as temporary and poorly fitted to the terrain.
- The final battle shifts into accumulative, disorienting observation rather than strategic overview.
- Dramatic irony makes the captain's prediction of sport immediately precede annihilation.
