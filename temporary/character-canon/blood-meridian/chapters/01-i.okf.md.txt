## Plot

- The kid grows up motherless in Tennessee, leaves home at fourteen, works and fights his way through river towns, and survives being shot in New Orleans (PDF p. 6).
- He travels to Texas, takes an old mule as wages, and reaches Nacogdoches (pp. 6–7).
- Judge Holden invents accusations against Reverend Green; the congregation becomes a violent mob, after which the judge admits that he had never known Green (pp. 7–9).
- The kid fights Toadvine in the mud. After their mutual violence subsides, they become companions (pp. 9–11).
- Toadvine burns Sidney's hotel-room door; he and the kid assault Sidney and the clerk, and the kid flees town while the hotel burns (pp. 11–14).
- The judge watches the kid leave and smiles (pp. 13–14).

## Character Evidence

- **The kid:** Habitually meets uncertainty with physical force, yet shows endurance and practical initiative after injury and flight (pp. 6, 9–13).
- **Judge Holden:** Uses calm, authoritative falsehoods to direct a crowd toward violence; afterward, he reveals the fraud without losing the crowd's favor (pp. 7–9).
- **Toadvine:** Escalates a chance obstruction into lethal combat, then rapidly converts his former opponent into an accomplice for revenge and arson (pp. 9–13).
- **Reverend Green:** Loses command of his audience when challenged by a more composed speaker and cannot counter fabricated specifics (pp. 7–8).

## Voice & Narrative Techniques

- Compressed biography moves quickly from childhood conditions to an established behavioral disposition.
- Scenic description alternates with abrupt violence, making brutality part of ordinary movement rather than an isolated event.
- Physical detail establishes character before motive is explained.
- Dramatic irony exposes the crowd's credulity after the damage is already underway.
- The narrator occasionally widens from individual action to historical or metaphysical scale without resolving causation.
