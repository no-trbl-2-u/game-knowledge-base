## Plot
- **Observed:** While the kid repairs a strap, Tobin describes the judge's languages, music, tracking, riding, and other skills, then recounts the gang's first encounter with him. [PDF pp.92–95]
- **Observed:** Tobin says the depleted gang found the judge alone in the desert; he redirected them toward a bat cave and extracted saltpeter from guano while producing charcoal. [PDF pp.94–97]
- **Observed:** The judge leads the men across lava to a volcanic cone, obtains sulfur, mixes gunpowder, and turns an apparently final refuge into an ambush. [PDF pp.97–100]
- **Observed:** The gang kills scores of pursuing Indigenous people; Tobin ends by warning the kid that the judge may hear his questions. [PDF pp.100–101]

## Character Evidence
- **Observed:** Tobin admires the judge's competence but alternates between calling him savior, lunatic, curse, and object of study. [PDF pp.93–98, 101]
- **Observed:** The kid tells Tobin he saw the judge in Nacogdoches and asks what the judge is a judge of. [PDF pp.93–94, 101]
- **Observed:** The judge combines observation, chemistry, logistics, deception, and marksmanship under extreme time pressure. [PDF pp.95–100]
- **Inference:** Tobin's account establishes operational brilliance but is retrospective testimony colored by fear, theology, and awe. [PDF pp.93–101]

## Voice & Narrative Techniques
- **Observed:** Most events arrive through Tobin's first-person oral account rather than direct scene narration. [PDF pp.93–101]
- **Observed:** Technical steps for powder manufacture coexist with religious analogies, infernal imagery, and comic obscenity. [PDF pp.96–100]
- **Inference:** The katabasis structure turns engineering success into moral danger: effectiveness and goodness are explicitly decoupled. [PDF pp.97–101]
