## Plot

- Chained prisoners, including the kid and Toadvine, are forced to collect refuse in Chihuahua under abusive guards (PDF pp. 58–59).
- A Kentucky veteran recounts prior campaigns, looting, punishments, and the desecration of a Lipan burial cave (pp. 59–60).
- Glanton's scalphunters enter the city with Judge Holden among them and gain access to the governor (pp. 60–61).
- Toadvine learns that Glanton has a government contract paying for scalps and for Gómez's head; he falsely presents himself, the kid, and the veteran as experienced killers (p. 61).
- Glanton equips the three on credit, securing their release; they leave Chihuahua with the gang after a public ceremony (pp. 61–62).

## Character Evidence

- **Toadvine:** Maintains gallows humor under forced labor, gathers actionable intelligence, and negotiates release by overstating the group's experience (pp. 58, 61).
- **The kid:** Endures labor, watches the judge closely, and recounts the Comanche attack to fellow prisoners (pp. 58–61).
- **The veteran:** Tells operationally detailed war stories while acknowledging looting, lethal punishment, and grave robbery; his tone is restrained rather than celebratory (pp. 59–60).
- **Glanton:** Leads a heavily armed group and turns state payments for human trophies into a commercial operation (pp. 60–62).
- **Judge Holden:** Appears socially polished within the gang, recognizes the kid, and silently acknowledges him before recruitment (pp. 60–61).

## Voice & Narrative Techniques

- Carceral routine reduces people to laboring bodies before personal histories re-emerge in dialogue.
- Embedded war testimony complicates official victory narratives with theft, punishment, and desecration.
- Civic ceremony and commercial bounty are juxtaposed with the gang's visible brutality.
- Recognition between the kid and judge is conveyed through gaze rather than explanation.
- Satirical understatement exposes the transformation of imprisonment into contractual violence.
