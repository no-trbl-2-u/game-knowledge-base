## Plot

- The kid, Toadvine, and Bathcat drink in a Janos cantina where an elderly former soldier reflects on Mexico's repeated bloodshed and warns of an inescapable rider (PDF pp. 77–79).
- A wounded cardplayer remains in the tavern because he has nowhere else to go (pp. 78–79).
- The gang rides through a raided Indigenous camp. Black Jackson rejoins after falling behind, while the veteran Chambers disappears (pp. 79–80).
- Judge Holden questions Toadvine and the kid about Chambers; two Delaware scouts leave the next morning (pp. 79–80).
- At camp, white Jackson orders Black Jackson away from a shared fire and threatens him with a revolver while Glanton watches (pp. 80–81).
- Black Jackson withdraws, returns with a bowie knife, and kills white Jackson with one stroke. No one punishes him, and the company rides on (pp. 81–82).
- Within an hour, Apaches ride upon the company (p. 82).

## Character Evidence

- **The kid:** Watches the elderly speaker closely, travels with the gang, and gives the judge no recorded explanation for Chambers's departure (pp. 78–80).
- **Toadvine:** Uses rough humor in the cantina, then answers the judge directly that Chambers appears to have chosen for himself (pp. 77, 79–80).
- **Bathcat:** Translates local information and explains that the wounded man's immobility comes from having nowhere to go (pp. 77–79).
- **The elderly former soldier:** Connects bounty violence to a longer national history of bloodshed and speaks from remembered military experience (pp. 77–79).
- **Judge Holden:** Audits the missing recruit, treats Toadvine as spokesman for his group, and looks to the kid for corroboration (pp. 79–80).
- **White John Jackson:** Uses racist exclusion, intoxication, and a firearm to assert control over an unregulated shared space (pp. 80–81).
- **Black John Jackson:** States an equal-use principle, checks whether the threat is final, withdraws, and returns to kill his aggressor (pp. 80–82).
- **Glanton:** Observes the conflict and does not intervene before or after the killing (pp. 80–82).

## Voice & Narrative Techniques

- An advisory encounter echoes earlier warning figures while refusing to specify a single literal referent.
- Sound and smell construct the predawn camp before visual detail becomes available.
- Disappearances are reported through gaps in routine, making organizational awareness visibly delayed.
- The campfire confrontation is staged through repeated looks toward authority and the authority's silence.
- The narration describes lethal escalation sequentially, exposing each missed opportunity for intervention.
- The final Apache arrival prevents the intragroup killing from becoming a closed resolution.

## SDLC Translation Candidates

> The following are design inferences, not claims about software in the novel.

- **Inference — Judge Holden:** **Operational behavior:** detect missing personnel and question the accountable subgroup. **Danger:** dependency on an informal spokesman leaves identity, consent, and status unverified. **Guardrail:** maintain direct check-ins, explicit ownership, and reliable offboarding records.
- **Inference — Bathcat:** **Operational behavior:** translate contextual blockers rather than only literal words. **Danger:** a single interpreter can misstate another person's situation. **Guardrail:** distinguish translation from inference and seek confirmation from the affected person.
- **Inference — Black Jackson:** **Operational behavior:** assert equal access to shared infrastructure and verify whether a threat is final. **Danger:** self-help retaliation turns governance failure into lethal escalation. **Guardrail:** provide immediate incident response, safe separation, neutral adjudication, and protection from retaliation.
- **Inference — Glanton:** **Operational behavior:** permit autonomous conflict resolution within a field team. **Danger:** leader silence legitimizes harassment and violence. **Guardrail:** define conduct rules, intervene at threat thresholds, and investigate every serious incident.
- **Inference — the elderly former soldier:** **Operational behavior:** introduce historical risk context into a profit-driven mission. **Danger:** symbolic warning may not alter execution. **Guardrail:** translate warnings into concrete scenarios, controls, and go/no-go decisions.
