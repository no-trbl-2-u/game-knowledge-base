## Plot
- **Observed:** In 1878 the man crosses north Texas, where a hunter recounts the industrial extermination of buffalo and bonepickers harvest the remains. [PDF pp. 228–230]
- **Observed:** Armed youths visit his camp; after Elrod returns before dawn and fires at him, the man kills the boy and allows his companions to carry away the body and rifle. [PDF pp. 230–234]
- **Observed:** At Fort Griffin's Beehive saloon, a man shoots a performing bear and the protagonist encounters the apparently unchanged judge. [PDF pp. 234–236]
- **Observed:** The judge argues that the gathering is an orchestrated ritual, that blood creates solidarity, and that only total submission to war permits the true dance. [PDF pp. 236–239]
- **Observed:** After an unsuccessful sexual encounter, the man enters the jakes; the naked judge embraces him and bars the door. Others later recoil from what is inside, while the judge dances naked and declares his immortality. [PDF pp. 239–243]
- **Inference:** The protagonist's death is strongly implied but not directly narrated or anatomically described. [PDF pp. 240–243]

## Character Evidence
- **Observed:** The man warns Elrod, relocates his bed, fires only after Elrod shoots, and returns the boy's rifle to the survivors. [PDF pp. 231–234]
- **Inference:** He exercises more restraint than his younger self but remains prepared for immediate lethal defense. [PDF pp. 231–234]
- **Observed:** He truthfully explains that he bought the Apache ears and knew the man who took them, yet still carries the trophies decades later. [PDF pp. 230–232]
- **Inference:** He has neither fully repudiated nor transformed his inheritance from the gang. [PDF pp. 230–232]
- **Observed:** The judge recognizes him, supplies alcohol, controls the conversation, interprets the room as ritual, and presents total war as the sole authentic participation. [PDF pp. 234–239]
- **Inference:** His persona design is anti-operational: charisma, omniscient framing, and forced binaries conceal coercion and make falsification impossible. [PDF pp. 234–239]
- **Observed:** The man repeatedly denies being with the judge and dismisses the judge's doctrine, but he continues drinking and remains in the venue. [PDF pp. 236–240]

## Voice & Narrative Techniques
- **Observed:** Buffalo carcasses, commercial bone collection, the shot bear, and the final dance link organized extraction with entertainment and ritualized violence. [PDF pp. 228–243]
- **Observed:** The protagonist's designation as “the man” marks elapsed time but preserves continuity with the kid. [PDF pp. 228–241]
- **Observed:** The jakes scene uses ellipsis: the encounter is shown, its result withheld, and witnesses' reactions substitute for exposition. [PDF pp. 240–242]
- **Inference:** The closing present tense makes the judge's dance feel ongoing rather than conclusively historical. [PDF pp. 241–243]

## SDLC Translation Candidates
- **Inference:** **Legacy-debt auditor:** identify artifacts retained from abusive systems and require explicit disposition rather than treating mere passage of time as remediation. [PDF pp. 230–232]
- **Inference:** **Escalation controller:** issue clear boundaries, reduce exposure, preserve evidence, and use force only against an active threat; still review whether disengagement was possible earlier. [PDF pp. 231–234]
- **Inference:** **Totalizing-rhetoric detector:** reject frameworks in which every outcome confirms the leader, dissent proves defect, and participation is defined as surrender. [PDF pp. 236–239]
- **Inference:** **Outcome-certainty grader:** label the man's fate “strongly implied,” not observed; preserve ambiguity where the event log omits the result. [PDF pp. 240–243]
