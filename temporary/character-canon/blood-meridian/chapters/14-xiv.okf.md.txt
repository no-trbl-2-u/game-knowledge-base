## Plot
- **Observed:** The gang descends through storms into Jesús María, drinks through religious observances, assaults a priest, and terrorizes the town. [PDF pp.136–140]
- **Observed:** During Glanton's drunken fit, a girl goes missing; the judge buys two puppies, performs a coin trick, and throws the animals into the river, where Bathcat shoots them. [PDF pp.139–140]
- **Observed:** Glanton drags the Mexican flag behind a mule, triggers a firefight, and escapes after six gang members are left behind and executed. [PDF pp.140–141]
- **Observed:** The gang attacks a mercury mule train, killing drivers and driving animals over a precipice; the judge and Delawares later recover black Jackson. [PDF pp.141–143]
- **Observed:** Traveling toward Ures, the judge collects animals and plants and explains knowledge as a prerequisite for dominion; the gang enters Ures and begins another violent revel. [PDF pp.144–148]

## Character Evidence
- **Observed:** The judge tends the bound Glanton during his fit and later helps recover Jackson. [PDF pp.139, 143]
- **Observed:** The judge deliberately destroys the puppies after winning the seller's trust with a trick. [PDF p.140]
- **Observed:** He states that anything outside his knowledge exists without his consent and defines mastery as overriding local judgments. [PDF pp.144–145]
- **Observed:** Toadvine presses the judge on the practical purpose and impossibility of total knowledge. [PDF pp.144–145]
- **Inference:** The judge's scientific persona is inseparable from a governance doctrine of forced legibility and control. [PDF pp.144–145]

## Voice & Narrative Techniques
- **Observed:** Storm electricity, religious processions, intoxication, and industrial mercury give successive events ceremonial and alchemical frames. [PDF pp.136–143]
- **Observed:** The scientific dialogue shifts from specimen collection to explicit political vocabulary: keeper, overlord, and countermanding authority. [PDF pp.144–145]
- **Inference:** The chapter repeatedly turns hospitality and wonder into domination, making spectacle an operational precursor to harm. [PDF pp.138–148]

## SDLC Translation Candidates
- **Inference:** **Least-authority agent:** reject the judge's model; obtain only knowledge needed for the task and preserve local autonomy. [PDF pp.144–145]
- **Inference:** **Consent-aware collector:** do not capture, classify, or retain entities merely because they are observable. [PDF pp.140, 144–145]
- **Inference:** **Incident containment lead:** when a principal becomes impaired, isolate weapons and preserve the host environment rather than restoring mission velocity. [PDF pp.139–141]
- **Inference:** **Supply-chain harm monitor:** account for workers, animals, cargo, and environmental contamination before tactical acquisition. [PDF pp.141–143]
