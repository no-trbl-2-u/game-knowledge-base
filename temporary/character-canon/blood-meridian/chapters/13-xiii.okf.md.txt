## Plot
- **Observed:** Chihuahua counts 128 scalps and eight heads, pays the gang, bathes and outfits them, and honors them at a banquet that becomes recurrent riot and civic intimidation. [PDF pp.122–126]
- **Observed:** After leaving, the gang exploits Coyame, wanders the border, and the judge copies selected rock images at Hueco Tanks before erasing one original. [PDF pp.126–127]
- **Observed:** The gang knowingly massacres peaceful Tiguas; Toadvine and the kid voice concern beforehand but do not prevent the attack. [PDF pp.127–128]
- **Observed:** After trouble over stolen teeth and expulsion under escort, the gang kills dozens in a Nacori cantina, scalps the bodies, and later destroys another village. [PDF pp.129–133]
- **Observed:** Mounted soldiers pursue them; the gang ultimately kills the soldiers, buries its own dead, returns to Chihuahua, and then rides west. [PDF pp.133–136]

## Character Evidence
- **Observed:** Glanton refuses separate treatment for officers, divides the payment directly, and later looks toward the family in Texas he will not revisit. [PDF pp.124–126]
- **Observed:** The judge moves easily among governor, musicians, dancers, violence, and archival erasure. [PDF pp.124–127, 130–132]
- **Observed:** Toadvine and the kid recognize that the Tiguas have harmed no one, yet remain in formation. [PDF p.127]
- **Observed:** Tobin, posted with the horses, shoots fleeing witnesses after the cantina fight. [PDF pp.131–132]
- **Inference:** Individual misgivings have no effective escalation path inside Glanton's command. [PDF pp.127–128]

## Voice & Narrative Techniques
- **Observed:** Civic ceremony, luxury, and administrative counting are placed beside decomposing trophies and uncontrolled violence. [PDF pp.122–126]
- **Observed:** The aftermath of the Tigua massacre emphasizes how wind and time erase physical evidence. [PDF p.128]
- **Inference:** Repetition across towns converts exceptional violence into a process sustained by payment, hospitality, fear, and silence. [PDF pp.122–136]
